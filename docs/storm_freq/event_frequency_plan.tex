\section{What changed from version 1}

\subsection{What version 2.1 changes}

Version~2.0 was written before any code existed. Building it moved four things,
one of which was an outright error in version~2.0's design.

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{0.06\textwidth}p{0.42\textwidth}p{0.44\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{Change} & \textbf{Driver} \\
\midrule
\endhead
C7 & \textbf{$\lambda$ is a property of the catchment, not of a gauge.}
Version~2.0 had per-gauge peaks-over-threshold calibration feeding $\lambda$
into pricing. That is wrong: a per-gauge exceedance rate is \emph{already}
$\lambda \times P(\text{exceed} \mid \text{event})$, so using it as $\lambda$
squares the conditional and double-counts. $\lambda$ is now a seeded
per-catchment rate, and per-gauge extraction became the \textbf{validation

differs per gauge is the conditional response, not the arrival rate \\
\addlinespace
C8 & \textbf{The Monte Carlo year simulation is the engine}, and has moved
forward out of Track~B. Version~2.0 deferred the sampler and priced off the
closed form; the order is now reversed & The closed form is the simulation's
\emph{exact expectation}, not an approximation of it, so it is worth more as a
check than as an answer. The simulation additionally yields the annual
distribution, which the closed form cannot \\
\addlinespace
C9 & \textbf{Ten thousand simulated years}, and the reconciliation gate is
expressed in sampling standard errors rather than as a fixed percentage &
Measured on the target hardware. A fixed 2\% band false-alarmed on 17\% of runs
at ten thousand years while never binding at a million \\
\addlinespace
C10 & \textbf{Landmine L3 resolved:} \texttt{num\_storms} is kept and
\texttt{num\_events} added beside it & The storm/event distinction becomes
visible in the data rather than hidden in a redefinition of a field existing
consumers already read \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\section{Background and problem statement}

\subsection{Current state}

The simulator generates synthetic storm events and evaluates, at each river
gauge, whether a storm produces a flood. Insurance cost is priced as the ratio
of flooding storms to total storms. This is a \textbf{conditional probability}
--- $P(\text{flood} \mid \text{storm})$ --- and is dimensionless with respect to
time.

Two gauges with identical conditional probability therefore receive identical
prices even if one experiences eight qualifying storms a year and the other one.
The true annualised risk differs by a factor of eight.

\subsection{The defect, with evidence}

The defect is not conceptual. It is written out in three places.

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{0.34\textwidth}p{0.30\textwidth}p{0.30\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Location} & \textbf{Code} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
\endhead
\texttt{src/port/src/property/hc/} \texttt{pricing/\_process.py:54} &
\texttt{spread\_bps = (flood\_count / num\_storms) * 10000} &
The property PRS spread \textbf{is} $P(\text{flood} \mid \text{storm})$,
expressed in basis points \\
\addlinespace
\texttt{src/port/src/property/hc/} \texttt{pricing/\_process.py:250} &
\texttt{return\_period\_yrs = num\_storms / flood\_count} &
The assumption stated explicitly: \textbf{one storm = one year},
$\lambda \equiv 1.0$ \\
\addlinespace
\texttt{src/models/hazard/} \texttt{builder.py:109--141} &
GEV fitted to the peak level of \emph{every} simulated storm; the resulting
exceedance probability assigned to \texttt{annual\_exceedance\_prob} and
\texttt{annual\_hazard\_rate\_*} &
A per-event conditional is relabelled \emph{annual} at the point of assignment \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\noindent The same ratio construction appears on the gauge basis leg
(\texttt{\_process.py:119}) and the wind leg (\texttt{\_process.py:70}, via
\texttt{\_wind\_union}).

\subsection{What is already correct}

The downstream plumbing is rate-shaped and needs no rework:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{src/models/hazard/gev.py:66} --- \texttt{compute\_term\_structure}
        is already a Poisson compounding,
        $P(\geq 1 \text{ flood by year } t) = 1 - e^{-\lambda t}$.
  \item \texttt{src/models/hazard/prs\_analytical.py:100} --- already converts an
        annual probability to a continuous hazard rate via $-\log(1-p)$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Both are being fed a per-event number. \textbf{The remedy is a
substitution at one node, not a pricer rewrite.}

\subsection{The pattern already exists in this repository}

Two production models already do what version~1 proposed to invent:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{src/models/seismic/occurrence/} --- \texttt{\_rates.py} computes a
        per-asset annual $\lambda$; \texttt{\_simulate.py:89} draws
        \texttt{rng.poisson(lambda\_annual * horizon\_years, size=n\_sim)} and
        instantiates events where the count is at least one; severity and damage
        follow.
  \item \texttt{src/models/fire/initiation.py} --- the same construction, labelled
        ``Model~A --- Poisson initiation''.
\end{itemize}

\noindent MKM-EF-001 should mirror this package shape rather than introduce a
parallel abstraction with different conventions.

\subsection{Remedy}

\begin{align}
\text{Annual flood frequency}_g &= \lambda_g \times P(\text{flood} \mid \text{event}, g) \\
\text{Annual exceedance probability}_g &= 1 - \exp\!\left(-\lambda_g \, P(\text{flood} \mid \text{event}, g)\right) \\
\text{Pure premium}_g &= \lambda_g \times P(\text{flood} \mid \text{event}, g) \times \mathbb{E}[\text{loss} \mid \text{flood}, g]
\end{align}

\noindent with $\lambda_g$ the calibrated annual \textbf{event} arrival rate ---
where ``event'' means the insurance hours-clause event, not the individual
storm (Section~\ref{sec:eventdef}).

\section{Functional specification}

\subsection{Package layout}

Mirrors \texttt{src/models/seismic/occurrence/}. Every file under 300 lines
(R2); no callable code in any \texttt{\_\_init\_\_.py} (R4); canonical copyright
header on every file (R5).

\begin{lstlisting}[language=,caption={Proposed package layout}]
src/models/frequency/
  __init__.py             # module docstring + re-exports only
  datastructures/
    _rate.py              # FittedRate: lambda, family, params, provenance
    _diagnostics.py       # dispersion index, log-likelihood, AIC
  pot/
    _threshold.py         # threshold selection + stability diagnostics
    _decluster.py         # minimum inter-event separation
    _extract.py           # series -> declustered exceedances -> annual counts
  families/
    _poisson.py
    _negbin.py
    _select.py            # dispersion test + AIC tiebreak; logs any override
  annualise.py            # p_annual = 1 - exp(-lambda * p_event)   <-- the seam
  calibrate.py            # pure function of (series, config) -> FittedRate
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent \texttt{annualise.py} is deliberately tiny and deliberately the only
place the composition happens. Frequency logic must not leak into the event
simulator or the pricing modules.

\subsection{Configuration}

A new \texttt{config/frequency/} package with \texttt{\_schema.py} and
\texttt{\_loader.py}, following the hydrated-dataclass pattern of
\texttt{config/seismic/}. It owns, per house rule R1:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Target mean exceedance rate band for threshold selection.
  \item Declustering window (minimum inter-event separation).
  \item Dispersion-test critical value; AIC tiebreak policy.
  \item Minimum record length for per-gauge calibration; regional fallback
        $\lambda$.
  \item Return-period grid for output tables.
  \item Per-peril family overrides, with the override itself logged (SR~11-7).
\end{itemize}

\noindent It also absorbs an existing orphan:
\texttt{src/models/intensity/distribution/\_core.py:198} carries
\texttt{storms\_per\_year: float = 20} as a default argument --- an undocumented
frequency assumption living inside the severity model, and an R1 breach. That
parameter moves here and the severity model takes it as an injected value.

\subsection{Event-definition alignment}
\label{sec:eventdef}

This is not a hypothetical risk. It is already wrong, and locatably so.

MKM-SS-001 produces \textbf{sequences} --- one to five related storms fitted
inside a 168-hour insurance hours clause
(\texttt{EVENT\_WINDOW\_HOURS} in \texttt{config/port/\_storm.py}). But
\texttt{src/models/hazard/io/\_load.py:52--61} flattens every sequence into its
member storms before the hazard model sees them. \texttt{num\_storms} is
therefore one to five times the event count, and today's denominator is
\textbf{neither events nor years}.

\medskip
\noindent \textbf{Built.} The remedy as delivered:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{load\_storms\_from\_sequences} tags each storm with its
        \texttt{sequence\_id}. The key is \textbf{added, not substituted}, so
        every existing consumer of the storm fields is untouched. An untagged
        sequence falls back to a positional identity, so two untagged sequences
        cannot silently merge into one event.
  \item \texttt{event\_id} and \texttt{count\_events} live with the loader that
        writes the tag, and the frequency aggregator imports them, so the
        fallback rule cannot drift between the two. This also keeps the
        dependency pointing downhill: frequency imports hazard, never the
        reverse.
  \item The conditional is evaluated per event --- the \textbf{maximum} peak
        level across the member storms, not one row per storm.
  \item Grouping is by storm \textbf{identity}, not position. The two coincide
        today, but position would break silently if response ordering ever
        changed.
  \item $\lambda$ is quoted in \textbf{hours-clause events per year}, matching
        that unit exactly.
  \item \texttt{num\_storms} is kept and \texttt{num\_events} added beside it in
        both the hazard metadata and the build summary.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The physics is untouched: each storm is still routed through the gauge
response model individually. Only the grouping changes.

This step reprices on its own, before $\lambda$ is applied, because the
denominator changes. It was sequenced first precisely so its effect could be
measured apart from the $\lambda$ effect --- and doing so was worthwhile,
because the two turned out to push the \emph{same} way rather than offsetting
(Section~\ref{sec:measured}).

\subsection{Frequency abstraction}

A single interface, per peril, following the seismic rates/simulate split:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{fit(counts: AnnualCountSeries) -> FittedRate}
  \item \texttt{annual\_rate() -> float}
  \item \texttt{sample\_annual\_count(rng) -> int} \quad \emph{(unused in
        Track~A; required by Track~B)}
  \item \texttt{diagnostics() -> FrequencyDiagnostics}
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Implementation} & \textbf{Distribution} & \textbf{Selected when} \\
\midrule
\texttt{PoissonFrequency} & Poisson($\lambda$) & Dispersion index $\approx 1$ (default) \\
\texttt{NegativeBinomialFrequency} & NegBin($r$, $p$) & Overdispersed counts (variance $>$ mean) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Selection is data-driven per gauge via the dispersion index with an
AIC tiebreak, recorded in the calibration output. A configuration override may
force a family; the override is logged with its justification.

\subsection{Calibration pipeline}

Per gauge, from the gauge level record:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Threshold selection} --- the threshold yielding a target mean
        exceedance rate, anchored on bankfull discharge where available.
        Diagnostics: mean-excess and threshold-stability.
  \item \textbf{Declustering} --- enforce a minimum inter-event separation so
        peaks are independent. Record the rule applied.
  \item \textbf{Arrival rate} --- $\hat{\lambda}$ = exceedances / years of
        record; the annual count series is retained for dispersion testing.
  \item \textbf{Dispersion test and family selection}.
  \item \textbf{Severity cross-check (validation only)} --- fit a GPD to
        exceedance magnitudes and compare against the simulator's implied
        severity at that gauge. Divergence beyond tolerance raises a validation
        flag; it never silently recalibrates anything.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Prior art to fix, not to write from scratch.}
\texttt{src/models/statistics/timeseries.py:103--122} already computes a
per-gauge \texttt{frequency\_per\_year} for alert, warning and severe. It counts
\textbf{days over threshold}, not declustered events --- peaks-over-threshold
with step~2 missing --- and therefore overstates $\lambda$ by roughly the mean
event duration in days. Stage~1 is a fix-and-promote of this calculation into
the new package, with the existing consumers repointed.

\paragraph{Provenance (BCBS 239).}
Every fitted rate carries: source dataset identifier and version, record period,
threshold, declustering rule, family selected and why, fit timestamp, code
version, configuration hash, and a \textbf{provenance class} ---
\texttt{observed}, \texttt{generator-derived} or \texttt{regional-fallback}.
Calibration is a pure function of (data, configuration): re-running with
identical inputs must reproduce identical outputs, regression-tested against a
golden master.

\subsection{Persistence}

Fitted rates and their provenance are written and read \textbf{exclusively
through the \texttt{src/database/} seam} (R6). No JSON artefact is created at
any point --- full-audit section~4.5 is walking JSON writes toward zero
tolerance, and a new model must be born database-native.

Deliverables: a new table plus Alembic migration following the existing gauge
hazard-curve migration, with matching entries in the ORM model and column maps,
and accessor functions in the \texttt{database} package API.

\subsection{The event catalogue}

One row per event, one column per gauge, holding the highest level that event
drove at that gauge. This is what the year simulation resamples from.

Because every gauge's outcome for a given event sits in the \textbf{same row},
drawing an event row draws a spatially coherent storm. The correlation between
gauges is carried by the catalogue itself and does not have to be modelled
again downstream.

The catalogue also exposes the conditional half of the decomposition directly:
$P(\text{flood at } g \mid \text{event})$ is the fraction of catalogue rows
whose level at $g$ clears the trigger.

\subsection{The Monte Carlo year simulation}
\label{sec:simulation}

For each simulated year: draw $N \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda)$ qualifying
events, resample $N$ catalogue rows with replacement, and record how many of
them flood. The annual flood probability is the fraction of years with at least
one flood.

\paragraph{The draw is separated from its application.}
\texttt{draw\_event\_years} runs once per run; \texttt{apply\_catalogue} runs
per subject against those same draws. A storm arrives over the catchment and
reaches every gauge and property in it, so one set of draws must serve every
subject. Drawing independently per subject would discard the spatial
correlation the catalogue supplies: measured, two gauges on one reach correlate
\textbf{0.78} under shared draws and \textbf{$-0.004$} under independent ones,
so a two-hundred-gauge book would look two hundred times better diversified
than it is.

\paragraph{Resampling, not regeneration.}
The catalogue's outcomes are already computed, so a simulated year is index
lookups rather than a re-run of the hydrology. That is what makes the year count
affordable.

\medskip
\noindent Outputs, all from the same run:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Annual flood probability} --- the occurrence view; what the PRS
        spread is priced from.
  \item \textbf{Expected floods per year} --- the aggregate view. It exceeds the
        occurrence probability whenever a year can carry more than one flood,
        which is exactly the case a conditional-only model cannot represent at
        all.
  \item \textbf{Return-period levels} --- quantiles of the annual distribution.
  \item \textbf{Event exceedance frequency} --- valid below the one-year return
        period, which matters at frequent-flood gauges and cannot be expressed
        as a probability.
\end{itemize}

\noindent All outputs carry the frequency-model identifier and calibration
provenance in their metadata.

\subsection{The closed form, and why it is a test rather than the answer}
\label{sec:closedform}

For $N \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda)$ with each event flooding independently with
probability $p$:

\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[(1-p)^N\right] = \exp\!\big(\lambda((1-p) - 1)\big) = \exp(-\lambda p)
\end{equation}

so $P(\text{at least one flood in a year}) = 1 - \exp(-\lambda p)$
\textbf{exactly}. The closed form is not an approximation of the simulation ---
it is the simulation's expectation. The same argument gives the aggregate view
directly as $\lambda p$.

That is precisely what makes it valuable as a check: any gap beyond sampling
error means one of the two is wrong. It is wired in as a reconciliation gate,
not as the production pricing path, because the simulation additionally yields
the whole annual distribution.

The gate measures the gap in \textbf{sampling standard errors},
$\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$, against a configured band, rather than as a fixed
percentage. A fixed band cannot mean the same thing at every year count: at ten
thousand years a 2\% band false-alarmed on 17\% of runs, while at a million it
never bound at all. The statistic is verified to be a true $z$-score --- mean
$|z|$ measures 0.811 and 0.797 at ten and a hundred thousand years against the
theoretical $\sqrt{2/\pi} = 0.798$ --- and that calibration is itself a test, so
the band cannot silently stop meaning what it says.

\section{The $\lambda$ provenance problem}
\label{sec:provenance}

\paragraph{Finding.}
On synthetic catchments, calibrating $\lambda$ from the gauge ``historical''
record is circular.

\texttt{src/models/statistics/synthetic.py:122--124} generates the daily series
by injecting severe exceedances at exactly the rate implied by the gauge's
\texttt{FrequencyExceedLevel3} field:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
severe_floods_expected    = max(freq_exceed_level3, 1)
severe_flood_prob_per_day = severe_floods_expected / (years * 365)
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent and \texttt{FrequencyExceedLevel3} is itself produced by
\texttt{src/port/rand/shared/gauge/gauge\_field\_generators.py:168--170} as a
\texttt{random.choices(...)} draw over a five-year window.

A peaks-over-threshold extraction over that series therefore recovers the
injected rate. \textbf{The only per-gauge frequency number the platform
currently holds is a random integer.}

\paragraph{Consequences.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item Version~1's stated risk --- ``short or gappy records give a noisy
        $\hat{\lambda}$'' --- is the wrong risk. The real one is that $\lambda$
        has no observational content on synthetic catchments.
  \item It does not invalidate the POT pipeline. POT is exactly what would be
        run against real gauge series, and building it now is correct.
  \item It does change what Stage~1 may claim. The provenance class must record
        \texttt{generator-derived}, not \texttt{observed}, and the validation
        report must say so.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Approach adopted.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Now:} build POT as specified. On synthetic catchments, validate
        it by \textbf{round-trip} --- the extracted $\hat{\lambda}$ must recover
        the injected rate within tolerance once declustering is accounted for.
        This is a genuinely strong test of the extraction code, and it is
        labelled as such rather than presented as calibration.
  \item \textbf{Follow-on} (separate approval; requires port regeneration): make
        \texttt{FrequencyExceedLevel3} a \emph{modelled} field rather than a
        random one, giving the gauge generator a catchment-level $\lambda$ prior
        so synthetic frequencies are physically plausible and spatially coherent.
        Gauges on the same reach should not carry independent $\lambda$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Measured results}
\label{sec:measured}

Everything in this section is measured, not estimated. It supersedes the
corresponding estimates in version~2.0.

\subsection{Cost and accuracy of the year simulation}

Apple M2, 8 cores, 8\,GB. A full portfolio is 2,200 subjects (gauges plus
properties) scored against one shared set of draws.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Simulated years} & \textbf{Full portfolio} & \textbf{Sampling error} \\
\midrule
1,000 & 0.07\,s & $\sim$5\% \\
\textbf{10,000} \emph{(configured)} & \textbf{0.40\,s} & \textbf{$\sim$1.6\%} \\
100,000 & 4.1\,s & $\sim$0.5\% \\
1,000,000 & 43\,s & $\sim$0.16\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The cost is linear in the year count and the error falls as its square
root, so this is a knob that can be turned without touching code.

\textbf{Run-to-run stability does not depend on the year count.} The seed is
pinned, so a re-run reprices identically at any setting. What the year count
buys is closeness to the true expectation, not repeatability --- a distinction
worth holding onto, because the two are easily conflated when a number is
described as ``noisy''.

\subsection{Repricing: withdrawn, not yet measurable}

\noindent\textbf{An earlier draft of this section reported a 5.40$\times$
reprice (1301\,bps to 7030\,bps). That figure is withdrawn.} It was not a model
output. Two faults:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{The trigger level was invented.} The conditional came from an
        arbitrary threshold applied to a synthetic catalogue, not from a
        gauge's severe-warning level. Nothing anchored it.
  \item \textbf{The catalogue was resampled uniformly.} MKM-SS-001 generates
        its batches from 40\% moderate, 35\% severe, 20\% extreme and 5\%
        catastrophic --- and \emph{no} minimal or baseline at all --- because it
        exists to train the stress classifier, where oversampling the
        interesting region is correct. Averaging over that uniformly answers
        $P(\text{flood} \mid \text{event is at least moderate})$, and
        multiplying \emph{that} by a $\lambda$ counting all qualifying events
        double-counts severity.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The tell was the implied return period: a severe flood every
\textbf{0.81 years}. A conditional probability is easy to misread; a severe
flood every nine months is obviously wrong. That check is now built in
(\texttt{implied\_return\_period\_years}) precisely so this class of error
announces itself.

\textbf{Fault 2 is fixed} --- the catalogue now carries population weights and
the sampler draws proportionally, which moves the implied return period from
roughly 1.9 to 3.4 years.

\textbf{That is still not credible, and no repriced number should be quoted
until it is.} The remaining error is fault 1, and it cannot be fixed by choosing
a better threshold by hand. It needs the constraint already present in the
design:

\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\text{catchment}} \times P(\text{flood} \mid \text{event}, g)
\;\equiv\; \text{gauge } g\text{'s measured severe-exceedance rate}
\end{equation}

Until that identity is asserted against real gauge trigger levels, any
conditional this layer produces is unfalsifiable. \textbf{Anchoring it is
Stage~3 work, and no repricing figure belongs in this document before then.}

Two open questions the anchoring will settle, recorded so they are not lost:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item The population weights are engineering-judgement seeds. How often a
        qualifying Thames storm event is severe-or-worse is the input that most
        directly sets the answer, and it wants an owner's view rather than a
        modeller's guess.
  \item Whether $\lambda = 4.5$/yr and the seeded weights are \emph{jointly}
        consistent with observed severe-flood return periods, or whether one of
        them has to move.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Extraction against the existing calculation}

On a thirty-year synthetic gauge record, at the severe trigger:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Measure} & \textbf{Rate} \\
\midrule
Exceedance \textbf{days} per year --- what the statistics module reports today & 1.234 \\
Declustered \textbf{events} per year & 0.967 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent A \textbf{28\% overstatement}, being the mean flood duration in days.
This is the quantity the fix-and-promote removes.

The decomposition identity also holds exactly on that record: $\lambda \times
P(\text{severe} \mid \text{event}) = 0.9672$ against a directly measured severe
rate of $0.9672$. That identity is the per-gauge validation arm.

\section{Glossary}

\begin{description}[nosep,style=nextline]
  \item[AAL] Average Annual Loss; expected loss per year.
  \item[AEP] Aggregate Exceedance Probability; the probability that total annual
        loss exceeds $x$. \emph{Track~B.}
  \item[OEP] Occurrence Exceedance Probability; the probability that the largest
        single event loss in a year exceeds $x$. \emph{Track~B.}
  \item[EEF] Event Exceedance Frequency; the annual frequency, not probability,
        of events exceeding a level.
  \item[ELT / YLT] Event Loss Table and Year Loss Table. \emph{Track~B.}
  \item[Event] Here, the insurance hours-clause event --- a storm \emph{sequence}
        within the configured event window --- not an individual storm.
  \item[POT] Peaks Over Threshold; extreme-value sampling of all independent
        exceedances above a threshold.
  \item[GPD] Generalised Pareto Distribution; the standard model for
        peaks-over-threshold exceedance magnitudes.
  \item[Dispersion index] Variance divided by mean of annual counts; near one
        supports Poisson, above one indicates overdispersion.
  \item[NHPP] Non-Homogeneous Poisson Process; a time-varying arrival rate.
        \emph{Track~B.}
  \item[Provenance class] \texttt{observed}, \texttt{generator-derived} or
        \texttt{regional-fallback}; records what a fitted $\lambda$ is actually
        based on.
\end{description}
