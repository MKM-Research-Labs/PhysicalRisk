\mkmexecsummary
% =========================================================================

This document benchmarks the Flood Polynomial Model (MKM-FPO-001), v1.0, a closed-form
approximation of the per-gauge GBM flood classifiers (MKM-FC-001) that the
single-gauge stress scenario uses to price PRS trades hour by hour before a breach. It
asks whether the code computes what its model document (\texttt{flood\_poly.pdf}, v1.0)
says, whether the document's claims hold, and how well the polynomial approximates the
classifiers it stands in for.

\paragraph{The code computes its documented formula.} \texttt{p\_flood\_poly} agrees
with a 50-digit implementation of Eqs.~1--4 and the printed coefficients, and so do
\texttt{p\_flood\_series}, the copy in \texttt{stress.sensitivity} and its slope. The
port stress screen's JavaScript holds the same coefficients
(Table~\ref{tab:bm-checks}). \bmWolframConfirms

\paragraph{But the document's evidence does not hold} (Table~\ref{tab:bm-claims}).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Its tables have no source.} The code reproduces \bmTableOne{} of the 54
  cells of Table~1 and none of Table~2. Nor does the GBM reproduce them. No generator
  for either table ever existed.
\item \textbf{At the severe level, $P$ does not rise from 0.42 to 0.97.} It is 0.00 at
  hour~0 and 0.29 at hour~167, after peaking near 0.97 around hour~96.
\item \textbf{The fit is not as stated.} On every real storm state of the fitted gauge,
  $R^2$ is \bmFitRsq, not 0.9395. MAE is \bmFitMae, better than the stated 0.0456. The
  claimed 0.05 accuracy is not met near severe.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The approximation, measured.} The comparison uses the \bmGauges{} gauges'
classifiers on the real storm states they are trained on (Table~\ref{tab:bm-gauges}).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Below severe, the polynomial is within \bmBelowMae{} of the GBM on average.
\item Near severe (75--100\%), where stress P\&L is decided, the mean error is
  \bmNearMae, and \bmNearOver\% of states are off by more than 0.10.
\item The bias near severe ranges from \bmNearBias. It is right on average but
  imprecise state by state.
\item Early in a storm the surface is not monotone in the level: at hour~24, $P$ passes
  $\tfrac12$ twice below severe and falls as the water rises between the two levels.
\end{itemize}
Two things production does are not in the document. The stress route latches $P=1$ once
the level reaches severe. And \texttt{p\_flood\_simple}, which sets portfolio stress P\&L,
is the ratio $\min(1,w/s)$, not a probability.

\paragraph{Evidence.} \bmChecks{} checks, outcome: \bmOutcome{}
(Table~\ref{tab:bm-checks}).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Wolfram: \bmWolfram{}\bmWolframVia.
\item Code under test, at commit \bmCodeCommit{}\bmCodeDirty.
\item Numerics: \bmNumerics.
\item Generated \bmGenerated{} by \texttt{python -m tools.wolfram\_oracle fpo001}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is benchmarked}

Q8), not independent validation (Q9).

\mkmframework
% =========================================================================

The document's model is
\begin{equation}
P=\sigma(a h+b t+c h t+d h^2+e t^2+f),\quad h=\ln\max(w/s,10^{-8}),\quad
t=\ln\frac{\text{hour}+1}{168},
\end{equation}
with the exponent clamped to $\pm30$. The reference evaluates it at 50 digits from the
printed coefficients.

\paragraph{What the polynomial can know.} Each gauge's GBM also sees the rate of rise
$\Delta h$ and its change. A function of $(h,t)$ alone can at best reproduce the GBM's
average over the storm states that share a level and an hour. The approximation is
therefore judged on those real states, as production meets them, not on a grid at
$\Delta h=0$. A grid includes states storms do not produce, and states storms do produce
are left out of it.

% =========================================================================
\section{Benchmark Method}
% =========================================================================

\paragraph{The code.} The code is checked against the reference over three severe
thresholds, sixteen level ratios from zero to 1.5, and thirteen hours.

\paragraph{Wolfram.} From exact coefficients, Wolfram evaluates the clamped logistic at
every cell of Table~1, the levels where $P=\tfrac12$ (by \texttt{NSolve} over the reals
below severe), and $\partial P/\partial h$ symbolically.

\paragraph{The GBM comparison.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item For every gauge with a classifier, 2{,}000 storms are drawn once (seed 7) from the
  catalogue. Each storm's states are rebuilt through production's training-vector
  builder.
\item The polynomial and the GBM are compared on the states below severe.
\item For the fitted gauge, the fit metrics are recomputed on every state of the full
  catalogue. The cubic of \S9.1 is fitted to the same states.
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Benchmark, not validation.} The GBMs are the reference here; whether they
  are right is MKM-FC-001's question.
\item \textbf{Remediation.} The coefficients are near the best this form can do on real
  states: refitting the same six terms does not reduce the error near severe. The model
  is refitted as v2.0, pooled across gauges, with a richer kernel constrained to rise
  with the level and $P=1$ at or above severe. Its document is reissued with tables
  generated from the code, and it is benchmarked as v0.2 of this document.
\end{enumerate}
