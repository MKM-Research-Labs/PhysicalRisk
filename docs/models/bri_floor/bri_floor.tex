% 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
% ===================================================================
\mkmexecsummary

The BRI-Adjusted Floor Level Model (\texttt{MKM-BRF-001}) answers a narrow but
consequential question: at what water level does a property actually flood, once
its evidenced flood resilience is taken into account? It converts the Building
Resilience Index flood sub-score (\texttt{MKM-BRI-001}) into an additive
floor-level credit in metres, and adds that credit to the surveyed floor level
to give the effective flood threshold that every downstream consumer compares
against the attenuated water-surface elevation.

The model is small by design --- a monotone ramp and an addition --- but it sits
on the critical path of the Property Risk Spread. Because the PRS flood leg is
an \emph{event count}, and because the event count is decided by exactly this
threshold comparison, the credit this model awards translates directly into
basis points. A property that clears the threshold in fewer of the 10{,}000
simulated storms earns a narrower spread.

Two properties of the credit are deliberate and material:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{It is additive, never subtractive.} A poor BRI score awards zero
        credit; it can never lower the effective threshold below the surveyed
        floor. Below-reference vulnerability is expressed elsewhere --- through
        the signed \emph{stilt} on the damage curve (\texttt{MKM-DD-001}) ---
        so that vulnerability and resilience enter through separate, auditable
        channels rather than being netted inside one number.
  \item \textbf{It is banded, not continuous from zero.} No credit is awarded at
        or below a flood sub-score of 0.38 (the NR/B boundary); the full 3~m is
        awarded at or above 0.87 (the AA anchor); the credit ramps linearly in
        between. Resilience must be evidenced to a threshold standard before it
        earns anything.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Status:} Development (Tier 2, RAG Amber). The uplift ramp, the adjusted
floor level and the letter-grade fallback are delivered with unit tests. The
three anchors (\texttt{MAX\_M}, \texttt{SCORE\_LO}, \texttt{SCORE\_HI}) are
engineering-judgement seeds.

\textbf{Key risk:} A 3~m maximum uplift is very large relative to typical flood
depths in the modelled catchments. As the sensitivity analysis shows, an
AA-graded property with a 0.30~m surveyed floor does not flood until the water
is more than 3.3~m above ground --- which suppresses its flood event count to
near zero. The credit is therefore close to a binary exemption for top-graded
properties, and the anchor has no empirical basis. See BRF-L1.

% ===================================================================
% 2. MODEL PURPOSE AND SCOPE
% ===================================================================
\mkmpurpose

\subsection{Purpose}
To reward evidenced flood resilience in the flood threshold itself rather than
only in the damage curve. Physical flood-resilience measures --- raised
thresholds, flood doors, non-return valves, tanked basements, demountable
barriers --- act primarily by keeping water out, not by reducing damage once
water is in. Modelling them purely as a damage-curve adjustment would misplace
the mechanism; this model puts the credit where the physics is.

\subsection{Scope}
In scope: the mapping from BRI flood sub-score to a floor-level uplift in
metres; the addition of that uplift to the surveyed floor level; and the
representative-score fallback for assets that carry a BRI letter grade instead
of a numeric sub-score.

\subsection{Out of Scope}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Producing the BRI flood sub-score --- owned by \texttt{MKM-BRI-001}.
  \item The damage curve and its signed stilt --- owned by
        \texttt{MKM-DD-001}.
  \item The water-surface elevation and its attenuation --- owned by the flood
        propagation and spatial models.
  \item Surveying the floor level. \texttt{FloorLevelMeters} is a CDM input.
  \item Any assessment of whether the resilience measures are correctly
        installed, maintained or operable at the time of an event.
\end{itemize}

% ===================================================================
% 3. MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK
% ===================================================================
\mkmframework

\subsection{The Uplift Ramp}
Let $B_f \in [0,1]$ be the BRI flood sub-score, with anchors
$s_{\text{lo}} = 0.38$, $s_{\text{hi}} = 0.87$ and maximum uplift
$U_{\max} = 3.00$~m. The credit is
\[
  U(B_f) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    0, & B_f \le s_{\text{lo}}, \\[4pt]
    \dfrac{B_f - s_{\text{lo}}}{s_{\text{hi}} - s_{\text{lo}}}\; U_{\max},
       & s_{\text{lo}} < B_f < s_{\text{hi}}, \\[8pt]
    U_{\max}, & B_f \ge s_{\text{hi}}.
  \end{cases}
\]
The score is clamped to $[0,1]$ before evaluation. $U$ is continuous,
non-decreasing and bounded --- three properties the unit tests assert directly.

\subsection{Adjusted Floor Level}
\[
  F_{\text{adj}} \;=\; F_{\text{surveyed}} + U(B_f) \;\ge\; F_{\text{surveyed}}.
\]
Consumers compare $\text{GroundLevelMeters} + F_{\text{adj}}$ against the
attenuated water-surface elevation. The property floods when the water clears
the adjusted level.

\subsection{Anchor Placement}
The two score anchors are not arbitrary: they are the boundaries of the BRI
letter-grade bands (AA $\ge 0.87$, A $\ge 0.62$, B $\ge 0.38$, NR $< 0.38$).
Placing $s_{\text{lo}}$ at the NR/B boundary means an unrated property earns
nothing; placing $s_{\text{hi}}$ at the AA anchor means the full credit requires
top-band evidence. A property at the B/A boundary (0.62) earns
$(0.62-0.38)/(0.87-0.38) \times 3.00 = 1.47$~m, just under half.

\subsection{Letter-Grade Fallback}
Commercial assets record flood resilience as a Water/Flash grade envelope rather
than the residential numeric sub-score. For these, a representative score is
taken as the mid-point of the grade's band:
\[
  \text{AA} \to 0.935,\quad \text{A} \to 0.745,\quad
  \text{B} \to 0.500,\quad \text{NR} \to 0.190,
\]
and the continuous ramp is evaluated there. A trailing $+$ modifier is stripped
before lookup. An unknown, absent or ``N/A'' grade returns no representative
score --- the caller decides how to treat a non-applicable grade, rather than
the model silently substituting one.

Note the asymmetry this creates: a B-graded commercial asset evaluates at 0.500
and earns 0.73~m of credit, whereas an NR-graded one evaluates at 0.190 and
earns nothing, because 0.190 sits below $s_{\text{lo}}$.

\subsection{Relationship to the Damage-Curve Stilt}
The two BRI channels are complementary and must not be confused:

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{The two BRI flood channels}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{Floor uplift (this model)} & \textbf{Stilt (\texttt{MKM-DD-001})} \\
    \midrule
    Sign      & non-negative only & signed \\
    Cap       & $+3.00$~m & $\pm 1.00$~m \\
    Form      & piecewise-linear ramp & log-differential \\
    Acts on   & the flood \emph{trigger} & the \emph{damage} given flooding \\
    Drives    & PRS event count & loss severity \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

A property with a poor score is penalised through a negative stilt, not through
a negative uplift. A property with a strong score is rewarded through both --- it
floods less often \emph{and} takes less damage when it does. This double credit
is intentional but should be understood when attributing spread movements.

\subsection{Calibration Status}
The anchors are engineering-judgement seeds. $s_{\text{lo}}$ and $s_{\text{hi}}$
are defensible independently of loss data because they are defined by the BRI
grade bands, which are themselves the reviewed output of \texttt{MKM-BRI-001}.
$U_{\max}$ is not: 3~m is a judgement about how much water a
comprehensively-protected building can exclude, and it should be recalibrated
against post-event resilience performance as soon as loss data allows. This is
remediation item BRF-R1.

% 5. IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
% ===================================================================
\mkmimplementation

The model is implemented alongside the flood damage curve in
\texttt{src/models/floodrisk/depth\_damage.py}, which is the natural home
because the two share the BRI inputs and are always configured together.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Public functions}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Function} & \textbf{Returns} \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{bri\_floor\_uplift(score)} & credit in metres, $[0, U_{\max}]$ \\
    \texttt{bri\_adjusted\_floor\_level(floor, score)} & adjusted level in metres \\
    \texttt{flood\_rating\_to\_score(rating)} & representative score or \texttt{None} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

All three are pure functions of their arguments --- no configuration is read at
call time beyond the module-level constants, no I/O is performed, and no state
is retained. This makes the model trivially testable and means a change to the
anchors takes effect uniformly on the next process start.

\subsection{Consumer Contract}
Consumers must apply the adjusted floor level to the \emph{trigger} comparison
only. Passing $F_{\text{adj}}$ into \texttt{bri\_depth\_damage} as the
\texttt{floor\_level} argument would apply the uplift a second time, once
through the trigger and once through the effective-depth subtraction, and would
also compound with the stilt. The damage curve takes the \emph{surveyed} floor
level.

% ===================================================================
% 6. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
% ===================================================================
\mkmsensitivity
\label{sec:sensitivity}

Generated by calling the model directly
(\texttt{python -m docs.models.sensitivities.generate\_all --model brifloor}).

The material observations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The credit dominates the surveyed floor level.} Typical surveyed
        floors span 0 to 1.5~m; the credit spans 0 to 3~m. For an AA property
        the credit is the dominant term in the effective threshold by a factor
        of two or more.
  \item \textbf{The trigger table is the model's real output.} For a 0.30~m
        surveyed floor, NR floods at 0.5~m of water while AA needs more than
        3.3~m. Across a storm set where most events produce sub-metre depths,
        this is close to an exemption for AA properties --- the mechanism behind
        BRF-L1.
  \item \textbf{Grade granularity is coarse at the bottom.} NR (0.190) and any
        score below 0.38 all earn exactly zero, so the model cannot distinguish
        degrees of poor resilience. That distinction is left to the stilt.
  \item \textbf{Residual damage falls steeply with grade.} At 1.5~m of water the
        baseline curve gives materially different damage across grades purely
        because the depth above the adjusted floor differs.
\end{itemize}

% 7. VALIDATION AND BACKTESTING
% ===================================================================
\mkmvalidation

\subsection{Verification Approach}
Verified by unit test against calibration-independent properties:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bounds.} $U \in [0, U_{\max}]$ for every input including
        out-of-range and negative scores, which are clamped.
  \item \textbf{Monotonicity.} $U$ is non-decreasing in the score.
  \item \textbf{Anchor exactness.} $U(s_{\text{lo}}) = 0$ and
        $U(s_{\text{hi}}) = U_{\max}$ exactly, with continuity across both
        boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Non-subtractivity.} $F_{\text{adj}} \ge F_{\text{surveyed}}$ for
        every score, including zero.
  \item \textbf{Grade mapping.} Each letter maps to its band mid-point; the
        $+$ modifier is stripped; unknown, empty, \texttt{None} and ``N/A''
        return \texttt{None} rather than a default score.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Backtesting}
No backtest has been performed. A meaningful test requires paired observations
of resilience grade, water level and whether the property actually took water
in --- available only from post-event survey. This is remediation item BRF-R1
and is a precondition of MRC sign-off.

\subsection{Portfolio-Level Check}
The most informative available check is the distribution of flood event counts
by BRI grade across the 10{,}000-storm run. A monotone decline in mean event
count from NR to AA is the expected signature; a collapse to zero at AA would
confirm BRF-L1 is binding in practice and would argue for reducing $U_{\max}$.

% ===================================================================
% 8. LIMITATIONS
% ===================================================================
\mkmlimitations
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{description}
  \item[BRF-L1 --- The maximum uplift may act as an exemption.] $U_{\max}=3$~m
        exceeds the flood depth of most simulated events. Top-graded properties
        may therefore record near-zero flood event counts and near-zero flood
        spread, which is a much stronger statement than the underlying evidence
        supports. \emph{Mitigation:} monitor the event-count distribution by
        grade; recalibrate $U_{\max}$ under BRF-R1.
  \item[BRF-L2 --- Resilience measures are assumed always operable.] Demountable
        barriers must be deployed, non-return valves must not be blocked, flood
        doors must be closed. The model credits installed capability, not
        realised performance, and applies no deployment-failure probability.
  \item[BRF-L3 --- No depth-duration dependence.] A property's ability to
        exclude water degrades with the duration of inundation and with
        hydrostatic pressure. The credit is a fixed height regardless of how
        long the water stands against it.
  \item[BRF-L4 --- Coarse at the low end.] All scores at or below 0.38 receive
        identical treatment, so the model cannot rank poor properties against
        each other.
  \item[BRF-L5 --- Grade mid-points for commercial assets.] Using band
        mid-points for letter-graded assets understates within-grade dispersion,
        and the choice of mid-point rather than, say, band floor is a
        conservative-vs-central judgement that has not been validated.
  \item[BRF-L6 --- Double credit with the stilt.] A high-scoring property is
        rewarded twice --- fewer trigger events and lower damage per event.
        Intentional, but it means grade sensitivity of the total spread is
        stronger than either channel alone would suggest.
  \item[BRF-L7 --- No interaction with groundwater or sewer surcharge.] The
        credit is applied against surface water level only; ingress paths that
        bypass the floor threshold are not represented.
\end{description}

\subsection{Remediation}
\begin{description}
  \item[BRF-R1] Recalibrate $U_{\max}$ against post-event resilience
        performance; precondition of sign-off (addresses BRF-L1).
  \item[BRF-R2] Introduce a deployment-reliability factor for measures that
        require human action (addresses BRF-L2).
  \item[BRF-R3] Add the by-grade flood event-count distribution to the standard
        monitoring pack.
  \item[BRF-R4] Document and test the consumer contract that the damage curve
        receives the surveyed --- not the adjusted --- floor level, with an
        assertion at the integration boundary.
\end{description}

% 9. GOVERNANCE VALIDATION QUESTIONS
% ===================================================================
\mkmgovernance

\mkmvqitem{1}{Model purpose and use}{Converts the BRI flood sub-score into an
additive floor-level credit and returns the effective flood threshold used by
the flood trigger. Directly drives PRS flood event counts.}

\mkmvqitem{2}{Conceptual soundness}{Crediting flood resilience at the trigger
rather than in the damage curve matches the physical mechanism of the measures
concerned. The ramp anchors are taken from the reviewed BRI grade bands rather
than chosen freely. The deliberate non-negativity keeps resilience and
vulnerability in separate, auditable channels.}

\mkmvqitem{3}{Data quality}{Inputs are the CDM surveyed floor level and the BRI
flood sub-score or letter grade. The model performs no imputation: an unmappable
grade returns \texttt{None} and the caller must decide. Coverage of
\texttt{FloorLevelMeters} and \texttt{BRIFloodScore} should be monitored, since
a missing floor level silently becomes a zero baseline at the call site.}

\mkmvqitem{4}{Assumptions}{Resilience measures are always operable (BRF-L2);
excluded-water capability is a fixed height independent of duration (BRF-L3);
grade mid-points represent letter-graded assets (BRF-L5).}

\mkmvqitem{5}{Implementation verification}{Unit tests cover the ramp bounds,
monotonicity, both anchors and their continuity, clamping of out-of-range
scores, non-subtractivity of the adjusted level, and every branch of the
grade-mapping fallback.}

\mkmvqitem{6}{Sensitivity and stability}{See Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}. The
response is monotone and, at the top of the range, very strong --- strong enough
that BRF-L1 is raised as the model's principal risk. The model is deterministic
and closed-form; stability is not a concern.}

\mkmvqitem{7}{Limitations and weaknesses}{Documented in Section~8
(BRF-L1--BRF-L7). BRF-L1 is the material one: an uncalibrated 3~m maximum that
can suppress the flood leg of top-graded properties almost entirely.}

\mkmvqitem{8}{Ongoing monitoring}{Not yet in production. Monitoring under
BRF-R3 covers the by-grade flood event-count distribution and CDM field
coverage.}
