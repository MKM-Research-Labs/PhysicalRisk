\mkmexecsummary
% =========================================================================

This document benchmarks the Mortgage Pricing Model (MKM-MP-001), which values a
flood-exposed residential mortgage from its amortisation schedule, an
affordability-based credit spread and a loss given default. It asks whether the code
computes what its model document (\texttt{mortgage\_pricer.pdf}, v1.1) says, and
whether what the document says is right. An earlier review suspected that the fair
value counts the expected loss twice.

\paragraph{The code computes its documented formulae.} Over \bmCases{} loans ---
loan-to-value 0.5 to 1.0, recovery haircuts 0 to 40\%, two incomes, two coupons,
three terms, discounted at the coupon and at a risk-free rate --- the payment, spread,
expected cashflows, expected losses and fair value agree with a 50-digit
implementation of Eqs.~1--24 to \bmCodeErr. \bmWolframConfirms

\paragraph{The expected loss is counted twice: confirmed.} The document's expected
cashflow (Eq.~19) pays the lender, in the month of default, the balance \emph{less}
the loss given default. The fair value (Eq.~24) then subtracts the present value of
that same loss again. Wolfram values the loan independently, as the expectation over
the month of default of the payments made before it and the recovery made in it,
and that value equals Eq.~22 alone, with no loss term. The model understates fair
value by the full PV of expected loss: on \bmDoubleShare\% of the loans in the grid,
up to \bmDoubleWorst\% of par, so a GBP~\bmWorstLoan{} loan worth
GBP~\bmWorstPath{} is valued at GBP~\bmWorstFv{} (Table~\ref{tab:bm-double}). The
expected-loss outputs are themselves right; the fair value and the discount to par
are not. The document's own sensitivity tables never show it, because their
baseline (LTV 0.67, haircut 25\%) has no loss given default.

\paragraph{Other findings} (Table~\ref{tab:bm-claims}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The recovery is measured a month late.} Eq.~19 pays $B_{i-1}-\mathrm{LGD}_i$
  with the loss on the end-of-month balance $B_i$, so when the loan is under water
  the lender recovers a month's principal more than the property fetches: up to
  \bmExcess\% of par.
\item \textbf{Payment sensitivity is understated (\S6.1).} A 100\,bp rise raises a
  25-year payment by \bmPayLow--\bmPayHigh\%, not 6--8\%.
\item \textbf{The zero-income fallback cannot trigger (\S3.2.4).} Income is floored
  at GBP~1 before the spread is computed, so zero income prices at
  \bmZeroIncome\%, not 15\%.
\item \textbf{Production does not discount as documented (\S3.8).} The loan
  calculator discounts at a risk-free rate, not the coupon; at the grid's rates that
  moves fair value by up to \bmDiscount\%, and the discount to par no longer measures
  credit alone (\S5.3).
\end{enumerate}
The document's Table~7 is reproduced exactly, the expected-loss bound of \S7.2.2
holds, and Wolfram proves the par identity of \S5.3 and \S7.2.1 symbolically.

validation (Q9).

\mkmframework
% =========================================================================

Following the document (\S3): monthly payment $M$, balances $B_0,\dots,B_n$, monthly
default probability $h=1-e^{-s/12}$, survival $S_i=(1-h)^i$,
$\mathrm{PD}_i=S_{i-1}-S_i$, recoverable value $R=(1-\delta)V$,
$\mathrm{LGD}_i=\max(0,B_i-R)$ and discount factors $D_i$. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{CF}}=\sum_i D_i\bigl[M S_i+(B_{i-1}-\mathrm{LGD}_i)\,\mathrm{PD}_i\bigr],\quad
\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{EL}}=\sum_i D_i\,\mathrm{LGD}_i\,\mathrm{PD}_i,\quad
\mathrm{FV}=\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{CF}}-\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{EL}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The pathwise value.} Let $\tau$ be the month of default, so that
$P(\tau=i)=\mathrm{PD}_i$ and $P(\tau>n)=S_n$. On the path where it defaults in month
$\tau$, the lender receives $M$ in each month before $\tau$ and the recovery
$B_{\tau-1}-\mathrm{LGD}_\tau$ in month $\tau$; on the path where it never defaults,
all $n$ payments. Taking the expectation,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{PV}]=\sum_{i=1}^n D_i\bigl[M\,P(\tau>i)+(B_{i-1}-\mathrm{LGD}_i)\,P(\tau=i)\bigr]
=\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{CF}}.
\end{equation}
The loss is already in the recovery. Subtracting $\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{EL}}$ counts it a
second time, so $\mathrm{FV}$ understates value by exactly $\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{EL}}$.
Either Eq.~19 should carry the gross balance $B_{i-1}$ and Eq.~24 subtract the loss,
or Eq.~19 should stand and Eq.~24 be $\mathrm{FV}=\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{CF}}$.

\paragraph{The recovery.} The property fetches $R$ whatever the balance, so the
recovery in month $i$ is $\min(B_{i-1},R)$. Eq.~19's $B_{i-1}-\max(0,B_i-R)$ equals
$R+(B_{i-1}-B_i)$ when $B_i>R$: a month's principal more.

\section{Benchmark Method}
% =========================================================================

The equations above are evaluated with \texttt{mpmath} at 50 significant digits, from
the document rather than the code; the loan-to-value ratio is taken as the double the
code compares against its bands, since a band edge such as 0.95 falls between the
exact ratio and its double. Wolfram evaluates, from each input's exact value at
60-digit precision, both the document's fair value and the pathwise value, the
latter as an expectation over a \texttt{GeometricDistribution} default month with the
payments' present values accumulated, and it proves symbolically that the
contractual payments discounted at the coupon are worth par.
