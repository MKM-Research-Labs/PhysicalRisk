% ===================================================================
% 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
% ===================================================================
\mkmexecsummary

The Insurance Premium Model calculates flood insurance premiums using a
deterministic multi-factor approach. A base rate per \pounds1,000 of property
value is multiplied by seven factor loadings drawn from configuration tables,
each representing a distinct risk dimension.

The premium formula is:
\[
    P = \frac{V}{1000} \times r_{\text{base}}
        \times f_{\text{type}} \times f_{\text{flood}} \times f_{\text{age}}
        \times f_{\text{construction}} \times f_{\text{area}}
        \times f_{\text{security}} \times f_{\text{claims}}
        + P_{\text{contents}}
\]
where $V$ is the property value, $r_{\text{base}}$ is the base rate, and each
$f$ is a multiplicative factor from the corresponding lookup table.

The final premium is clamped to configurable bounds (\texttt{MIN\_PREMIUM},
\texttt{MAX\_PREMIUM}) to ensure commercial viability and regulatory compliance.

\textbf{Key risk:} Factors are independent with no interaction terms. Cross-effects
(e.g.\ flood zone \texttimes{} construction type) are not captured.


% ===================================================================
% 2. MODEL PURPOSE AND SCOPE
% ===================================================================
\mkmpurpose

\subsection{Purpose}

The model serves two primary functions:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Premium quotation}: Compute annual flood insurance premiums for
          individual residential properties.
    \item \textbf{Portfolio pricing}: Support bulk premium calculation across
          property portfolios for underwriting and reserving.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope}

The model covers UK residential properties with flood exposure. Factor tables
are calibrated to UK market conditions and EA flood zone classifications.

% 3. METHODOLOGY

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Factor Structure}

Seven independent multiplicative factors adjust the base premium:

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Premium factor dimensions.}
    \begin{tabular}{lll}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Factor} & \textbf{Input} & \textbf{Source Table} \\
        \midrule
        Property type & Detached / Semi / Terrace / Flat & \texttt{PROPERTY\_TYPE\_PREMIUM\_FACTORS} \\
        Flood risk zone & EA flood zone classification & \texttt{FLOOD\_RISK\_PREMIUM\_FACTORS} \\
        Building age & Construction year $\to$ band & \texttt{AGE\_PREMIUM\_FACTORS} \\
        Construction type & Brick / Timber / Concrete / Steel & \texttt{CONSTRUCTION\_TYPE\_FACTORS} \\
        Floor area & $m^2$ banded & \texttt{AREA\_PREMIUM\_BANDS} \\
        Security & Alarm / locks / flood gates & Fixed discount \\
        Claims history & Prior claims count & Loading multiplier \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Age Banding}

Properties are classified into four age bands based on construction year:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{New build}: age $< 10$ years
    \item \textbf{Modern}: $10 \leq$ age $< 30$ years
    \item \textbf{Established}: $30 \leq$ age $< 100$ years
    \item \textbf{Heritage}: age $\geq 100$ years
\end{itemize}

Each band maps to a factor range $(f_{\min}, f_{\max})$ from configuration.

\subsection{Premium Bounds}

The calculated premium is clamped:
\[
    P_{\text{final}} = \max\!\big(P_{\min},\; \min(P_{\max},\; P_{\text{raw}})\big)
\]
ensuring that no premium falls below the minimum viable level or exceeds the
regulatory cap.

\section{Inputs and Outputs}

\subsection{Inputs}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Property value (\pounds).
    \item Base rate per \pounds1,000 (from \texttt{BASE\_RATE\_RANGE}).
    \item Property type, flood zone, construction year, construction type, floor area.
    \item Security features (boolean flags).
    \item Claims history count.
    \item Contents premium add-on (optional).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Outputs}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Annual insurance premium (\pounds), clamped to bounds.
\end{itemize}


% ===================================================================
% 5. ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS
% ===================================================================
\section{Assumptions and Limitations}

\subsection{Assumptions}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item All premium factors are independent (no interaction effects).
    \item Factor tables are calibrated to current UK market conditions.
    \item Property value is an accurate reflection of rebuilding cost.
    \item EA flood zone classifications are current and accurate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item No cross-factor interactions (e.g.\ flood zone $\times$ age).
    \item Factor tables are static and require periodic recalibration.
    \item No explicit modelling of climate change trends on flood premiums.
    \item Contents premium is an additive flat amount, not risk-adjusted.
\end{enumerate}

% 7. VALIDATION QUESTIONS (HANDBOOK CHAPTER 5)
% ===================================================================
\section{Validation Questions}

\mkmvqitem{1}{Purpose}{Is the model being used for its intended purpose?}
Yes. The model calculates flood insurance premiums for UK residential properties,
consistent with its design scope.

\mkmvqitem{2}{Conceptual Soundness}{Is the model conceptually sound?}
The multiplicative factor approach is standard actuarial practice for property
insurance rating. Factor independence is a simplification but is well-understood.

\mkmvqitem{3}{Data Quality}{Is the data quality adequate?}
Factor tables are derived from industry data and EA classifications. Property
data quality depends on upstream data feeds.

\mkmvqitem{4}{Developmental Evidence}{Is there adequate developmental evidence?}
Factor tables are sourced from published industry rating guides. Unit tests
verify calculation correctness and boundary clamping.

\mkmvqitem{5}{Outcomes Analysis}{Are outcomes reasonable?}
Premium outputs are validated against market benchmarks for representative
property archetypes.

\mkmvqitem{6}{Ongoing Monitoring}{What ongoing monitoring is required?}
Annual review of factor tables against market loss experience. Quarterly
monitoring of premium distribution statistics.

\mkmvqitem{7}{Benchmarking}{Has the model been benchmarked?}
Premiums are compared against Flood Re pricing and commercial insurance quotes
for representative properties.

\mkmvqitem{8}{Monitoring}{What type of monitoring is required?}
Monitor premium adequacy ratios (premium / expected loss) by flood zone.
Track claims frequency and severity against premium assumptions.

\mkmvqitem{9}{Overall Risk}{Is the overall model risk acceptable?}
The model is a standard actuarial rating tool. Risk is Low provided factor
tables are recalibrated annually.
