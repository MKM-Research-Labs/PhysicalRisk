This document presents the Property Valuation and Insurance Premium models
developed by MKM Research Labs for the Physical Risk platform. The models
serve two complementary purposes:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Property Valuation} --- a multi-factor deterministic model
          that estimates residential property values within the London market,
          incorporating property characteristics, flood risk exposure, energy
          performance, and Thames proximity;
    \item \textbf{Insurance Premium Calculation} --- a factor-based model that
          derives annual buildings and contents insurance premiums from the
          property value and a comprehensive set of risk-adjusted multipliers.
\end{enumerate}

Both models are calibrated to London residential market data and are
designed for use within the broader Physical Risk assessment framework.
They employ bounded factor tables with (min, max) ranges, allowing the
portfolio generator to introduce controlled stochastic variation whilst
preserving economically meaningful relationships between property
attributes and valuations.

A supplementary \textbf{Rental Yield} sub-model provides monthly rent
estimates by blending yield-on-value and per-square-metre approaches,
supporting mortgage affordability and buy-to-let analyses.

\textbf{Key model outputs:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Property valuations bounded to \pounds150,000--\pounds5,000,000
          (London residential range).
    \item Annual insurance premiums bounded to \pounds200--\pounds20,000.
    \item Monthly rental estimates derived from dual-method averaging.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model Purpose}

The Property Valuation model generates realistic, internally consistent
property valuations for use in flood risk portfolio analysis, mortgage
pricing, and insurance premium estimation. The model is \emph{not} intended
as a point-estimate valuation tool for individual properties; rather, it
provides a statistically calibrated framework for generating portfolios of
properties with valuations that reflect the joint distribution of property
characteristics observed in the London market.

The Insurance Premium model translates property valuations and risk
characteristics into annual premium estimates, enabling the platform to
assess insurance costs under varying flood risk scenarios and to quantify
the financial impact of physical risk on property portfolios.

\subsection{Scope}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Geographic scope:} London and the Thames catchment.
          Factor tables are calibrated to London market data (Land Registry,
          ONS House Price Index).
    \item \textbf{Property types:} Six residential categories --- Flat,
          Mid-terrace, End-terrace, Semi-detached, Detached, and Bungalow.
    \item \textbf{Risk factors:} Five flood risk categories (Very Low to
          Very High), seven EPC ratings (A--G), five condition grades, five
          age bands, and four Thames proximity zones.
    \item \textbf{Temporal scope:} Static factor tables calibrated to
          2024--2025 market conditions. No time-varying market dynamics.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Regulatory Context}

The models support Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) SS3/19
expectations regarding climate-related financial risk management and the
Bank of England's Climate Biennial Exploratory Scenario (CBES) requirements
for physical risk assessment of mortgage portfolios. The documentation
follows the SR 11-7 / SS1/23 model risk management framework.

% ── 3.1 Property Valuation ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Property Valuation}

The property valuation model computes:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:valuation}
V = A \times P_{\mathrm{sqm}} \times f_{\mathrm{loc}} \times f_{\mathrm{age}}
    \times f_{\mathrm{cond}} \times f_{\mathrm{flood}} \times f_{\mathrm{epc}}
    \times f_{\mathrm{prox}} \times \varepsilon
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item $A$ is the floor area in square metres,
    \item $P_{\mathrm{sqm}}$ is the base price per square metre (GBP),
    \item $f_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is a location-based value multiplier,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{age}}$ is the construction age adjustment factor,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{cond}}$ is the property condition factor,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{flood}}$ is the flood risk discount/premium factor,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{epc}}$ is the energy performance certificate adjustment,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{prox}}$ is the Thames proximity adjustment, and
    \item $\varepsilon$ is a random noise factor (default $\varepsilon = 1.0$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent The final value is clamped to bounds:
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{final}} = \max\bigl(\pounds150{,}000,\;\min(\pounds5{,}000{,}000,\;\text{round}(V, 2))\bigr)
\end{equation}

Each factor is drawn uniformly from a $(\min, \max)$ range defined by the
relevant factor table. The following subsections document every factor table
exactly as implemented in the source code.

% ── 3.2 Insurance Premium ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Insurance Premium}

The insurance premium model computes:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:premium}
P = \frac{V}{1{,}000} \times r_{\mathrm{base}} \times f_{\mathrm{type}}
    \times f_{\mathrm{flood}} \times f_{\mathrm{age}}
    \times f_{\mathrm{constr}} \times f_{\mathrm{area}}
    \times f_{\mathrm{sec}} \times f_{\mathrm{claims}}
    + P_{\mathrm{contents}}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item $V$ is the property value in GBP,
    \item $r_{\mathrm{base}}$ is the base rate per \pounds1,000 of property value,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{type}}$ is the property type factor,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{flood}}$ is the flood risk premium multiplier,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{age}}$ is the building age factor,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{constr}}$ is the construction type factor,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{area}}$ is the floor area factor,
    \item $f_{\mathrm{sec}}$ is the security discount factor (default 1.0),
    \item $f_{\mathrm{claims}}$ is the claims history multiplier (default 1.0), and
    \item $P_{\mathrm{contents}}$ is the additional contents premium (default \pounds0).
\end{itemize}

\noindent The final premium is clamped:
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{final}} = \max\bigl(\pounds200,\;\min(\pounds20{,}000,\;\text{round}(P, 2))\bigr)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Base Rate}

The base rate $r_{\mathrm{base}}$ is drawn from the range:
\[
r_{\mathrm{base}} \in [1.5,\; 4.0] \quad \text{per \pounds1,000 of property value}
\]

\noindent This range is calibrated to ABI household insurance premium
survey data for London.

% ── 3.3 Rental Yield ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Rental Yield}

Monthly rent is estimated by averaging two independent methods:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rent}
R_{\mathrm{monthly}} = \frac{1}{2}\left(R_{\mathrm{yield}} + R_{\mathrm{sqm}}\right) \times \text{adjustments}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{align}
R_{\mathrm{yield}} &= \frac{V \times y}{12} & \text{(yield method)} \\
R_{\mathrm{sqm}} &= A \times r_{\mathrm{sqm}} & \text{(per-square-metre method)}
\end{align}

\subsection{Calibration Sources}

Factor table ranges are calibrated using the following data sources:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{HM Land Registry Price Paid Data} --- Transaction prices
          by property type, postcode, and date. Used to calibrate
          \texttt{BASE\_PRICE\_PER\_SQM} ranges.
    \item \textbf{ONS House Price Index (HPI)} --- Regional and
          property-type indices used to validate relative price
          differentials.
    \item \textbf{EPC Register} --- Distribution of EPC ratings across
          London boroughs, used to validate rating-value relationships.
    \item \textbf{Environment Agency Flood Map for Planning} --- Flood
          zone classifications used to calibrate flood risk categories.
    \item \textbf{ABI Household Insurance Premium Tracker} --- Average
          premiums by region and cover type, used to calibrate base rate
          range and validate premium output distributions.
    \item \textbf{Flood Re Transition Plan Data} --- Premium loadings for
          high-risk properties, used to validate flood risk premium factors.
    \item \textbf{Valuation Office Agency (VOA) Council Tax Bands} ---
          Cross-referenced for valuation plausibility checks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Source Code Organisation}

The model is implemented across two Python modules:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{src/models/valuation/property\_value.py} --- Property
          valuation factor tables, age band classification, proximity zone
          classification, and the \texttt{apply\_valuation\_factors()}
          function.
    \item \texttt{src/models/valuation/insurance.py} --- Insurance premium
          factor tables, age premium band classification, area premium band
          lookup, and the \texttt{apply\_insurance\_factors()} function.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational Flow}

\subsubsection{Valuation}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Determine property type $\rightarrow$ look up area range and
          price-per-sqm range.
    \item Draw floor area $A$ uniformly from type-specific range.
    \item Draw $P_{\mathrm{sqm}}$ uniformly from type-specific range.
    \item Classify construction year into age band via
          \texttt{get\_age\_band()}.
    \item Classify Thames proximity via \texttt{get\_proximity\_zone()},
          which jointly considers distance and flood risk.
    \item Draw each factor uniformly from its table-specified $(\min, \max)$
          range.
    \item Compute $V$ via Equation~(\ref{eq:valuation}).
    \item Clamp to $[\pounds150{,}000,\;\pounds5{,}000{,}000]$ and round
          to two decimal places.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Insurance}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Take property value $V$ from the valuation model.
    \item Draw base rate $r_{\mathrm{base}}$ from $[1.5, 4.0]$.
    \item Look up property type, flood risk, construction type, and
          construction age factors from their respective tables.
    \item Classify floor area into premium band via
          \texttt{get\_area\_premium\_factor\_range()}.
    \item Draw each factor uniformly from its $(\min, \max)$ range.
    \item Compute $P$ via Equation~(\ref{eq:premium}).
    \item Clamp to $[\pounds200,\;\pounds20{,}000]$ and round to two
          decimal places.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Numerical Considerations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item All monetary values are computed in GBP and rounded to two
          decimal places at the final step only (no intermediate rounding).
    \item Factor multiplication is commutative; the ordering in source code
          is for readability only.
    \item The clamping bounds prevent implausible outliers when extreme
          factor combinations coincide (e.g., a maximum-area Detached
          property with all factors at their upper bounds).
    \item No external numerical libraries are required; the model uses
          only Python standard library arithmetic.
\end{itemize}

\mkmlimitations
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The following limitations should be considered when using the model outputs:

\subsection{London-Specific Calibration}

All factor tables are calibrated to the London residential market. The
price-per-square-metre ranges, rental yields, and area-based assumptions
reflect London-specific market conditions and are \textbf{not transferable}
to other UK regions without recalibration. In particular:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Base prices (\pounds5,500--\pounds15,000/m\textsuperscript{2})
          significantly exceed national averages.
    \item The Thames proximity factor is specific to the River Thames and
          would require reformulation for other river systems.
    \item Rental yield rates reflect London's low-yield, high-capital-growth
          market profile.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Static Factor Tables}

Factor tables are fixed at calibration time and do not incorporate:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Time-varying market conditions (house price inflation/deflation).
    \item Seasonal variation in property values or insurance premiums.
    \item Post-event market impacts (e.g., valuation drops following a
          major flood).
    \item Regulatory changes (e.g., future MEES tightening, Flood Re
          transition milestones).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Independence of Factors}

The multiplicative model assumes all factors are conditionally independent
given the property type. In practice, correlations exist:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Older properties (heritage/period) tend to have lower EPC ratings.
    \item Properties in poor condition are more likely to be in flood-prone
          areas (selection effects).
    \item Construction type is correlated with age (timber frame is rare
          in period properties).
\end{itemize}

\noindent These correlations are partially addressed by the portfolio
generator's property-level logic but are not enforced within the factor
model itself.

\subsection{Simplified Insurance Model}

The insurance model does not account for:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Individual underwriting criteria (excess levels, no-claims
          discounts, named perils).
    \item Flood Re eligibility and ceded premium mechanics.
    \item Contents valuation (treated as an additive flat amount).
    \item Multi-peril interactions (e.g., subsidence risk correlated with
          flood risk in clay soil areas).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Valuation Bounds}

The hard bounds (\pounds150,000--\pounds5,000,000 for valuation,
\pounds200--\pounds20,000 for premiums) truncate the tails of the
output distribution. This is intentional for portfolio generation purposes
but means the model cannot represent:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Ultra-prime London properties ($>\pounds5$M).
    \item Social housing or shared-ownership properties ($<\pounds150$K).
    \item Commercial or mixed-use properties.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{No Spatial Autocorrelation}

The model treats each property independently and does not model spatial
autocorrelation in property values. Neighbouring properties will not
exhibit the correlated valuations observed in practice unless the portfolio
generator explicitly introduces spatial structure.

\mkmgovernance

\mkmvqitem{1}{Ronseal Test}{Does the model do what it says on the tin?}
Yes. The model delivers its three stated functions: (i)~multi-factor property valuation using seven factor tables (area, price per sqm, age, condition, flood risk, EPC rating, Thames proximity), producing valuations bounded to the London residential range (\pounds150,000--\pounds5,000,000); (ii)~insurance premium calculation from a comprehensive set of risk-adjusted multipliers, bounded to \pounds200--\pounds20,000; and (iii)~rental yield estimation via dual-method averaging. All outputs are produced and consumed downstream.

\mkmvqitem{2}{Accuracy and Stability}{Does the model provide timely, accurate and stable results?}
The model is deterministic: identical property attributes produce identical valuations. Factor tables are calibrated to London market data (Land Registry, ONS House Price Index). The bounded factor ranges (min, max) allow the portfolio generator to introduce controlled stochastic variation whilst preserving economically meaningful relationships. Valuation and premium calculations are pure arithmetic operations completing in under 1\,ms per property. Portfolio generation (5,000 properties) completes in under 3 seconds.

\mkmvqitem{3}{Resource Usage}{Does the model consume excessive computational resources?}
No. The model performs simple table lookups and multiplicative factor application with no optimisation, simulation, or numerical solving. Computation is $O(1)$ per property. Memory usage is negligible (factor tables total fewer than 200 entries). The model has no external dependencies beyond base Python. Batch processing scales linearly with portfolio size.

\mkmvqitem{4}{Override Controls}{Are there controls over manual overrides?}
All factor tables and value bounds are defined as class-level constants and are not overridable at runtime. The stochastic variation in generated portfolios is controlled by a random seed for reproducibility. No mechanism exists for manual override of individual property valuations or insurance premiums. Changes to factor tables require code modification subject to version control and code review. The valuation methodology is fully transparent and auditable.

\mkmvqitem{5}{Output Consumption}{How is the output consumed for risk decision-making?}
Property valuations are consumed by: (a)~the Mortgage Credit Risk Pricer (MKM-MORT-001) for LTV calculation and expected loss estimation; (b)~the Risk Assessment Engine (MKM-RISK-001) for value-at-risk computation; (c)~the Flood Risk Model (MKM-FLOOD-001) for loss magnitude estimation. Insurance premiums inform affordability assessment and total cost of ownership analysis. Rental yields support buy-to-let mortgage underwriting. Results are rendered in property-level PDF reports and the interactive dashboard.

\mkmvqitem{6}{Model Chaining}{Is the model's output used as input to another model?}
Yes. The model is an upstream component with no model dependencies of its own (standalone factor model). Direct downstream consumers: (i)~Mortgage Credit Risk Pricer (MKM-MORT-001), which uses property values for LTV and LGD; (ii)~Risk Assessment Engine (MKM-RISK-001), which uses property values for value-at-risk; (iii)~Flood Risk Model (MKM-FLOOD-001), which uses property values for loss magnitude; (iv)~PRS Pricing Engine (MKM-PRS-001), which uses property characteristics for basis waterfall inputs.

\mkmvqitem{7}{Weaknesses}{Does the model have weaknesses and limitations of use?}
Yes; the principal limitations are documented in Section~8. Key weaknesses: (i)~London-specific calibration (factor tables may not generalise to other UK regions); (ii)~deterministic factor application (no stochastic valuation uncertainty); (iii)~no temporal dynamics (property values do not evolve over time within a simulation); (iv)~simplified EPC and flood risk factor tables that may not capture non-linear interactions between property attributes; (v)~six residential property types only (no commercial properties).

\mkmvqitem{8}{Monitoring}{What type of monitoring is required?}
Monitoring should include: (a)~annual recalibration of base price-per-sqm factors against updated Land Registry transaction data; (b)~validation of generated portfolio value distributions against ONS House Price Index quartiles; (c)~review of flood risk discount factors against published literature on flood risk capitalisation; (d)~EPC factor review following updates to energy performance regulations; (e)~insurance premium factor comparison against ABI published premium data.

\mkmvqitem{9}{Overall Risk}{Is the overall model risk acceptable?}
Acceptable. As a Tier~2 model, the Property Valuation Engine produces important but intermediate outputs. The factor-table approach is transparent, auditable, and conservative (bounded ranges prevent extreme valuations). The London-specific calibration is appropriate for the current Thames catchment scope. The primary risk---staleness of factor tables---is mitigated by the annual recalibration cycle. Extension to other UK regions would require a dedicated recalibration exercise and is flagged as a future development item.
