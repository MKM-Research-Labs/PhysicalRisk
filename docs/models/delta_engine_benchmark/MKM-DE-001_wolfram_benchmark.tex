\mkmexecsummary
% =========================================================================

This document benchmarks the Delta Engine (MKM-DE-001), which values and risk-manages
the PRS book: for every trade it computes the risky annuity, the flood sensitivity
FS01 and the mark-to-market. It runs in production, behind the trading routes, the
blotter, the XVA service and the historical end-of-day series. The fair spread it
differences is PR-001's, benchmarked separately.

\paragraph{The code computes its documented formulae, to the precision it reports.}
Over \bmCases{} cases --- annual hazard rates \bmRates, tenors 1 to 10 years, a flat
3\% curve and the book's yield curve, notional GBP~\bmNotional{} --- the risky annuity
agrees with a 50-digit reference to \bmAnnuity, and the spread change, FS01 and the
returned annuity agree to within half a unit of the rounding the engine applies.
\bmWolframConfirms{} The exception is mark-to-market: the engine reuses the annuity
\emph{after} rounding it to six decimal places, which moves MTM by up to
GBP~\bmMtm{} (GBP~\bmMtmLow{} for hazard rates up to 5\%). That is immaterial on a
GBP~\bmNotional{} trade, but it is an error, and the fix is to round only for display.

\paragraph{Findings on the model document} (Table~\ref{tab:bm-claims}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{FS01 does not scale with the hazard rate.} The document says it scales
  approximately linearly over 50--500\,bp. It barely moves: FS01 at 500\,bp is
  \bmLinear{} times FS01 at 50\,bp, not ten times (Table~\ref{tab:bm-fs01}). A 1\,bp
  rise in $h$ raises the spread by about 1\,bp at any level, so FS01 is set by the
  risky annuity and the notional.
\item \textbf{The yield-curve sensitivity is overstated.} A 100\,bp parallel rise in
  yields lowers FS01 by \bmYieldLow--\bmYieldHigh\%, not the 3--5\% stated.
\item \textbf{The hand calculation is wrong.} The 3-year annuity at 200\,bp and 4\% is
  \bmHand, not \bmHandStated. The model computes it correctly.
\item \textbf{FS01 is blind below the spread floor.} At $h=\bmFloorH$ the fair spread
  sits on the 2\,bp floor, so the 1\,bp bump moves it by \bmFloorModel\,bp where the
  true sensitivity is \bmFloorGrad\,bp: a position on a quiet gauge reports almost
  no flood risk.
\item \textbf{Basis delta is the gauge delta by construction.} The code ignores the
  basis it is given; the document's approximation is exact only because nothing
  else is computed.
\end{enumerate}
Two claims hold: the 1\,bp bump is within \bmFd\,bp of the exact gradient (the
document says $<0.01$\,bp), and a 5-year trade carries \bmTenor{} times the FS01 of
a 1-year (stated 4.5). A \bmMove\,bp move is \bmConvexity\% from full revaluation.

\subsection{What is benchmarked}
\texttt{compute\_risky\_annuity}, \texttt{compute\_gauge\_delta},
\texttt{compute\_basis\_delta} and \texttt{compute\_mark\_to\_market} in
\texttt{src/models/trading/delta\_engine/pricer.py}, called as
\texttt{DeltaEngine.enrich\_trade} calls them, with the rounding they apply.

\subsection{What is not}
The fair spread itself (MKM-PR-001's benchmark); the market-state inputs the trader
maintains; the risk-grid aggregation, which is a sum. The document's architecture
section names \texttt{delta\_engine.py}, which is now a package. This is a benchmark
of the implementation (validation question Q8), not independent validation (Q9).

\mkmframework
% =========================================================================

With annual hazard rate $h$, $\lambda=-\ln(1-h)$, quarterly dates $t_i=i/4$ and
discount $D(t)=e^{-r(t)t}$, the document defines
\begin{equation}
A(h,T)=\sum_{i=1}^{4T}\tfrac14\,e^{-\lambda t_i}D(t_i),\qquad
\text{FS01}=\delta\,\frac{s(h+10^{-4})-s(h)}{10^4}\,A(h,T)\,N,\qquad
\text{MTM}=\delta\,\frac{s-s_0}{10^4}\,A\,N,
\end{equation}
with $s$ PR-001's fair spread, floored at 2\,bp, $\delta=\pm1$ for a payer or receiver,
and $N$ the notional.

\paragraph{Why FS01 is flat in $h$.} On a flat curve PR-001's spread has the closed form
$s=10^4(1-R)\,(e^{\lambda\Delta t}-1)\,e^{r\Delta t/2}/\Delta t$, so
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}h}=10^4\,(1-R)\,\frac{e^{\lambda\Delta t}\,e^{r\Delta t/2}}{1-h}
\approx 10^4 .
\end{equation}
A 1\,bp bump in $h$ moves the spread by about 1\,bp at any level, and FS01 is about
$10^{-4}A\,N$: it follows the annuity, which falls slowly as $h$ rises.

% =========================================================================
\section{Benchmark Method}
% =========================================================================

The equations above are evaluated with \texttt{mpmath} at 50 significant digits, from
the documents rather than the code; the exact gradient $\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}h$ is a
high-precision central difference, checked against the closed form above. Wolfram
evaluates the same sums exactly, with the book curve as a piecewise function,
differentiates the spread symbolically, and evaluates the bumped spread at the very
double the engine computes, $h+10^{-4}$ in floating point. The engine rounds what it
returns, so each rounded output is judged to half a unit in its last place. MTM uses
an inception spread of \bmTradeSpread\,bp.
