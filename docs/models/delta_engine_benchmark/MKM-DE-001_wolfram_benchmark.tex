\mkmexecsummary
% =========================================================================

This document benchmarks the Delta Engine (MKM-DE-001), which values and risk-manages
the PRS book: for every trade it computes the risky annuity, the flood sensitivity
FS01 and the mark-to-market. It runs in production, behind the trading routes, the
blotter, the XVA service and the historical end-of-day series. The fair spread it
differences is PR-001's, benchmarked separately.

\paragraph{The code computes its documented formulae, to the precision it reports.}
Over \bmCases{} cases --- annual hazard rates \bmRates, tenors 1 to 10 years, a flat
3\% curve and the book's yield curve, notional GBP~\bmNotional{} --- the risky annuity
agrees with a 50-digit reference to \bmAnnuity, and the spread change, FS01, the
returned annuity, the hazard sensitivity and mark-to-market agree to within half a
unit of the rounding the engine applies. \bmWolframConfirms

\paragraph{What changed since v0.1.} Benchmark v0.1 tested the engine against model
document v1.0 and found two defects in the code and three errors in the document:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{MTM used a rounded annuity}, rounded to six places for display, which
  moved MTM by up to GBP~3.40 on GBP~10m. The engine now values with the unrounded
  annuity; MTM now agrees to the penny.
\item \textbf{FS01 was silent on the spread floor.} At $h=\bmFloorH$ the fair spread
  sits on the 2\,bp floor and FS01 reflects a \bmFloorModel\,bp spread change ---
  the right P\&L sensitivity, since MTM does not move either, but blind to the flood
  risk. The engine now flags the floor and reports the hazard sensitivity beside
  FS01: \bmFloorHazard\,bp, against an exact \bmFloorGrad\,bp.
\item \textbf{The document} said FS01 scaled linearly with the hazard rate (it is
  nearly flat), put the yield sensitivity at 3--5\% (it is 0.4--2.4\%), and gave the
  worked annuity as 2.738 (it is 2.724084). Model document v1.1 corrects all three,
  states that the basis delta \emph{is} the gauge delta, and documents the floor.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The model document's claims} (v1.1, Table~\ref{tab:bm-claims}): all
\bmClaimsMet{} of the \bmClaimsTested{} claims tested hold, each to the precision the
document quotes: FS01 at 500\,bp is \bmLinear{} times FS01 at 50\,bp; a 100\,bp
yield rise lowers FS01 by \bmYieldLow--\bmYieldHigh\%; a 5-year trade carries
\bmTenor{} times the FS01 of a 1-year; the worked annuity is \bmHand; and the 1\,bp
bump is within \bmFd\,bp of the exact gradient. A \bmMove\,bp move is
\bmConvexity\% from full revaluation.

\subsection{What is benchmarked}
\texttt{compute\_risky\_annuity}, \texttt{compute\_gauge\_delta} (FS01, the floor
flag and the hazard sensitivity), \texttt{compute\_basis\_delta} and
\texttt{compute\_mark\_to\_market} in \texttt{src/models/trading/delta\_engine/pricer.py},
called as \texttt{DeltaEngine.enrich\_trade} calls them, with the rounding they apply,
against model document v1.1 (\texttt{delta\_engine\_v1.1.pdf}).

\subsection{What is not}
The fair spread itself (MKM-PR-001's benchmark); the market-state inputs the trader
maintains; the risk-grid aggregation, which is a sum. This is a benchmark
of the implementation (validation question Q8), not independent validation (Q9).

\mkmframework
% =========================================================================

With annual hazard rate $h$, $\lambda=-\ln(1-h)$, quarterly dates $t_i=i/4$ and
discount $D(t)=e^{-r(t)t}$, the document defines
\begin{equation}
A(h,T)=\sum_{i=1}^{4T}\tfrac14\,e^{-\lambda t_i}D(t_i),\qquad
\text{FS01}=\delta\,\frac{s(h+10^{-4})-s(h)}{10^4}\,A(h,T)\,N,\qquad
\text{MTM}=\delta\,\frac{s-s_0}{10^4}\,A\,N,
\end{equation}
with $s$ PR-001's fair spread, floored at 2\,bp, $\delta=\pm1$ for a payer or receiver,
and $N$ the notional.

\paragraph{Why FS01 is flat in $h$.} On a flat curve PR-001's spread has the closed form
$s=10^4(1-R)\,(e^{\lambda\Delta t}-1)\,e^{r\Delta t/2}/\Delta t$, so
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}h}=10^4\,(1-R)\,\frac{e^{\lambda\Delta t}\,e^{r\Delta t/2}}{1-h}
\approx 10^4 .
\end{equation}
A 1\,bp bump in $h$ moves the spread by about 1\,bp at any level, and FS01 is about
$10^{-4}A\,N$: it follows the annuity, which falls slowly as $h$ rises.

% =========================================================================
\section{Benchmark Method}
% =========================================================================

The equations above are evaluated with \texttt{mpmath} at 50 significant digits, from
the documents rather than the code; the exact gradient $\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}h$ is a
high-precision central difference, checked against the closed form above. Wolfram
evaluates the same sums exactly, with the book curve as a piecewise function,
differentiates the spread symbolically, and evaluates the bumped spread at the very
double the engine computes, $h+10^{-4}$ in floating point. The engine rounds what it
returns, so each rounded output is judged to half a unit in its last place. MTM uses
an inception spread of \bmTradeSpread\,bp.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Benchmark, not validation.} The engine is shown to compute its
  documented formulae; whether a parallel 1\,bp bump in the annual rate is the right
  risk measure is a separate question.
\item \textbf{The floor.} The engine now flags it, but risk limits and the risk grid
  aggregate FS01 alone; positions on the floor contribute almost nothing to them.
  Whether limits should also read the hazard sensitivity is for the desk and the
  model owner.
\item \textbf{Parallel bump only.} FS01 bumps the annual rate in parallel across
  tenors; term-structure and cross-gauge sensitivities are outside this benchmark.
\end{enumerate}
