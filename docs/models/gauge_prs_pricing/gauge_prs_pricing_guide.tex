\section{Introduction}
% =========================================================================

A \textbf{Physical Risk Swap} (PRS) is a credit-derivative-style contract
that transfers flood risk between two parties.  The protection buyer (payer)
pays a periodic premium in exchange for a contingent payment if a specified
flood event occurs at a river gauge.  The structure mirrors a single-name
Credit Default Swap (CDS), with the ``credit event'' replaced by a physical
flood event.

\subsection{Why PRS Exists}

Property owners, mortgage lenders, and insurers face concentrated exposure
to river flooding.  A PRS allows them to hedge this exposure by
transferring the risk to a willing counterparty (hedge fund, reinsurer, or
capital markets investor) in exchange for a spread.  The spread compensates
the protection seller for the probability of having to make a payout.

\subsection{Gauge vs.\ Property PRS}

A \textbf{Gauge PRS} references a single river gauge.  The trigger is
defined by a water level threshold (alert, warning, or severe) at that
gauge.  The pricing uses an analytical hazard-rate model.

A \textbf{Property PRS} references a specific property and its nearest
gauges.  It is priced from Monte Carlo event counts rather than
analytical hazard rates.  This guide covers gauge PRS; property PRS is
documented separately.

\section{From Hazard Rate to Fair Spread}
% =========================================================================

The pricing pipeline converts an annual flood probability into a fair
spread in basis points (bps).  This section walks through each step.

\subsection{Annual Probability to Continuous Hazard Rate}

The gauge hazard curve provides an \textbf{annual hazard rate} --- the
probability that a flood event occurs within one year.  For example, a
5\% annual probability at the severe trigger level.

The CDS pricing framework requires a continuous hazard rate $\lambda$,
converted via:

\[
  \lambda = -\ln(1 - p_{\mathrm{annual}})
\]

\noindent For $p = 0.05$: $\lambda \approx 0.0513$.

\subsection{Survival Probability}

The survival probability $S(t)$ at time $t$ (years) is the probability
that no flood event has occurred by time $t$:

\[
  S(t) = e^{-\lambda t}
\]

\noindent For $\lambda = 0.0513$ and $t = 5$: $S(5) = e^{-0.2565} \approx 0.7738$.

\subsection{Risky Annuity}

The risky annuity is the present value of \pounds1 paid quarterly, conditional
on no flood event having occurred, discounted at the risk-free rate:

\[
  A = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \Delta t \cdot S(t_i) \cdot D(t_i)
\]

\noindent where $\Delta t = 0.25$ (quarterly), $n = 4 \times \text{tenor}$,
and $D(t) = e^{-r(t) \cdot t}$ is the discount factor from the yield curve.

A typical 5-year risky annuity is in the range 3.5--5.0, depending on the
hazard rate and yield curve.  The risky annuity is the denominator of the
CDS spread formula and also serves as the DV01 (see Chapter~4).

\subsection{Protection Leg}

The protection leg is the present value of the payout made upon a flood
event, weighted by the probability of default in each period:

\[
  P = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (1 - R) \cdot \bigl(S(t_{i-1}) - S(t_i)\bigr)
      \cdot D(t_{\mathrm{mid},i})
\]

\noindent where $R$ is the recovery rate (default 0.0 for flood events,
meaning full loss) and $t_{\mathrm{mid},i}$ is the midpoint of period $i$.

\subsection{Fair Spread}

The fair spread equates the premium leg with the protection leg:

\[
  s_{\mathrm{fair}} = \frac{P}{A}
\]

\noindent Expressed in basis points:

\[
  s_{\mathrm{bps}} = s_{\mathrm{fair}} \times 10{,}000
\]

\subsection{Worked Example}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textbf{Input} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Annual hazard rate (severe)  & 5\% \\
Tenor                        & 5 years \\
Recovery                     & 0\% \\
Risk-free rate               & 3\% flat \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\lambda = -\ln(0.95) = 0.0513$
  \item Risky annuity $A \approx 4.71$
  \item Protection leg $P \approx 0.0245$
  \item Fair spread $= 0.0245 / 4.71 = 0.00520 = 52$ bps
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The protection buyer would pay 52 basis points per annum on
the notional to hedge severe flood risk at this gauge.

\section{Trade Structure}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Payer vs.\ Receiver}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item[Payer (protection buyer)] Pays the periodic spread.  Profits
        when flood risk increases (spread widens).  Long risk.
  \item[Receiver (protection seller)] Receives the spread.  Profits
        when flood risk decreases (spread tightens).  Short risk.
\end{description}

\subsection{Key Terms}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llp{7cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Typical Value} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
Notional      & \pounds10M  & Face value; maximum payout \\
Tenor         & 1, 2, 3, or 5Y & Contract length \\
Trigger Level & severe     & Water level threshold (alert/warning/severe) \\
Trade Spread  & 45 bps     & Premium agreed at inception \\
Day Count     & ACT/360    & ISDA convention \\
Payment Freq  & 6M         & Semi-annual premium payments \\
Recovery      & 0\%        & Full loss on flood event \\
Currency      & GBP        & Denomination \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Trigger Levels}

Each gauge has three statutory flood levels (metres above ordnance datum):

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item[Flood Alert] Early warning --- be prepared. Lowest threshold.
  \item[Flood Warning] Flooding expected --- take action.
  \item[Severe Flood Warning] Severe flooding --- danger to life. Highest threshold.
\end{description}

\noindent The trigger level determines which hazard rate drives the
spread.  Severe triggers have lower probabilities (rarer events) and
therefore lower spreads.

\subsection{Yield Curve}

The risk-free yield curve used for discounting is a term structure of
continuously compounded rates.  The default curve is:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rr}
\toprule
\textbf{Tenor} & \textbf{Rate} \\
\midrule
1Y & 3.5\% \\
2Y & 4.0\% \\
3Y & 4.3\% \\
4Y & 4.5\% \\
5Y & 4.0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Rates between tenors are linearly interpolated.  Adjusting the
yield curve changes discount factors and therefore the risky annuity and
fair spread.  A parallel 50\,bp shift in rates typically moves the fair
spread by 1--3\,bps.

\section{Greeks \& Sensitivity}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{FS01 --- Flood Sensitivity}

FS01 is the P\&L change from a 1 basis point increase in the annual
hazard rate.  It is computed by bumping:

\[
  \text{FS01} = \frac{\Delta s}{10{,}000} \times A \times N
\]

\noindent where $\Delta s = s(p + 0.01\%) - s(p)$ is the spread change
in bps from a 1\,bp hazard rate bump, $A$ is the risky annuity, and
$N$ is the notional.

\textbf{Example:} If $\Delta s = 0.75$ bps and $A = 4.71$ on \pounds10M:

\[
  \text{FS01} = \frac{0.75}{10{,}000} \times 4.71 \times 10{,}000{,}000
             = \pounds3{,}532
\]

\noindent Interpretation: if the gauge's annual severe flood probability
increases by 1\,bp (e.g.\ 5.00\% to 5.01\%), a payer's position gains
\pounds3,532 and a receiver's position loses the same.

\subsection{Risky Annuity as DV01}

The risky annuity $A$ also serves as the spread DV01.  A 1\,bp change in
the \emph{traded spread} (not hazard rate) produces a P\&L of:

\[
  \text{DV01} = \frac{1}{10{,}000} \times A \times N
\]

\noindent For $A = 4.71$ on \pounds10M: DV01 $=$ \pounds4,710 per bp.

\subsection{Direction Convention}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
& \textbf{Spread widens} & \textbf{Spread tightens} \\
\midrule
Payer (buyer)   & Gains  & Loses \\
Receiver (seller) & Loses & Gains \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Mark-to-Market}
% =========================================================================

The mark-to-market (MTM) of an open trade is:

\[
  \text{MTM} = \frac{s_{\mathrm{fair}} - s_{\mathrm{trade}}}{10{,}000}
               \times A \times N \times d
\]

\noindent where $d = +1$ for a payer and $d = -1$ for a receiver.

\subsection{Worked Example: Day 1 to Day 2}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
& \textbf{Day 1} & \textbf{Day 2} \\
\midrule
Annual hazard rate & 5.00\% & 4.50\% \\
Fair spread        & 52 bps & 48 bps \\
Trade spread       & 45 bps & 45 bps \\
Risky annuity      & 4.71   & 4.71 \\
\midrule
MTM (payer, \pounds10M) & \pounds32,970 & \pounds14,130 \\
\midrule
\textbf{Daily P\&L} & --- & $-$\pounds18,840 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The payer's position lost \pounds18,840 because the hazard rate
tightened (flood risk decreased), narrowing the fair spread towards the
trade spread.
