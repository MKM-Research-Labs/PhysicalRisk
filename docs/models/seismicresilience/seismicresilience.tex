% 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
% ===================================================================
\mkmexecsummary

The Building Seismic-Resilience Credit Model (\texttt{MKM-SEIS-001}) prices the
seismic resilience of a commercial asset so that seismic loss aggregates into
the same resilience-credit currency as the existing 10{,}000-storm Monte Carlo
engine and the fire model (\texttt{MKM-FIRE-001}). The credit is consumed by the
Commercial PRS Pricer as an independent \emph{seismic} leg, combined with the
flood, wind and fire legs through a root-sum-of-squares all-in coupon. The model
is built from four governance-separated component models:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Model A --- Occurrence.} A Poisson occurrence model with
        per-asset annual rate $\lambda_i = \lambda_{\text{zone}}\, m_{\text{fault}}\,
        m_{\text{soil}}$. A spatial sub-layer draws the rupture location along the
        catchment fault-trace polyline --- structured identically to the
        river-centreline geometry used in the storm module --- and computes the
        Joyner-Boore source-to-site distance $R_{JB}$.
  \item \textbf{Model B --- Ground Motion Intensity.} A Ground Motion Prediction
        Equation (GMPE) maps the sampled $(M, R_{JB})$ pair to a site peak ground
        acceleration (PGA), adjusted for local soil amplification through
        $V_{S30}$.
  \item \textbf{Model C --- Seismic Response Effectiveness.} A deterministic
        mapping from the asset's BRI geoseismic measures (GS01--GS18) to the
        fragility median multipliers, the soil-amplification gate, the
        post-earthquake-fire cascade probability and the recovery acceleration
        factor consumed by Model D.
  \item \textbf{Model D --- Damage State, Loss \& Resilience Credit.} A
        single-step lognormal fragility draw assigns one of four damage states
        (DS0--DS3). Aggregated over $n_{\text{sim}}$ draws the model emits annual
        expected loss, the no-collapse rate, and the full-collapse (DS3)
        frequency in basis points --- the seismic leg the PRS pricer consumes.
\end{itemize}

The \emph{point-of-no-return analogue} is DS3 (complete damage / collapse): once
this state is assigned, loss is 100\% and recovery requires demolition and
rebuild. The DS3 frequency, $n_{\text{DS3}}/n_{\text{sim}} \times 1.0 \times
10{,}000$, is the bps the PRS pricer charges for the seismic leg.

The model is designed to be \emph{sensitive to all three primary axes}:
\textbf{distance} (the source-to-site $R_{JB}$ governs GMPE attenuation),
\textbf{intensity} (the hazard zone governs occurrence frequency and the
magnitude distribution) and \textbf{resilience} (the BRI geoseismic measures
shift the fragility medians). Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} demonstrates each.

\textbf{Status:} Development (Tier 2, RAG Amber). All four component models, the
end-to-end orchestrator, the port stage, the PRS-pricer and Loan-Calculator
integration and the sensitivity harness are delivered with unit tests; the six
Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} acceptance gates all pass. Every numeric parameter
is a first-pass engineering-judgement seed (\texttt{config/seismicmatrices.json}
and \texttt{config/seismic\_zones.json}, \texttt{version "seed-0"}); the results
in this document are therefore \emph{directional, not calibrated}.

\textbf{Key risk:} The model uses placeholder calibration throughout. It is not
approved for production until historical calibration completes and the Model Risk
Committee (MRC) issues sign-off (remediation items SEIS-R1--R5).

% ===================================================================
% 2. MODEL PURPOSE AND SCOPE
% ===================================================================
\mkmpurpose

\subsection{Purpose}
To assign each commercial asset a seismic-resilience credit --- an annual
unconditional loss frequency, a conditional no-collapse rate and a full-collapse
(DS3) frequency in bps --- priced pari-passu with the flood, wind and fire
hazards. The credit rewards genuine, evidenced seismic resilience (seismic
foundation design, lateral force resistance, base isolation, independent audit)
and penalises the configurations that historically drive total losses, most
notably unreinforced masonry or pre-code concrete frames in high-hazard zones
without base isolation or lateral bracing.

\subsection{Scope}
The model covers commercial assets described by the v2 commercial-asset and
resilience sections of the Common Data Model (CDM). It reads only the derived
\texttt{AssetSeismicFeatures} bundle --- never the raw CDM record --- so the
feature contract is explicit, testable and stable against CDM schema churn. The
in-scope drivers are commercial type, construction type, number of storeys, soil
type, seismic hazard zone, the eighteen BRI geoseismic measures (GS01--GS18) and
fault proximity. The catchment fault trace is a companion spatial file
(\texttt{data/catch/<name>/fault\_trace.json}) structured identically to the
river polyline used in the storm module.

\subsection{Out of Scope}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Finite-element or nonlinear time-history structural analysis.
  \item Casualty, life-safety or evacuation modelling.
  \item Aftershock sequences (Poisson mainshock occurrence only --- SEIS-L2).
  \item Liquefaction or landslide as independent primary hazards.
  \item Business-interruption quantum (the DS3 leg is fixed at 100\% loss).
  \item Explicit floor-level geometry (height enters only through
        \texttt{NumberOfStoreys}).
\end{itemize}

% 3. MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK
% ===================================================================
\mkmframework

\subsection{Occurrence (Model A)}
Earthquake occurrence is a homogeneous Poisson process. The per-asset annual
rate is
\[
  \lambda_i = \lambda_{\text{zone}}\; m_{\text{fault}}\; m_{\text{soil}},
\]
where $\lambda_{\text{zone}}$ is the mean annual rate of events $M \ge m_{\min}$
at the asset's seismic hazard zone (Appendix~\ref{app:occ}), $m_{\text{fault}} =
1.0 + \delta_{\text{GS01}}\,\mathbf{1}[\text{FaultProximityKm} < 1.0 \text{ and
GS01 absent}]$ with $\delta_{\text{GS01}} = 0.15$, and $m_{\text{soil}}$ is the
soil recurrence modifier. Over a run horizon $h$, $N \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_i
h)$ is sampled per draw; an earthquake instantiates when $N \ge 1$. The magnitude
is drawn from the truncated Gutenberg-Richter law
\[
  P(M > m) = \frac{10^{-b m} - 10^{-b\, m_{\max}}}
                  {10^{-b\, m_{\min}} - 10^{-b\, m_{\max}}},
\]
with $b = 1.0$, $m_{\min} = 5.0$, $m_{\max} = 7.5$ by default (zone-configurable).

\subsection{Fault Geometry and Spatial Draw}
For each instantiated earthquake the rupture location is sampled from the
catchment fault-trace polyline. Cumulative arc-length $S(k)$ is computed at each
vertex with the Haversine formula; a uniform draw $u$ selects $s^\star = u\,
S(n)$ and the rupture centroid is linearly interpolated. The surface rupture
length follows the Wells-Coppersmith scaling $\log_{10} L_r = -2.44 + 0.59\,M$,
and the rupture occupies $[s^\star - L_r/2,\, s^\star + L_r/2]$ clipped to the
fault endpoints. The Joyner-Boore distance $R_{JB}$ is the minimum distance from
the asset to the rupture segment --- the same flat-Earth polyline primitive the
flood module uses for river-bank distance, so no new GIS dependency is
introduced.

\subsection{Ground Motion Intensity (Model B)}
The site PGA is sampled from a GMPE in lognormal form,
\[
  \ln(\text{PGA}) = \ln\overline{\text{PGA}}(M, R_{JB}, V_{S30}) + \sigma_{\text{tot}}\,
  \varepsilon, \qquad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1),
\]
with $\sigma_{\text{tot}} = 0.65$ by default. Soil amplification enters
implicitly through $V_{S30}$ (mapped from \texttt{SoilType} or read directly from
\texttt{SoilVs30Mps}). When GS02 is absent and the site class is D or E an
additional factor $F_a = 1.30$ multiplies the GMPE median before sampling. The
seed GMPE is a parametric Boore-Atkinson-style form per tectonic regime,
calibrated to reproduce the indicative attenuation of
Table~\ref{tab:seis_distance}.

\subsection{Seismic Response Effectiveness (Model C)}
Model C is deterministic. The fragility median multiplier per damage state is the
product of upward shifts for each present measure,
\[
  \theta^{\text{adj}}_{i} = \theta^{\text{base}}_{i}\; \theta^{\text{mult}}_{i},
  \qquad
  \theta^{\text{mult}}_{i} = \prod_{j} \bigl(1 + \delta_j\,
  \mathbf{1}[\text{measure } j \text{ present}]\bigr),
\]
with the modifiers $\delta_j$ of Appendix~\ref{app:resp}. The governance measures
GS16--GS18 contribute a single combined factor capped at $+0.05$. Model C also
emits the recovery acceleration $r_{\text{acc}}$ (from the BRI geoseismic rating,
with a GS18 uplift) and the post-earthquake-fire probability $p_{\text{PEF}}$
(reduced 80\% by GS15).

\subsection{Damage State, Loss and Resilience Credit (Model D)}
For each instantiated earthquake the lognormal fragility CDF is evaluated at the
sampled PGA for each damage-state threshold,
\[
  P(\text{DS} \ge \text{DS}_i \mid \text{PGA}) =
  \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln(\text{PGA}) - \ln(\theta^{\text{adj}}_{i})}{\beta}\right),
  \qquad \beta = 0.60,
\]
and the highest $\text{DS}_i$ with $P(\text{DS} \ge \text{DS}_i) > U$ is assigned
($U \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$). A loss draw conditioned on the damage state gives
the structural loss ratio (DS3 fixed at $1.0$). If $\text{DS} \ge \text{DS2}$ a
post-earthquake-fire cascade may fire ($V < p_{\text{PEF}}$); the fire model is
invoked with \texttt{InitiationClass = ExternalExposure} and the scenario loss
becomes $\max(L_{\text{seismic}}, L_{\text{fire}})$. A functionality-recovery
curve $Q(t)$ ramps from $Q(t_0)$ to $1.0$ over $T_{\text{rec}}/r_{\text{acc}}$.

Aggregating over $n_{\text{sim}}$ draws (of which $n_{\text{events}}$ instantiate
earthquakes),
\[
  \text{loss frequency} = \frac{\sum_s L_s}{n_{\text{sim}}}, \quad
  \text{no-collapse rate} = \frac{n_{\text{DS0}}+n_{\text{DS1}}+n_{\text{DS2}}}
                                  {n_{\text{events}}}, \quad
  \text{seismic spread (bps)} = \frac{n_{\text{DS3}}}{n_{\text{sim}}}\times 10{,}000.
\]
The seismic spread is combined with the flood, wind and fire legs by
root-sum-of-squares: $\text{all-in} = \sqrt{\sum_{\text{perils}} \text{leg}^2}$.

% 4. INPUT PARAMETERS AND CALIBRATION
% ===================================================================
\mkminputs

All numeric seeds live in \texttt{config/seismicmatrices.json} and
\texttt{config/seismic\_zones.json} (\texttt{version "seed-0"}) and are loaded by
\texttt{config/seismic}; no number is embedded in the model code. The
resilience-measure vocabulary (GS01--GS18) is single-sourced from the CDM via the
model's CDM adapter. \emph{Calibration status:} every value is a first-pass
engineering-judgement seed chosen for plausible direction and ordering, not
magnitude. Statistical calibration against historical commercial seismic-loss
data (SEIS-R1) and independent GMPE benchmarking (SEIS-R2) are open remediation
items; until they close the outputs must be read as relative resilience signals,
not absolute loss probabilities. The full parameter set is reproduced in the
Appendices.

% 5. IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS

\mkmimplementation

\subsection{Module Layout}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{config/seismic/} --- parameter schema (enums, dataclasses,
        loader); \texttt{seismicmatrices.json} and \texttt{seismic\_zones.json}
        --- seed numeric parameters.
  \item \texttt{src/models/seismic/datastructures/} --- runtime value types.
  \item \texttt{src/models/seismic/occurrence/} --- Model A (Poisson draw,
        magnitude sample, fault spatial draw, $R_{JB}$).
  \item \texttt{src/models/seismic/groundmotion.py} --- Model B (GMPE).
  \item \texttt{src/models/seismic/responseeffectiveness.py} --- Model C.
  \item \texttt{src/models/seismic/damage.py} --- Model D, plus the
        \texttt{simulate\_asset\_seismic} / \texttt{simulate\_portfolio\_seismic}
        orchestrators.
  \item \texttt{app/commands/port/stages/seismic.py} --- the port stage writing
        \texttt{seismic/seismic.json}.
  \item \texttt{src/routes/commercial/hazard/} --- the read-time join attaching
        \texttt{seismic\_spread\_bps} to the asset hazard payload.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Randomness}
All randomness flows through a single caller-owned
\texttt{numpy.random.Generator}, so an entire portfolio run is reproducible from
one seed --- matching the storm, typhoon and fire convention. Model A draws the
Poisson count, the magnitude and the rupture location; Model B draws one GMPE
residual per event; Model D draws the damage-state uniform, the loss sample and
the cascade gate. Model C is deterministic.

% 6. VALIDATION AND BACKTESTING
% ===================================================================
\mkmvalidation

Historical calibration and backtesting are open remediation items (SEIS-R1,
SEIS-R2) to be completed before production sign-off. Current validation is by
directional unit tests, which confirm the model behaves as designed: a BRI-AA
asset has materially lower DS3 frequency than a BRI-NR asset; base isolation
(GS12) produces the largest single-measure shift; low-hazard zones have
near-zero collapse frequency; the post-earthquake-fire cascade fires at the
expected conditional rate; and a whole-portfolio run is bit-for-bit reproducible
from a single seed.

\mkmsensitivity
\label{sec:sensitivity}

The tables below are generated by
\texttt{docs/models/sensitivities/seismicresilience} running the real
\texttt{simulate\_asset\_seismic} against the baseline of Section~7.1 and varying
one input at a time over 20{,}000 Monte Carlo draws per cell. The baseline asset
is a 4-storey Office in Fair condition, RC pre-code construction, stiff soil
(class B), Moderate seismic zone, located 20~km from the fault trace, with all
BRI geoseismic measures absent.

The model is required to exhibit monotonic and material sensitivity along each of
the three primary axes --- distance, intensity and resilience --- and to satisfy
five acceptance conditions: (i)~distance monotonicity (spread non-increasing in
$R_{JB}$); (ii)~intensity monotonicity (spread non-decreasing in zone);
(iii)~resilience monotonicity (no-collapse non-decreasing and spread
non-increasing from NR to AA); (iv)~interaction ordering
$(\text{Very high}, \text{NR}) > (\text{High}, \text{AA}) \ge (\text{Moderate},
\text{AA})$; and (v)~a near-zero floor ($< 1$~bps in the Very low zone at
$R_{JB} = 100$~km). All five gates pass against the current seeds.

% 8. MODEL LIMITATIONS AND KNOWN WEAKNESSES
% ===================================================================
\mkmlimitations

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No historical calibration (SEIS-L1).} Every parameter is a
        first-pass placeholder; outputs are relative signals, not absolute loss
        probabilities.
  \item \textbf{Mainshock-only Poisson (SEIS-L2).} The HPP assumption ignores
        aftershock clustering; appropriate for long-run annualised loss but not
        real-time post-event risk.
  \item \textbf{Single fault trace per catchment (SEIS-L3).} Multiple
        independent sources require a superposition of Poisson processes
        (planned, SEIS-R4).
  \item \textbf{Deterministic GMPE median (SEIS-L4).} Only the aleatory residual
        is sampled; epistemic logic-tree uncertainty is not represented.
  \item \textbf{Height proxy (SEIS-L5).} Building height enters only through
        \texttt{NumberOfStoreys}.
\end{itemize}

% 9. GOVERNANCE VALIDATION QUESTIONS

\mkmgovernance

\mkmvqitem{1}{Model purpose and use}{The model produces a seismic-resilience
credit (loss frequency, no-collapse rate and a full-collapse bps leg) consumed by
the Commercial PRS Pricer as an independent peril. Use is consistent with design;
it is not a life-safety or absolute-loss model and must not be used as one.}

\mkmvqitem{2}{Data quality and lineage}{Inputs are the CDM-derived
\texttt{AssetSeismicFeatures}; lineage runs from the v2 CDM resilience fields
(GS01--GS18) through Model C. Seismic zone and fault-trace files are
catchment-level spatial assets versioned alongside the model. Missing GS flags
default to the absence case. Calibration data quality is not yet established
(SEIS-R1).}

\mkmvqitem{3}{Conceptual soundness}{The four-model decomposition (Poisson
occurrence with a fault-geometry spatial draw, GMPE intensity, BRI measure
mapping, lognormal fragility with a loss draw) is the standard PSHA/PBEE
framework used in HAZUS, GEM and Eurocode 8 risk assessment. The Poisson
mainshock assumption is well supported for engineering annual-loss estimation.}

\mkmvqitem{4}{Calibration and parameters}{All parameters are
engineering-judgement seeds (\texttt{seed-0}) chosen for direction and ordering,
not magnitude. Statistical calibration is open (SEIS-R1, SEIS-R2); this is the
dominant model limitation.}

\mkmvqitem{5}{Implementation verification}{Verified by unit tests covering each
component model, the orchestrator and the cascade gate, plus a reproducibility
test. The sensitivity harness exercises the full path and passes the five
Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} acceptance gates.}

\mkmvqitem{6}{Sensitivity and stability}{See Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}. The
model exhibits monotonic and material sensitivity along distance, intensity and
resilience, with the designed intensity~$\times$~resilience non-linearity
(strong BRI measures most valuable in high-hazard zones). Stability is adequate
at $n_{\text{sim}} = 20{,}000$.}

\mkmvqitem{7}{Limitations and weaknesses}{Documented in Section~8 (SEIS-L1--L5).
The placeholder calibration and the single fault-trace restriction are the most
material.}

\mkmvqitem{8}{Ongoing monitoring}{Not yet in production. A monitoring plan
(input-distribution drift; backtest of realised vs predicted seismic-loss
frequency) is defined under SEIS-R3 before sign-off.}
