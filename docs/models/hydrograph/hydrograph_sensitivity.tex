% 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
% ===================================================================
\mkmexecsummary

This document presents the sensitivity analysis of the Hydrograph Superposition
Model (v2.2) implemented in \texttt{src/models/floodrisk/hydrograph.py}.  The
v2.2 model replaces the single-peak / single-shape hydrograph approach (v2.1)
with per-pulse gamma-shaped templates, antecedent saturation scaling, linear
superposition, and flow-path infiltration.

The analysis uses a synthetic property at varying distances and elevations from a
flooding gauge, exercised under both isolated (single storm) and doublet (two
storm) sequences.  Four sensitivity axes are examined:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Peak WSE at gauge} --- the primary driver of flood depth.
    \item \textbf{Distance from gauge} --- controls retention and travel time.
    \item \textbf{Elevation above gauge} --- controls the flood threshold.
    \item \textbf{Pulse duration} --- controls hydrograph breadth and flooded hours.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Key findings:}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Doublet sequences produce materially higher flood depths and longer
          durations than isolated storms at the same peak WSE, confirming that
          compound effects are captured by the v2.2 superposition.
    \item Distance sensitivity is flat within the 2\,km near-field (retention = 1.0),
          then degrades gradually --- consistent with the Book~210 near-field bypass.
    \item Elevation is the dominant control on whether a property floods at all;
          a 3\,m elevation difference eliminates flooding for events up to 10\,m WSE.
    \item Pulse duration primarily affects flooded hours rather than peak depth,
          which is governed by the gamma shape peak.
\end{itemize}

% 2. MODEL PURPOSE AND SCOPE
% ===================================================================
\mkmpurpose

\subsection{Purpose}
The Hydrograph Superposition Model (v2.2) constructs property-level flood
hydrographs from individual storm pulses within a 168-hour (7-day) event window.
It addresses a critical gap in v2.1 where multi-storm sequences (doublets,
clusters, persistent patterns) were reduced to their single worst pulse, losing
temporal compounding effects.

\subsection{Scope of This Analysis}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{In scope:} Sensitivity of peak depth, flood duration, arrival
          time, and damage ratio to gauge peak WSE, distance, elevation, pulse
          duration, and sequence type (isolated vs doublet).
    \item \textbf{Out of scope:} Cluster and persistent sequences (4+ pulses);
          calibration of gamma shape parameters; portfolio-level impact; PRS
          pricing sensitivity.
\end{itemize}

% 3. MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK
% ===================================================================
\mkmframework

\subsection{Gamma Shape Template}
Each pulse $i$ in a sequence of type $T$ uses a normalised gamma-like shape:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_T(u) = \frac{1}{\alpha^{\alpha}} \left(\frac{u}{\alpha}\right)^{\alpha}
    \exp\!\left(\alpha - \frac{u}{\alpha}\right), \quad u = \frac{h - t_i}{D_i} \in (0, 1]
    \label{eq:gamma_shape}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha = \alpha_T$ is the shape parameter for sequence type $T$, $t_i$ is
the pulse start hour, and $D_i$ is the pulse duration.  The shape peaks at
$u = \alpha^2$ with $\phi(\alpha^2) = 1$.

\subsection{Per-Pulse Gauge Hydrograph}
The water surface elevation at the gauge for pulse $i$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{WSE}_{g,i}(h) = B_g + \bigl(\tilde{P}_i - B_g\bigr)\,\phi_T\!\left(\frac{h - t_i}{D_i}\right)
\end{equation}
where $B_g$ is the gauge base level and $\tilde{P}_i$ is the saturation-adjusted
peak for pulse $i$.

\subsection{Antecedent Saturation}
Later pulses are amplified by prior precipitation:
\begin{equation}
    s_i = 1 + \beta \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{A_i}{P_0}\right), \qquad
    \tilde{P}_i = B_g + (P_i - B_g)\,s_i
\end{equation}
where $A_i = \sum_{j < i} \mathrm{precip}_j$ (mm), $\beta = 0.2$, $P_0 = 50$\,mm.

\subsection{Superposition}
Pulses are linearly superimposed above the base level:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{WSE}_g^{(s)}(h) = B_g + \sum_{i} \bigl(\mathrm{WSE}_{g,i}(h) - B_g\bigr)
\end{equation}
with an optional cap at $C = 2.5 \times (\text{severe threshold} - B_g)$.

\subsection{Property Propagation}
The gauge hydrograph is time-shifted by Manning's travel time $\bar{\tau}$ and the
flood depth at the property is:
\begin{equation}
    d_p(t) = \max\!\left(0,\; \bigl[\mathrm{WSE}_g(t - \bar{\tau}) - z_g\bigr] \cdot r
    - (\Delta z + z_{\mathrm{floor}})\right)
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the distance retention factor, $\Delta z = z_p - z_g$ is the
elevation difference, and $z_{\mathrm{floor}}$ is the property floor step.

\subsection{Flow-Path Infiltration}
A cumulative infiltration bucket reduces flood depth in early hours:
\begin{equation}
    \ell_k = \min\!\bigl(\kappa \cdot d_p^{\mathrm{raw}}(t_k),\; Y_{\max} - Y_{\mathrm{inf}}(t_{k-1})\bigr),
    \qquad
    d_p(t_k) = d_p^{\mathrm{raw}}(t_k) - \ell_k
\end{equation}
where $Y_{\max} = (1 - f_{\mathrm{imperv}}) \times 0.10$\,m and
$\kappa = 0.005$\,hr$^{-1}$.  Once $Y_{\mathrm{inf}} = Y_{\max}$ the ground is
saturated and all subsequent water passes through unchanged.

\subsection{Interpretation}

\subsubsection{Peak WSE}
Flood depth scales approximately linearly with peak WSE above the flood threshold.
The doublet consistently produces deeper flooding than the isolated storm at the
same nominal peak because: (a) antecedent saturation from Pulse~1 amplifies Pulse~2's
effective peak by $s_2 = 1 + 0.2\ln(1 + 60/50) \approx 1.16$ (16\% boost), and
(b) if any temporal overlap exists, superposition raises the combined level above
either individual pulse.

\subsubsection{Distance}
Within the 2\,km near-field, retention is 1.0 and distance has no effect on depth
--- only travel time increases.  Beyond 2\,km, exponential decay reduces the
effective WSE: at 5\,km, retention is 0.61, reducing depth by approximately 40\%.
This behaviour is consistent with Book~210's near-field bypass recommendation.

\subsubsection{Elevation}
Elevation above the gauge is the single strongest control.  A 0.5\,m elevation
difference admits deep flooding (depth $>$3\,m at 10\,m WSE); a 5\,m difference
eliminates flooding entirely.  This confirms that the elevation threshold
dominates the property-level flood decision, as intended by the v2.1/v2.2 design.

\subsubsection{Pulse Duration}
Longer pulses increase flooded hours substantially (from $\sim$5\,hrs at $D=12$h
to $\sim$50\,hrs at $D=120$h for isolated storms) but have almost no impact on
peak depth or damage ratio.  Table~\ref{tab:sens_hydro_dur_iso} shows depth locked
at 3.26\,m and damage at 77.6\% across all durations.  This is a direct consequence
of two model properties: (a)~the gamma shape is normalised to $\max\phi = 1$
regardless of duration, so the peak WSE always reaches the full pulse peak; and
(b)~the depth-damage curve (\texttt{scalar\_depth\_damage}) uses only peak depth,
not flood duration.

\textbf{This is a known limitation.}  In reality, a property submerged for 50
hours suffers materially more structural damage than one submerged for 5 hours at
the same depth --- sustained saturation degrades building fabric, electrical
systems, and contents progressively.  The current depth-only damage function does
not capture this.  A duration-weighted damage adjustment is identified as a
priority enhancement (see~\S\ref{sec:iterations}).

The doublet amplifies the duration effect: longer pulses create wider recession
tails that overlap with the second pulse, further extending flood duration.

\subsubsection{Isolated vs Doublet Comparison}
Table~\ref{tab:sens_hydro_iso_vs_dbl} directly compares the two sequence types.
Key observations:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item At moderate peaks (7--9\,m), the doublet produces 0.6--0.9\,m additional
          depth versus the isolated storm.
    \item At high peaks ($\geq$10\,m), the depth differential increases to
          $>$1\,m.
    \item Flood duration is consistently 60--100\% longer for doublets.
    \item The doublet can produce flooding at peaks where the isolated storm does
          not (e.g.\ at 6\,m WSE: isolated $=$ 0\,m, doublet $=$ 0\,m; but at
          7\,m the doublet floods where both do).
\end{itemize}

\noindent These results validate that v2.2 superposition materially changes the
flood risk profile for compound events, which represent approximately 60\% of
severe+ sequences in the Thames catchment.

% ===================================================================
% 6. LIMITATIONS
% ===================================================================
\mkmlimitations

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Depth-damage curve is peak-only.}  The current
          \texttt{scalar\_depth\_damage()} function maps peak depth to a damage
          ratio with no duration component.  As Table~\ref{tab:sens_hydro_dur_iso}
          demonstrates, this makes the damage ratio entirely insensitive to pulse
          duration (77.6\% at both 12\,h and 120\,h), which is not physically
          realistic.  Sustained inundation causes progressive degradation of
          building fabric, services, and contents beyond what peak depth alone
          captures.  This is the most material limitation identified by this
          analysis.
    \item \textbf{One-at-a-time sensitivity only.}  This analysis varies each axis
          independently while holding others at baseline.  This is a standard
          first-stage approach but does not capture parameter interactions.  An
          external validator would expect a global sensitivity analysis (e.g.\
          Sobol indices or Morris screening) to quantify how much variance in
          depth, duration, and damage is attributable to each parameter group and
          their interactions.
    \item \textbf{Gamma shape parameters are illustrative.}  The $\alpha$ values
          (0.3, 0.7, 1.0) are taken from the Book~210 recommendation and have not
          been calibrated to observed Thames hydrographs.  A $\pm$0.1 shift in
          $\alpha$ would change peak timing and breadth but not the peak WSE itself.
    \item \textbf{Saturation parameters are defaults.}  $\beta = 0.2$ and
          $P_0 = 50$\,mm are reasonable but uncalibrated.  Higher $\beta$ would
          amplify the doublet advantage further.
    \item \textbf{Infiltration is a screening model.}  The constant-rate bucket
          ($\kappa = 0.005$, $Y_{\max} = 0.06$\,m for urban) provides only a small
          reduction.  A Green--Ampt or Philip model would be more physically
          grounded for permeable catchments.
    \item \textbf{Only isolated and doublet tested.}  Cluster (3--4 pulses) and
          persistent (4--5 pulses) sequences would show even larger compound
          effects.
    \item \textbf{Fixed inter-pulse gap.}  The doublet gap is set at
          $\max(12, 0.5D)$ hours.  Shorter gaps would increase superposition;
          longer gaps would reduce it.
    \item \textbf{No tidal interaction.}  Thames tidal lock-out effects are not
          modelled in the recession limb.
    \item \textbf{No observed-event validation.}  All scenarios are synthetic.
          Validation against observed compound flood events with known hydrographs
          at gauged sites is required before production use.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Planned Iterations}
\label{sec:iterations}

The following enhancements are identified based on this sensitivity analysis and
external review feedback.

\subsection{Iteration 1: Duration-Weighted Damage (Q2 2026)}

The most material gap.  Extend the depth-damage function to include a duration
multiplier:

\begin{equation}
    D_{\mathrm{adj}} = D_{\mathrm{peak}} \times \left(1 + \gamma \,\ln\!\left(1 + \frac{T_{\mathrm{flood}}}{T_0}\right)\right)
\end{equation}

\noindent where $D_{\mathrm{peak}}$ is the current peak-depth damage ratio,
$T_{\mathrm{flood}}$ is the number of hours above threshold, $T_0$ is a reference
duration (e.g.\ 6\,h), and $\gamma$ is a calibration parameter controlling
duration sensitivity.  This would make Table~\ref{tab:sens_hydro_dur_iso} show
increasing damage with duration as physically expected.  Candidate calibration
sources: UK Multi-Coloured Manual damage curves which include a duration dimension.

\subsection{Iteration 2: Global Sensitivity Analysis (Q2--Q3 2026)}

Replace the one-at-a-time sweep with a variance-based global sensitivity study:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Method:} Morris screening to identify non-influential parameters,
          followed by Sobol first-order and total-effect indices for the material
          parameters.
    \item \textbf{Parameter space:} $\alpha$ (gamma shape), $\beta$ and $P_0$
          (saturation), $\kappa$ and $Y_{\max}$ (infiltration), retention length
          $L$, near-field threshold, inter-pulse gap, and the new duration-damage
          $\gamma$.
    \item \textbf{Outputs:} Variance decomposition of peak depth, flooded hours,
          and damage ratio.  Ranking of parameter importance.
    \item \textbf{Tool:} SALib (Python) integrated into the sensitivity generator
          framework.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Iteration 3: Observed-Event Validation (Q3 2026)}

Replay well-documented Thames flood events through the v2.2 model and compare
modelled vs observed depth and duration at properties with known outcomes:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Event selection:} 2--3 events with clear compound behaviour
          (multi-peak hydrographs) and available gauge data.
    \item \textbf{Calibration:} Fit $\alpha$, $\beta$, $P_0$ to observed
          hydrograph shapes and peak amplification.
    \item \textbf{Cross-check:} Compare distance-retention curve against
          Muskingum--Cunge routing calibrated to the same reach.
    \item \textbf{Deliverable:} Updated sensitivity tables with calibrated
          parameters; model validation report for MRC review.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Per MRC action A-001, independent validation of Tier~1 models is
targeted for Q3~2026.  Iterations 1--3 are designed to support that milestone.
