\mkmexecsummary
% =========================================================================

This document benchmarks the Storm Sequence Generator (MKM-SS-001): the generators in
\texttt{src/port/src/storm\_multi/}, which draw the 10{,}000 multi-storm sequences the
stress pipeline turns into hydrographs and trains its flood classifiers on. It asks two
questions. Does the code compute what its model document (\texttt{storm\_multi\_v3.1.pdf},
v3.1, 9 November 2026) says? And does what the document says hold?

\paragraph{The code computes its documented equations.} A reference written from the
document's text replays \bmReplayN{} generated sequences on the code's own random stream.
Every sequence type and storm count matches, and every duration, gap, start time,
intensity and aggregate agrees to \bmCodeErr. In bulk, the code's sequence types, storm
counts and capped sequences follow the exact law the document's tables imply, within
sampling error (Table~\ref{tab:bm-checks}). \bmWolframConfirms{}

\paragraph{The document's claims hold:} \bmClaimsMet{} of \bmClaims{}
(Table~\ref{tab:bm-claims}).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{The recalibrated tables are the code's.} Table~1's storm counts (a cluster
  has three, a persistent sequence four) and Table~3's durations, a third of v3.0's, are
  what \texttt{config.port} holds, and the code draws from them.
\item \textbf{The storms fit the window.} The cap binds on \bmCapPct{} of duration draws
  (v3.0: 60.5\%). Gaps are shortened in \bmShortPct{} of multi-storm sequences. Every storm
  is capped in \bmPinnedPct{}. Both of Q6's thresholds hold at the baseline, as v3.1 says.
\item \textbf{Every gap is at least its minimum,} and Table~9's counts (\bmIsolated{}
  isolated and so on) are the exact expectation.
\item \textbf{The correlation is as stated:} a later storm is at least as strong as the
  first with probability \bmSubsequent.
\item \textbf{The override reaches the generator.} \texttt{storm\_control.json} changes the
  sequence probability and the duration table whether it is applied before or after the
  generator is imported.
\item \textbf{The paths, command line, outputs and modules} are those in the repository,
  and \textbf{the classifier figures} are those of the classifiers retrained on v3.1's
  catalogue.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the benchmark does not settle.} v3.1's tables are calibrated to the
window, not to observed storms, and the benchmark cannot say whether they are realistic.
Each sequence's \texttt{sequence\_start}, which the stress pipeline reads as the season, is
the time it was generated (the default batch here: \bmSeasonMonths{} distinct month). The
document records both as limitations; neither is a claim this benchmark could pass or
fail.

\paragraph{Evidence.} \bmChecks{} checks, outcome: \bmOutcome{}
(Table~\ref{tab:bm-checks}).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Wolfram: \bmWolfram{}\bmWolframVia.
\item Code under test, at commit \bmCodeCommit{}\bmCodeDirty.
\item Numerics: \bmNumerics.
\item Generated \bmGenerated{} by \texttt{python -m tools.wolfram\_oracle ss001}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is benchmarked}
The generation path the document describes (\S5.1--\S5.2), loaded as its own modules:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \texttt{generators/batch\_generator.py}: \texttt{generate\_event\_set}, the category and
  base-intensity draws;
\item \texttt{generators/sequence\_generator.py}: type, count, the cap with its reserved gap
  minima, gap shortening, placement, intensities, precipitation and the aggregates;
\item the duration, gap and intensity samplers, and the tables in \texttt{config.port};
\item \texttt{utils/validation.py}.
\end{itemize}
Also tested: the repository the document cites, namely its paths, command line, outputs
and override control, and the classifier evidence.

\subsection{What is not}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item The package's \texttt{models/} subpackage (sequence response, spatial correlation,
  hydrology), which the document places outside the model.
\item Whether the sequence types, durations and gaps are realistic. The document cites no
  observed sequences, and none are tested.
\item The classifier itself (MKM-FC-001). Only the figures this document cites from its
  training summary are checked.
\end{itemize}
This is a benchmark of the implementation (validation question Q8), not independent
validation.

\subsection{Earlier issue}
Benchmark v0.1 (7 November 2026) tested v2.0 and found that 11 of its 17 claims did not
hold. It is kept as evidence of the check. v3.0 is the model and document that answered
it. Benchmark v0.2 (8 November 2026) tested v3.0 and found all 17 of its claims held,
including its own record that the cap bound on 60.5\% of draws, above its Q6 threshold.
v3.1 recalibrates Tables~1 and~3 to resolve that, and this issue tests v3.1.

\mkmframework
% =========================================================================

The reference implements the document's equations from its text, in two halves.

\paragraph{Exact.} In rational arithmetic:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item the type mix of Eq.~4 and Table~2, weighted by Table~6;
\item the storm counts of Table~1;
\item the chance that Eq.~6's cap, $(W_P - (n-1)\,g_{\min})/n$, binds;
\item the chance that every storm is capped, which holds every gap at its minimum;
\item the chance that Eq.~7 triggers Eq.~8's shortening;
\item the chance that a later storm is at least as strong as the first (Eqs.~12--13).
\end{itemize}
Durations and gaps are rounded to whole hours, as the document says.

\paragraph{Replay.} Eqs.~4--20 re-run on a \texttt{RandomState} seeded as the code's, drawing
in the code's order. Timings are exact rationals, so ``every storm capped'' is decided
without rounding; intensities are worked to 50 digits. The first storm's redraw (\S3.7) is
mirrored so the two streams stay in step.

\section{Benchmark Method}
% =========================================================================

\paragraph{The cases.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item The default batch: 10{,}000 sequences, seed 42, Table~6's weights, the Thames.
\item Every category with every forced type: 400 sequences each, 9{,}600 in all.
\item 100 seeds of 10{,}000 for the type mix (\S7.2).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The checks.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item The replay compares each sequence number for number.
\item The Monte Carlo compares the code's shares with the exact ones, in standard errors.
\item The validator runs on every sequence generated, and on a sequence broken five ways.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The statements.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Figures (Table~9, \S6.2, \S6.3, \S7.2, Table~8) and Q6's baseline rates are judged
  against the exact law, to the precision the document states them.
\item Every gap is judged against its type's minimum, over all \bmReplayN{} sequences.
\item Q4's override is judged by applying a \texttt{storm\_control.json} that switches
  sequences off and shortens every storm. This runs in a separate interpreter, once with
  the generator imported before the file is applied and once after.
\item Paths, command-line flags and files are judged by searching the repository.
\item The classifier figures are judged against the training summary in the data store.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Wolfram.} Wolfram works from its own \texttt{TriangularDistribution} and
\texttt{UniformDistribution}, independently of the reference:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item the type mix;
\item the cap's chance of binding in each configuration, as a single tail of the
  triangular law (the reference sums the rounded law);
\item the chance that every storm is capped, and of shortening, by convolving the rounded
  laws;
\item the correlation, by \texttt{Probability} over Eq.~13's mixture.
\end{itemize}
