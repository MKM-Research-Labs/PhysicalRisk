% 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
% ===================================================================
\mkmexecsummary

The Building Fire-Resilience Credit Model (\texttt{MKM-FIRE-001}) prices the
fire resilience of a commercial asset so that fire loss aggregates into the same
resilience-credit currency as the existing 10{,}000-storm Monte Carlo engine.
The credit is consumed by the Commercial PRS Pricer as an independent
\emph{full-conflagration} leg, combined with the flood and wind legs through a
root-sum-of-squares all-in coupon. The model is built from four
governance-separated component models:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Model A --- Initiation.} A Poisson ignition frequency
        $\lambda_i$ per asset, composed from a base annual rate by commercial
        type and multiplicative modifiers for occupancy, process load,
        condition, protection and fire history.
  \item \textbf{Model B --- Intensity Track.} A 15-minute latent intensity
        $I_t$ whose growth is damped by passive (structural) defence, scaled by
        the structural frame's combustibility, cut once active suppression bites,
        and capped at a combustibility-implied fuel ceiling.
  \item \textbf{Model C --- Response Effectiveness.} A deterministic mapping
        from the asset's CDM resilience levels --- and its structural-frame
        \texttt{ConstructionType} --- to the dynamic quantities Models B and D
        consume (detection time, suppression-bite time, growth rate, critical
        threshold, controllability, the fire-service-reach gate, and the
        combustibility that drives the growth rate, the fuel ceiling and the
        passive-containment credit).
  \item \textbf{Model D --- Containment, Loss \& Resilience Credit.} An 8-state
        discrete-time Bayesian chain marched in 15-minute steps to an absorbing
        terminal state, aggregated into the credit currency.
\end{itemize}

The central concept is the \emph{point of no return} (PNR): the first step at
which latent intensity $I_t$ exceeds the critical threshold $I_{\text{crit}}$
\emph{before} suppression has bitten --- or, equivalently, the first step of any
fire in a building where suppression is structurally unreachable \emph{and} the
structural frame is combustible. Once PNR latches, the containment chain switches
to an absorbing matrix whose Contained column is zero: the fire can no longer be
contained and absorbs into \{partial loss, total loss\}. The PNR frequency,
$n_{\text{PNR}}/n_{\text{sim}}$, multiplied by a 100\% loss-given-event, is the
bps the PRS pricer charges for the fire leg.

Building \emph{material} is a first-class driver: the structural frame's
combustibility scales how fast the fire grows (a non-combustible frame buys
suppression time), caps the latent intensity below the critical threshold (so a
non-combustible frame cannot run to a whole-building conflagration even above the
fire-service reach --- only a small jittered residual remains), and credits the
containment chain (a non-combustible structure contains the fire floor-by-floor
even when active suppression cannot reach it). This is what stops building height
alone from forcing a conflagration in a reinforced-concrete or steel tower.

\textbf{Status:} Development (Tier 2, RAG Amber). All four component models and
the end-to-end orchestrator are delivered with unit tests, including the
fire-service-reach interaction. Every numeric parameter is a first-pass
engineering-judgement seed (\texttt{config/fire\_matrices.json},
\texttt{version "seed-0"}); the results in this document are therefore
\emph{directional, not calibrated}.

\textbf{Key risk:} The model uses placeholder calibration throughout. It is not
approved for production until historical calibration completes and the Model
Risk Committee (MRC) issues sign-off (remediation items FIRE-R1--R7).

% ===================================================================
% 2. MODEL PURPOSE AND SCOPE
% ===================================================================
\mkmpurpose

\subsection{Purpose}

To assign each commercial asset a fire-resilience credit --- expressed as an
annual unconditional loss frequency, a conditional containment rate, and a
full-conflagration (PNR) frequency --- that can be priced pari-passu with the
flood and wind hazards already in the portfolio engine. The credit rewards
genuine, evidenced fire resilience (detection, suppression, compartmentation,
tested procedures) and penalises the configurations that historically drive
total losses, most notably tall buildings without adequate internal
suppression.

\subsection{Scope}

The model covers commercial assets described by the v2 commercial-asset and
resilience sections of the Common Data Model (CDM). It reads only the derived
\texttt{AssetFireFeatures} bundle --- never the raw CDM record --- so that the
feature contract is explicit, testable and stable against CDM schema churn. The
in-scope drivers are: commercial type, occupancy status, business-rates
category, property condition, the nine resilience-level fields (automatic
detection, suppression systems, emergency procedures, structural fire
resistance, compartments, fire stopping, external materials, access route,
business continuity), the structural-frame \texttt{ConstructionType},
fire-damage history, and number of storeys. The model
produces a per-asset resilience credit and a portfolio roll-up; both are
reproducible from a single random seed.

\subsection{Out of Scope}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Physical (CFD) fire-spread and thermal-plume modelling.
  \item Smoke / life-safety egress and casualty modelling.
  \item Wildland--urban interface and external conflagration spread.
  \item Business-interruption quantum (only the resilience credit is produced;
        the loss-given-event is fixed at 100\% for the conflagration leg).
  \item Explicit floor-level geometry --- building height enters only through
        \texttt{NumberOfStoreys}.
\end{itemize}

% 3. MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK
% ===================================================================
\mkmframework

\subsection{Initiation (Model A)}

The per-asset annual ignition rate is the base rate for the commercial type
scaled by five independent multipliers:
\[
\lambda_i = \lambda_{0,c}\; m_{\text{occ}}\; m_{\text{proc}}\; m_{\text{cond}}\;
            m_{\text{protection}}\; m_{\text{history}},
\]
where $\lambda_{0,c}$ is the base annual frequency by commercial type $c$;
$m_{\text{occ}}$ from occupancy status; $m_{\text{proc}}$ from the process load
implied by type (raised, never lowered, by a business-rates override such as
\emph{Restaurant}); $m_{\text{cond}}$ from property condition;
$m_{\text{protection}}$ from the mean resilience level across the protection
fields; and $m_{\text{history}}$ from the severity and recency of any prior
fire. Unknown CDM options fall back to a neutral multiplier of $1.0$, so a
partial record still yields a usable $\lambda_i$.

Over a run horizon of $h$ years the effective rate is
$\lambda_{\text{eff}} = \lambda_i\, h$. For each of $n_{\text{sim}}$ draws a
Poisson count $N \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_{\text{eff}})$ is sampled, and a
fire instantiates for that draw when $N \ge 1$ --- the direct analogue of
storm-event generation, where only a subset of draws produce an event. Each
instantiated fire is tagged with an \texttt{InitiationClass} drawn from the
per-type categorical prior (Appendix~\ref{app:priors}).

\subsection{Response Effectiveness (Model C)}

Each resilience level is mapped to an index $k \in \{0,\dots,4\}$ in the CDM
\texttt{RESILIENCE\_LEVELS} vocabulary and to a fraction
$f = k/4 \in [0,1]$. A field-group effectiveness is the mean over its fields of
the field's configured range linearly interpolated by $f$:
\[
e_{\text{group}} = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{j\in G}
   \big[\,\ell_j + f_j\,(u_j - \ell_j)\,\big],
\]
giving the aggregate passive effectiveness $e_{\text{passive}}$ (structural
resistance, compartments, fire stopping, external materials) and response
effectiveness $e_{\text{response}}$ (access route, procedures, continuity).

Detection time is $\tau_{\text{det}} = \tau_{\text{det,0}}\,\delta_{\ell}$ where
$\delta_\ell$ is the detection multiplier for the asset's detection level.
Suppression-bite time applies a gentle per-storey height penalty
$p \in [p_{\min},p_{\max}]$ interpolated over storeys \emph{and} a
suppression-level multiplier $b_{\text{supp}}$ (a better system engages sooner),
but is governed first by the \textbf{fire-service-reach gate}: external attack
reaches only up to $R$ storeys, and above $R$ effective suppression requires
internal sprinklers, i.e. a suppression level index $\ge \theta_{\text{int}}$.
Writing $s$ for the storey count and $k_{\text{supp}}$ for the suppression-level
index,
\[
\text{reachable} \;=\; \big(k_{\text{supp}} \ge \theta_{\text{int}}\big)
                       \;\lor\; \big(s \le R\big),
\]
\[
\tau_{\text{supp}} =
\begin{cases}
\tau_{\text{supp,0}}\; p \; b_{\text{supp}} & \text{if reachable},\\[2pt]
\tau_{\infty} \;(\gg \text{step budget}) & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Compartmentation quality
$q = \tfrac12\big(k_{\text{comp}}+k_{\text{stop}}\big)$ selects the
well- vs.\ poorly-compartmented growth rate; the suppression level selects both
the post-bite growth multiplier $\mu_{\text{supp}}$ and the critical intensity
$I_{\text{crit}}$, the latter graded across all five levels (not a binary
strong/weak step) so every suppression level shifts the controllability race.
Finally the controllability score is the weighted blend
\[
C = w_p\,e_{\text{passive}} + w_r\,e_{\text{response}}
    + w_s\,f_{\text{supp}},
\]
(renormalised by $w_p+w_r+w_s$), which is remapped to an active matrix-blend
weight $w_{\text{active}} = \operatorname{clamp}\big((C - C_{\text{floor}})/
(C_{\text{ceil}} - C_{\text{floor}}),0,1\big)$. The blend weight that actually
mixes the pre-PNR matrices (Model~D) takes the larger of the active weight and a
\emph{structural-containment} weight $w_{\text{struct}} = \operatorname{clamp}
(\kappa\,(1-\gamma),0,1)$ driven by the frame combustibility $\gamma$ (below):
\[
w \;=\; \max\!\big(w_{\text{active}},\, w_{\text{struct}}\big) \in [0,1].
\]
A fire is controllable if it can be \emph{either} actively suppressed
\emph{or} passively contained by a non-combustible structure, so a
reinforced-concrete frame ($\gamma\!\to\!0$) blends near the controllable matrix
even when suppression cannot reach it. A continuous blend, rather than a hard
threshold, is what lets the well-graded score $C$ actually grade containment: a
hard switch saturates because passive and response defences alone already clear
it, leaving suppression unable to move the matrix.

\paragraph{Structural-frame combustibility.} The CDM
\texttt{Construction.ConstructionType} maps to a combustibility fraction
$\gamma\in[0,1]$ (0 = non-combustible reinforced concrete; 1 = timber frame;
unknown $\to$ a cautious default at the scale midpoint), via
\texttt{construction.combustibility\_by\_type}. It drives three quantities the
conflagration leg consumes: (i) a \emph{growth scale} interpolated over
$[\,g^{-},g^{+}]$ that multiplies the per-step intensity growth, so a
non-combustible frame catches and grows slower (the \emph{timing} leg, giving
suppression more of the race); (ii) the \emph{intensity ceiling} $I_{\max}$
(Model~B); and (iii) the structural-containment weight $w_{\text{struct}}$ above.
The frame also supplies the structural-fire-resistance fallback level when the
granular checklist is silent (a non-combustible frame is the physical evidence
for that field), taking priority over the BRI-grade fallback for that one field.

\subsection{Intensity Track (Model B)}

The latent intensity starts at $I_0=0$ and grows each 15-minute step by
\[
g_t = g_{\text{base}}\,(1 - w_{\text{damp}}\, e_{\text{passive}}), \qquad
g_t \leftarrow g_t \cdot \mu_{\text{supp}} \;\text{ once suppression is active},
\]
\[
I_{t+1} = \min\!\big(I_t + g_t,\; I_{\max}\big), \qquad g_t \ge 0,
\]
where $g_{\text{base}}$ is the compartmentation-selected growth rate (already
scaled by the combustibility growth factor of Model~C) and suppression becomes
active at the step $\lceil \tau_{\text{supp}} \rceil$. The \emph{fuel ceiling}
$I_{\max}$ is threshold-anchored on combustibility: every non-combustible frame
($\gamma$ at or below the combustible threshold) shares one low plateau that sits
\emph{below} even the worst $I_{\text{crit}}$, so its intensity can never cross
the critical threshold via the race; above the threshold the ceiling grades up to
a value exceeding any $I_{\text{crit}}$ as the frame becomes fully combustible.

The \emph{mean} profile fixes $g_{\text{base}}$, $\tau_{\text{supp}}$ and
$I_{\max}$, but each fire draws its own race realisation: per-fire lognormal
jitter is applied once to the growth rate
($g_{\text{base}}\!\cdot\!e^{\sigma_g Z}$), the bite time
($\tau_{\text{supp}}\!\cdot\!e^{\sigma_\tau Z}$) and the fuel ceiling
($I_{\max}\!\cdot\!e^{\sigma_c Z}$) before the march. Without the timing/growth
jitter the PNR verdict would be a deterministic property of the asset, forcing
the conflagration leg to be all-or-nothing; the ceiling jitter leaves a non-combustible
frame with no effective suppression a \emph{small residual} chance that an
unlucky (high-fuel-load) fire's ceiling exceeds $I_{\text{crit}}$ --- the
``eventually it catches'' tail --- rather than being absolutely immune. Setting
$\sigma_g=\sigma_\tau=\sigma_c=0$ recovers the deterministic track and hard cap.

\subsection{Point of No Return}

PNR latches at the first step $t$ for which suppression is not yet active and
\[
I_t > I_{\text{crit}} \quad\lor\quad
\big(\neg\,\text{reachable} \;\land\; \text{combustible}\big),
\]
where \emph{combustible} means the frame combustibility $\gamma$ clears the
combustible threshold; the latch is sticky (once set, it stays set). The second
disjunct is the operational heart of the model: a building taller than the
fire-service reach with no adequate internal suppression \emph{and a combustible
frame} is uncontrollable \emph{from ignition}, so PNR latches at step~1. A
\emph{non-combustible} tall, unsprinklered building does \emph{not} auto-latch:
its fuel ceiling $I_{\max}$ sits below $I_{\text{crit}}$, so the first disjunct
also cannot fire, and the structure contains the fire (only the jittered residual
remains). This is what turns building \emph{material} --- not height alone ---
into the live driver of total loss: height forces a conflagration only when the
frame is combustible.

\subsection{Containment Chain (Model D)}

Each fire is a discrete-time chain over the eight states
\[
S_0\text{ Ignition},\;S_1\text{ Growth},\;S_2\text{ Detected},\;
S_3\text{ Internal response},\;S_4\text{ External response},
\]
\[
S_5\text{ Contained},\;S_6\text{ Partial loss},\;S_7\text{ Total loss},
\]
with $\{S_5,S_6,S_7\}$ absorbing. At each step the intensity track advances and
the active $8\times8$ row-stochastic matrix is selected: while PNR is unlatched
the pre-PNR matrix is the controllability-weighted blend
\[
M \;=\; w\,M_{\text{AA}} \;+\; (1-w)\,M_{\text{weak}},
\]
(row-stochastic for any $w\in[0,1]$), interpolating continuously from the weak
matrix at $w=0$ to the controllable matrix at $w=1$; once PNR latches, every
transient state uses the absorbing point-of-no-return matrix, whose $S_5$
(Contained) column is identically zero. The next state is sampled from the
current state's row (with defensive renormalisation) until an absorbing state is
reached or the step budget is exhausted. The three base matrices are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:transitions}.

\subsection{Resilience Credit}

Aggregating over the $n_{\text{sim}}$ draws (of which $n_{\text{fires}}$
ignite), with terminal counts $n_{\text{contained}}$, $n_{\text{partial}}$,
$n_{\text{total}}$ and $n_{\text{PNR}}$ fires that reached the point of no
return, the credit currency is
\[
\text{loss frequency} = \frac{n_{\text{partial}}+n_{\text{total}}}{n_{\text{sim}}},
\qquad
\text{containment rate} = \frac{n_{\text{contained}}}{n_{\text{fires}}},
\]
\[
\text{fire spread (bps)} =
   \frac{n_{\text{PNR}}}{n_{\text{sim}}}\times 1.0 \times 10{,}000,
\]
the last being the full-conflagration leg the Commercial PRS Pricer consumes
(frequency $\times$ 100\% loss-given-event). It is combined with the flood/wind
legs into an all-in coupon by root-sum-of-squares,
$\text{all-in} = \sqrt{\sum_{\text{perils}} \text{leg}^2}$, treating the perils
as independent.

% 4. INPUT PARAMETERS AND CALIBRATION

\mkminputs

All numeric seeds live in \texttt{config/fire\_matrices.json}
(\texttt{version "seed-0"}) and are loaded by \texttt{config/fire.py}; no number
is embedded in the model code. The resilience-level vocabulary is imported from
\texttt{port.cdm.asset.resilience} so the level-to-index-to-fraction
mapping stays single-source with the rest of the CDM. The full
parameter set --- initiation base rates and the five modifier tables,
initiation-class priors, the progression dynamics (growth, critical intensity,
detection/suppression multipliers, passive/response ranges, height penalty,
fire-service reach, timing, controllability weights) and the three transition
matrices --- is reproduced verbatim in the Appendices.

\textbf{Calibration status.} Every value is a first-pass engineering-judgement
seed chosen for plausible \emph{direction} and \emph{ordering}, not magnitude:
e.g.\ hotels and mixed-use ignite more often than offices; worse condition and
worse protection raise frequency; better resilience levels shorten detection and
suppression times and raise the critical intensity. Calibration against
historical commercial fire-loss data (FIRE-R1) and an independent benchmarking
exercise (FIRE-R2) are open remediation items; until they close, the outputs
must be read as relative resilience signals, not absolute loss probabilities.

\subsection{Randomness}

All randomness flows through a single caller-owned
\texttt{numpy.random.Generator}, so an entire portfolio run is reproducible from
one seed --- matching the storm and typhoon model convention. Model~C is
deterministic, and Model~B is deterministic \emph{given a profile}; the per-asset
mean profile is drawn into a per-fire realisation inside Model~D, which jitters
the bite time and growth rate once per fire and then samples the chain march.
Every draw from the generator therefore still happens in Model~D, and the whole
run stays bit-for-bit reproducible.

\textit{Historical calibration and backtesting are open remediation items

run is bit-for-bit reproducible from a single seed. The sensitivity tables in
the next section are produced by running the \emph{actual} end-to-end model.

% 7. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
% ===================================================================
\mkmsensitivity

The tables below are generated by
\texttt{docs/models/sensitivities/fire\_resilience/} running the real
\texttt{simulate\_asset\_fire} against a fixed baseline (a fully-occupied
3-storey \emph{Office} in \emph{Fair} condition with every resilience field at
\emph{Meets minimum}), varying one input at a time over
20{,}000 Monte Carlo draws per cell.

The results show a clear \textbf{division of labour} across the four levers,
which is how the credit is designed to respond.

\emph{Suppression drives containment and loss frequency.} On the suppression
sweep the containment rate climbs monotonically from $\approx 47\%$ at
\emph{Not assessed} to $\approx 87\%$ at \emph{Verified}, and the annual loss
frequency falls roughly four-fold ($1.15\% \to 0.26\%$). This is the continuous
matrix blend at work: a better-suppressed asset has a higher controllability
score $C$, a blend weight $w$ nearer the controllable matrix, and so a higher
chance of reaching $S_5$ (Contained). This is the economically meaningful credit
the suppression system buys.

\emph{Compartmentation drives conflagration.} Passive compartmentation selects
the growth rate, so a poorly-compartmented asset grows fast enough to cross
$I_{\text{crit}}$ before the chain absorbs and shows a small but non-zero
fire-spread ($\approx 5$~bps), whereas a well-compartmented one stays at zero.
Commercial type and condition move loss frequency in the expected order (hotels,
mixed-use and poor condition worst).

\emph{The fire-service reach drives the catastrophic tail.} The height sweep
(held at sub-threshold \emph{Partial} internal suppression \emph{and} the default
combustibility) is the headline non-linearity: at or below the 4-storey reach the
credit is ordinary ($\approx 63\%$ containment, zero spread), but at \emph{five
storeys and above} suppression can no longer engage and a combustible-frame asset
collapses to $0\%$ containment and $\approx 203$~bps of fire spread --- a
\textbf{cliff, not a gradient}. The height~$\times$~suppression grid confirms the
cliff appears precisely when internal suppression is below the sprinkler
threshold; \emph{Meets minimum} or better keeps the asset reachable at any height.

\emph{Building material gates the high-rise tail.} The construction sweep (a
12-storey, sub-threshold-suppressed asset --- above the reach, so material is the
only thing left to govern the outcome) is the new lever. A non-combustible frame
is contained by its own structure: \emph{reinforced concrete} $\approx 88\%$
containment with near-zero spread, \emph{steel} / \emph{brick} $\approx 79\%$,
\emph{modern methods} $\approx 64\%$ (seeded less combustible than mixed), while
\emph{mixed construction} and \emph{timber} stay combustible and suffer the full
$0\%$/$\approx 203$~bps conflagration. The reach cliff above therefore fires only
for combustible frames; material, not height alone, decides the high-rise tail.

\subsection{Interpretation and known model weaknesses}

The sensitivity surface above is directionally correct, but two structural
features of the seed model deserve to be read alongside it:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The conflagration leg is muted for reachable buildings (by
        construction).} The chain march absorbs in $\approx 3$ steps on average,
        while latent intensity climbs only $\approx 0.8$ per step, so a
        \emph{reachable} fire is contained or lost long before the slow
        point-of-no-return race can cross $I_{\text{crit}}$. The PNR gate
        therefore fires almost only for the structural reach cliff (tall,
        under-sprinklered) plus a sliver of no-suppression cases. This is
        \emph{semantically} correct --- a fire contained in three steps did not
        reach a point of no return --- but it means fire-spread (bps) is driven
        chiefly by compartmentation and the reach cliff rather than by the active
        suppression level. Making spread respond strongly to suppression as well
        would require retuning the intensity-race timescale (a lower
        $I_{\text{crit}}$, of order 2--4) so the race competes with the
        three-step march; that is deferred as an enhancement because it is
        arguably less physical than letting compartmentation own conflagration.
  \item \textbf{The height sweep only shows the cliff at sub-threshold internal
        suppression.} A building with \emph{Meets minimum} or better internal
        sprinklers is reachable at any height, so its height sweep is flat by
        design; the cliff table here deliberately holds suppression at
        \emph{Partial} to expose the reach behaviour. A future enhancement would
        model fire-service reach as a brigade-specific, possibly height-graded
        capability rather than a single global 4-storey threshold, and would let
        internal-suppression adequacy degrade with height rather than flip at a
        hard index.
\end{itemize}

The exposed calibration knobs make the surface tunable without code changes:
$C_{\text{floor}}$/$C_{\text{ceil}}$ position and stretch the containment band;
\texttt{suppression\_bite\_multiplier\_by\_level} and \texttt{i\_crit\_by\_level}
set the gradient steepness; the \texttt{race\_jitter} sigmas
($\sigma_\tau,\sigma_g,\sigma_c$) control how smoothly the conflagration
probability grades and how large the non-combustible residual tail is; and the
\texttt{construction} block (\texttt{combustibility\_by\_type},
\texttt{combustible\_threshold}, \texttt{intensity\_ceiling},
\texttt{growth\_combustibility\_scale}, \texttt{structural\_containment\_weight})
sets where each frame material sits on the combustibility axis and how strongly
it gates growth, the fuel cap and passive containment.

% ===================================================================
% 8. LIMITATIONS AND ASSUMPTIONS
% ===================================================================
\mkmlimitations

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No historical calibration (FIRE-L1).} Every parameter is a
        first-pass placeholder; outputs are relative signals, not absolute loss
        probabilities.
  \item \textbf{Height proxy (FIRE-L2).} Building height is proxied by
        \texttt{NumberOfStoreys}; the commercial schema carries no explicit
        floor-level or floor-area metric, and the fire-service reach is a single
        global threshold rather than a brigade-specific capability.
  \item \textbf{Muted conflagration leg for reachable buildings (FIRE-L3).} The
        chain march absorbs faster ($\approx 3$ steps) than the latent intensity
        race reaches $I_{\text{crit}}$, so the point-of-no-return / fire-spread
        leg responds chiefly to compartmentation and the reach cliff rather than
        to the active suppression level (see Section~7, ``Interpretation and
        known model weaknesses''). Suppression instead expresses itself through
        containment rate and loss frequency. Retuning the intensity-race
        timescale to make spread suppression-sensitive is an open enhancement
        (FIRE-R5).
  \item \textbf{Single global reach threshold (FIRE-L4).} The fire-service reach
        is one global 4-storey cut-off and internal-suppression adequacy flips at
        a hard index, so the height cliff only appears at sub-threshold internal
        suppression. A brigade-specific, height-graded reach and a graded
        internal-suppression adequacy are an open enhancement (FIRE-R6).
  \item \textbf{Initiation class is cosmetic.} The initiation-class draw seeds an
        entry-point label but does not yet change the chain start state or
        dynamics.
  \item \textbf{Fixed loss-given-event.} The conflagration leg assumes a 100\%
        loss given a point-of-no-return event; partial-loss quantum is not
        modelled.
  \item \textbf{Construction combustibility is a single seeded fraction
        (FIRE-L5).} Frame combustibility is one number per
        \texttt{ConstructionType}; the threshold separating non-combustible from
        combustible frames, the placement of the ambiguous categories
        (\emph{Modern methods} seeded below the threshold and less combustible
        than \emph{Mixed construction}), the growth-scale band and the
        ceiling-jitter sigma are engineering-judgement seeds, not calibrated.
        Combustibility is a property of the structural frame only --- combustible
        cladding over a non-combustible frame (the Grenfell case) is not yet
        modelled. Recalibration against loss experience is an open item
        (FIRE-R7).
  \item \textbf{Independence assumption.} The root-sum-of-squares all-in coupon
        treats fire, flood and wind as independent perils.
\end{itemize}

% 9. GOVERNANCE VALIDATION QUESTIONS

\mkmgovernance

\mkmvqitem{1}{Model purpose and use}{What is the model used for, and is that use
consistent with its design?}
The model produces a fire-resilience credit (loss frequency, containment rate
and a full-conflagration bps leg) consumed by the Commercial PRS Pricer as an
independent peril. Use is consistent with design; it is \emph{not} a life-safety
or absolute-loss model and must not be used as one.

\mkmvqitem{2}{Data quality and lineage}{Are the model inputs of sufficient
quality, and is their lineage documented?}
Inputs are the CDM-derived \texttt{AssetFireFeatures}; lineage runs from the
v2 CDM resilience fields through Model~C. Missing options degrade gracefully to
neutral multipliers. Data \emph{quality} for calibration (historical fire-loss
records) is not yet established --- open under FIRE-R1.

\mkmvqitem{3}{Conceptual soundness}{Is the methodology theoretically sound and
appropriate?}
The four-model decomposition (Poisson initiation, per-fire-stochastic intensity
track, resilience$\rightarrow$dynamics mapping, controllability-blended absorbing
Bayesian chain) is a standard and defensible structure. The point-of-no-return
gate is the one bespoke mechanism and is documented in Section~3.4. Its known
weakness --- that the gate is muted for reachable buildings because the chain
absorbs faster than the intensity race --- is documented in Sections~7 and~8
(FIRE-L3).

\mkmvqitem{4}{Calibration and parameters}{How were parameters estimated, and are
they appropriate?}
All parameters are engineering-judgement seeds (\texttt{version "seed-0"}) chosen
for direction and ordering, not magnitude. Statistical calibration is an open
remediation item (FIRE-R1, FIRE-R2). This is the dominant model limitation.

\mkmvqitem{5}{Implementation verification}{Has the implementation been verified
against the specification?}
Yes, by unit tests covering each component model, the orchestrator and the
fire-service-reach interaction, plus a reproducibility test. The sensitivity
harness exercises the full end-to-end path.

\mkmvqitem{6}{Sensitivity and stability}{How sensitive are outputs to inputs and
assumptions?}
See Section~7. Outputs respond in the expected order to type, condition and
resilience levels, and exhibit the designed height$\times$suppression cliff.
Stability under draw count is adequate at $n_{\text{sim}}=20{,}000$;
formal stability bands are an open item.

\mkmvqitem{7}{Limitations and weaknesses}{Are the model's limitations understood
and documented?}
Yes --- see Section~8 (FIRE-L1--L4 plus three further documented assumptions) and
the ``Interpretation and known model weaknesses'' discussion in Section~7. The
placeholder calibration and the height proxy are the most material; the muted
conflagration leg (FIRE-L3) and single global reach threshold (FIRE-L4) are
documented enhancements.

\mkmvqitem{8}{Ongoing monitoring}{How will the model be monitored in production?}
Not yet in production. A monitoring plan (drift in input distributions,
backtest of realised vs.\ predicted fire frequency) will be defined as part of
FIRE-R3 before sign-off.

\mkmvqitem{9}{Governance and approval}{What is the approval status and who owns
the model?}
Owner: David K Kelly. Status: Development, Tier~2, RAG Amber. Not approved for
production; MRC sign-off is contingent on closing FIRE-R1--R7.
