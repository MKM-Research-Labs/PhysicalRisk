\mkmexecsummary
% =========================================================================

This document benchmarks the Event Frequency Model (MKM-EF-001), which turns a
gauge's event arrival rate $\lambda$ and its per-event flood probability $p$ into
the annual flood probability every PRS is priced from. It asks whether the code
computes what its model document (\texttt{event\_frequency\_plan.pdf}, v2.3) says,
and whether what the document says holds.

\paragraph{The code computes its documented formulae.} Over \bmCases{} cases ---
arrival rates from 0.5 to 50 a year, per-event probabilities from $10^{-6}$ to 0.5,
tenors of 1 to 10 years with a constant and a growing rate, three loss catalogues
and seven count series --- every quantity agrees with a 50-digit implementation of
the document's equations to better than $10^{-12}$ (Table~\ref{tab:bm-checks}):
the annual probability and rate, the tenor probability, the compound-Poisson
average annual loss and its standard error, the dispersion test and its
$p$-values, and both families' log-likelihoods. \bmWolframConfirms{} Wolfram's
own maximisation of the Negative Binomial likelihood finds nothing higher than
the model's fit.

\paragraph{Findings} (Table~\ref{tab:bm-claims}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The production term structure understates multi-year risk.} The
  hazard builder compounds the per-year \emph{probabilities} as if they were
  intensities (\texttt{annual\_hazard\_by\_year} into GH-001's
  \texttt{compute\_term\_structure}). The probability of at least one flood is
  understated by \bmTermLow\% to \bmTermHigh\% across the grid, worst at one year
  and high $\lambda p$, where it is $1-e^{-(1-e^{-\lambda p})}$ rather than
  $1-e^{-\lambda p}$ (Table~\ref{tab:bm-term}). The convention is deliberate and
  documented in the code, to reproduce the earlier term structure; the model's
  own \texttt{term\_exceedance\_probability} is exact, and the sensitivity module
  uses it, so the model reports two different tenor probabilities for one gauge.
\item \textbf{Annualisation loses precision at small $\lambda p$.} The code
  evaluates $1-\exp(-x)$, which cancels: the relative error reaches \bmPrecision{}
  below $\lambda p=\bmPrecisionBelow$. Immaterial at the probabilities priced, and
  removed exactly by $-\mathrm{expm1}(-x)$.
\end{enumerate}
The document's three numerical claims hold: the closed form of \S5.9 is an
identity (Wolfram proves it symbolically), the gate statistic's mean $|z|$ is
$\sqrt{2/\pi}=0.798$, and the sampling errors of \S7.2 scale as $1/\sqrt n$.

\subsection{What is benchmarked}
In \texttt{src/models/frequency}: \texttt{annual\_exceedance\_probability},
\texttt{annual\_exceedance\_rate} and \texttt{annual\_hazard\_by\_year}
(\texttt{annualise.py}); \texttt{term\_exceedance\_probability} and the rate
processes (\texttt{rate\_process.py}); \texttt{analytic\_average\_annual\_loss} and
\texttt{loss\_standard\_error} (\texttt{ylt/\_reconcile.py}); and the count
families, \texttt{fit\_poisson}, \texttt{fit\_negbin} and \texttt{dispersion\_test}
(\texttt{families/}). With them, \texttt{compute\_term\_structure} in
\texttt{src/models/hazard/gev.py}, which the per-year hazards feed. Each file is
loaded on its own and called as production calls it.

\subsection{What is not}
The Monte Carlo year simulation is checked by its own reconciliation gate, whose
statistic is tested here; the simulation's draws are not. The calibration of
$\lambda$ from gauge records, which the document's \S6 and \S8 identify as the
model's open problem, is not a calculation an oracle can check. This is a
benchmark of the implementation (validation question Q8), not independent
validation (Q9).

\mkmframework
% =========================================================================

With $N\sim\text{Poisson}(\lambda)$ events a year, each flooding independently with
probability $p$, the document's \S5.9 gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[(1-p)^N\bigr]=e^{-\lambda p},\qquad
P(\text{at least one flood in a year})=1-e^{-\lambda p},
\end{equation}
and the aggregate rate $\lambda p$, which is never smaller. Over a tenor of $T$
years with rates $\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_{T-1}$ (constant, or growing
geometrically as $\lambda_0(1+g)^y$), the exact tenor probability is
$1-\exp\bigl(-p\sum_y\lambda_y\bigr)$.

\paragraph{The convention.} The hazard builder instead takes each year's
\emph{probability} $q_y=1-e^{-\lambda_y p}$ and compounds it as an intensity,
$1-\exp\bigl(-\sum_y q_y\bigr)$. Since $q_y<\lambda_y p$, this always understates;
the gap grows with $\lambda p$ and is largest relative to the answer at one year.

\paragraph{Compound loss.} With event weights $w_k$ and losses $L_k$, the annual
loss is compound Poisson, with mean $\lambda\sum_k w_kL_k$ and, over $n$ simulated
years, standard error $\sqrt{\lambda\sum_k w_kL_k^2/n}$.

\paragraph{Count families.} The dispersion statistic $(n-1)s^2/\bar x$ is
$\chi^2_{n-1}$ under Poisson; the Negative Binomial is fitted with its mean at
$\bar x$ and its dispersion $\alpha=1/r$ by bounded search, which is the joint
maximum likelihood because the Negative Binomial's estimating equation for the mean
is solved by $\bar x$ at every $r$.

% =========================================================================
\section{Benchmark Method}
% =========================================================================

The equations above are evaluated with \texttt{mpmath} at 50 significant digits, from
the document rather than the code. Wolfram evaluates them independently from each
input's exact value at 60-digit precision: the compound loss through
\texttt{CompoundPoissonDistribution} of an \texttt{EmpiricalDistribution} (its rate
scaled by the weights' exact total, which \texttt{EmpiricalDistribution} would
otherwise renormalise away), the $p$-values through \texttt{ChiSquareDistribution},
and the Negative Binomial maximum by its own \texttt{FindMaximum} over both
parameters, with the Poisson limit as the supremum for under-dispersed data. It
proves the identity of \S5.9 with \texttt{Expectation} and \texttt{Simplify}.
Annual probabilities below $\lambda p=\bmOperating$ are reported as a finding
rather than judged against the $10^{-12}$ tolerance.
