% 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
% ===================================================================
\mkmexecsummary

The Event Wind Lookup Model (\texttt{MKM-WS-001}) answers a single question:
given a stored tropical-cyclone event, what was the sustained wind speed at an
arbitrary point $(\lambda, \phi)$ at an arbitrary hour $t$? It is the query
layer that sits between the Tropical Cyclone Progression and Wind-Field model
(\texttt{MKM-TC-001}), which generates and persists event trajectories, and the
Wind Damage model (\texttt{MKM-WD-001}), which converts per-property peak winds
into damage ratios and PRS event counts.

The model contributes no new physics. The radial profile, the motion-linked
asymmetry correction and the surface reduction are all owned by
\texttt{MKM-TC-001}; \texttt{MKM-WS-001} composes them with two additions of its
own:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Temporal interpolation.} Trajectories are persisted on a
        discrete hourly grid. A query at a non-grid hour linearly interpolates
        the latent state --- with circular interpolation on the heading to
        handle the $0/360^\circ$ seam and nearest-neighbour assignment for the
        discrete regime and land flags. Queries outside the trajectory's time
        range clamp to the first or last stored state; the model never
        extrapolates.
  \item \textbf{Event resolution and caching.} A query accepts a path to an
        \texttt{EVT-*.json} file, a raw JSON payload or an already-materialised
        trajectory. The \texttt{WindSpeedModel} class binds a catchment
        configuration once and caches parsed events, so repeated lookups against
        the same event do not re-parse JSON.
\end{itemize}

The model is catchment-agnostic: it consumes a \texttt{WindFieldParams} bundle
and a land mask, both typically drawn from the active
\texttt{CatchmentTyphoonConfig}, and holds no catchment constants of its own.

\textbf{Status:} Development (Tier 3, RAG Amber). The query path, interpolation,
loader and time-series helpers are delivered with unit tests. The model
inherits the calibration status of \texttt{MKM-TC-001}: the wind-field
parameters are engineering-judgement seeds, so absolute wind speeds are
directional rather than calibrated.

\textbf{Materiality note:} The model is deliberately thin. Its material risk is
not in its own mathematics but in the possibility of \emph{silently} returning a
plausible number for a query that should have been rejected --- see
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, WS-L1 (clamping) and WS-L2 (no staleness check
against the source event).

% 2. MODEL PURPOSE AND SCOPE
% ===================================================================
\mkmpurpose

\subsection{Purpose}
To provide a single, reusable wind-at-point query so that every downstream
consumer --- the per-property wind time-series stage of the port pipeline, the
wind-damage composer, ad-hoc analytical queries and the Trader's Workstation
wind views --- evaluates the wind field identically. Before this model existed,
the evaluation order (geometry, profile, asymmetry, surface) risked being
re-implemented per consumer.

\subsection{Scope}
In scope:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Sustained wind speed in m/s at a point, for a stored event, at an hour.
  \item Linear interpolation of the latent typhoon state between stored hours.
  \item Multi-hour queries at one point (\texttt{windspeed\_series}).
  \item Event loading from \texttt{EVT-*.json}, including tolerant stripping of
        the pipeline's \texttt{summary} header block.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out of Scope}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Generating trajectories --- owned by \texttt{MKM-TC-001}.
  \item The wind-field physics itself (radial profile, asymmetry, surface
        reduction) --- owned by \texttt{MKM-TC-001}; this model calls it.
  \item Gust factors, gust duration statistics and directional gust conversion.
  \item Damage, loss or pricing --- owned by \texttt{MKM-WD-001} and the PRS
        pricer.
  \item Bulk per-property pre-computation. The port pipeline writes per-property
        wind time-series to disk; this model serves ad-hoc queries at points not
        in the configured portfolio, or on a finer time grid than the pipeline's
        $\Delta t$.
\end{itemize}

% 3. MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK
% ===================================================================
\mkmframework

\subsection{Query Composition}
For an event with time-ordered states $\{s_i\}$, a query at hour $t$ and point
$x = (\lambda_x, \phi_x)$ evaluates
\[
  V(x, t) \;=\; \underbrace{\rho_{\text{surf}}(x)}_{\text{surface}}\;
                \underbrace{\bigl(1 + \epsilon \cos(\theta - \varphi)\bigr)}_{\text{asymmetry}}\;
                \underbrace{V_{\text{sym}}\bigl(r;\, s(t)\bigr)}_{\text{radial profile}},
\]
where $s(t)$ is the interpolated state at hour $t$, $r$ is the great-circle
distance from the storm centre to $x$ and $\theta$ is the forward azimuth from
the centre to $x$. The result is floored at zero.

\subsection{State Interpolation}
Let $s_a$ and $s_b$ be the stored states bracketing $t$, with
$\tau = (t - t_a)/(t_b - t_a) \in [0, 1]$. Continuous fields --- position,
translation speed, $V_{\max}$, $R_{\max}$, $R_{\text{outer}}$ and time --- are
interpolated linearly:
\[
  z(t) = z_a + \tau\,(z_b - z_a).
\]
Heading is interpolated on the circle. Writing $\Delta\psi$ for the signed
compass difference $\psi_b \ominus \psi_a$ taken on the shorter arc,
\[
  \psi(t) = \bigl(\psi_a + \tau\,\Delta\psi\bigr) \bmod 360^\circ,
\]
which avoids the pathological interpolation a naive linear blend would produce
across the $0/360^\circ$ seam (for example $350^\circ$ to $10^\circ$ blending
through $180^\circ$ rather than through $0^\circ$).

Discrete fields --- the regime class $k$ and the land flag --- take the nearest
neighbour, $k(t) = k_a$ for $\tau < 0.5$ and $k_b$ otherwise. Interpolating a
categorical variable is not meaningful, and a mid-point switch keeps the field
piecewise constant with a single transition between stored steps.

\subsection{Clamping}
Queries outside the stored time range return the boundary state:
\[
  s(t) = \begin{cases}
    s_1                & t \le t_1, \\
    s_n                & t \ge t_n, \\
    \text{interpolated}& \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
This is a deliberate design choice, not a numerical guard: extrapolating a
cyclone trajectory beyond its simulated horizon would fabricate hazard. The
consequence is that an out-of-range query returns a valid-looking number rather
than an error --- see WS-L1.

\subsection{Geometry}
Distance uses the Haversine great-circle formula in its $\operatorname{atan2}$
form, which is stable for closely-spaced points:
\[
  a = \sin^2\!\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}
      + \cos\phi_c \cos\phi_x \sin^2\!\frac{\Delta\lambda}{2},
  \qquad
  r = 2R_\oplus \operatorname{atan2}\bigl(\sqrt{a}, \sqrt{1-a}\bigr).
\]
Azimuth is the standard forward-bearing formula, returned in compass degrees on
$[0, 360)$. The bearing is undefined at $r = 0$; the implementation substitutes
$\theta = 0$ there, which is harmless because the asymmetry term at the storm
centre multiplies the eye-floor wind $\alpha_{\text{eye}} V_{\max}$ by a factor
that is bounded within $1 \pm \epsilon$ for any azimuth.

\subsection{Interfaces}
Two entry points are offered deliberately. The free function
\texttt{windspeed(event, hour, lon, lat, *, wind\_field\_params, land\_mask)} is
stateless and suits one-shot queries. The \texttt{WindSpeedModel} class binds a
\texttt{CatchmentTyphoonConfig} at construction and caches loaded events by
source key; it is the correct choice inside any loop over properties or hours.
The class is callable, so \texttt{ws(event, hour, lon, lat)} and
\texttt{ws.query(\dots)} are equivalent.

\subsection{Performance Characteristics}
Interpolation performs a linear scan over the state list. For the 168-state
hourly trajectories the pipeline produces this is adequate and keeps the code
obvious; a bisection search becomes worthwhile only if event horizons grow by an
order of magnitude. The dominant cost in a portfolio-scale sweep is JSON
parsing, which the model's event cache removes for all queries after the first
against a given event.

\subsection{Unit Discipline}
Wind speeds are m/s throughout the model's own surface. The latent state stores
translation speed in km/h, and the conversion to m/s happens inside the
asymmetry term where the two quantities must be commensurate. Callers should
treat every value returned by this model as m/s; conversion to km/h, if needed
for presentation, belongs in the presentation layer.

% 6. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
% ===================================================================
\mkmsensitivity
\label{sec:sensitivity}

The tables below are generated by calling the model directly
(\texttt{python -m docs.models.sensitivities.generate\_all --model windspeed}).
They demonstrate the four axes along which the query responds: radial distance,
storm intensity, azimuth relative to motion, and surface class --- plus the
temporal interpolation behaviour.

Three features are worth reading closely:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The radial profile is \emph{not} monotone in $V_{\max}$ at long range.
        Beyond roughly 250~km a more intense storm produces \emph{lower} wind
        than a weaker one, because the outer profile is anchored at
        $V_{\text{outer,ref}}$ at a fixed $R_{\text{outer}}$: a higher peak wind
        must decay faster to meet the same anchor. This is a property of the
        anchored parameterisation, not a defect, but it means the model should
        not be used to rank storm severity at points far outside the gale radius.
  \item The decay-length calibration is ill-conditioned just above the anchor
        wind. At $V_{\max} = 20$~m/s the solved $L$ exceeds 800~km, against
        213~km at $V_{\max} = 50$~m/s, because $-\ln(V_{\text{ref}}/V_{\max})$
        approaches zero. See WS-L3.
  \item Asymmetry is modest at realistic translation speeds: at 18~km/h the
        multiplier spans roughly $0.96$ to $1.06$. It becomes material only for
        fast-moving systems.
\end{itemize}

\mkmvalidation

\subsection{Verification Approach}
The model is verified by unit test against properties that hold independently of
calibration:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Interpolation exactness.} A query at a stored hour returns the
        stored state unchanged.
  \item \textbf{Interpolation monotonicity.} For a monotone field, the
        interpolated value at the midpoint lies between the bracketing values.
  \item \textbf{Circular heading.} Interpolating $350^\circ \to 10^\circ$
        crosses $0^\circ$, not $180^\circ$.
  \item \textbf{Clamping.} Queries before the first and after the last stored
        hour return the boundary states exactly.
  \item \textbf{Loader tolerance.} An \texttt{EVT-*.json} payload carrying the
        pipeline's \texttt{summary} header parses identically to one without it;
        a missing file raises \texttt{FileNotFoundError}.
  \item \textbf{Cache transparency.} A cached query returns the same value as an
        uncached one, and \texttt{clear\_cache()} restores the uncached path.
  \item \textbf{Series consistency.} \texttt{windspeed\_series} over $n$ hours
        equals $n$ independent \texttt{windspeed} calls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Backtesting}
No backtest against observed anemometer records has been performed. Such a
backtest would validate \texttt{MKM-TC-001}'s wind-field parameterisation rather
than this model's composition logic, and is tracked as a remediation item
against that model. This model's own correctness claim rests on the verification
properties above.

% 8. LIMITATIONS
% ===================================================================
\mkmlimitations
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{description}
  \item[WS-L1 --- Silent clamping.] Out-of-range hours return the boundary state
        rather than raising. A caller that mis-computes an hour offset receives a
        plausible wind speed with no signal that the query was invalid.
        \emph{Mitigation:} consumers that compute hours arithmetically should
        assert the hour lies within the event horizon before querying.
  \item[WS-L2 --- No event-staleness check.] The loader does not verify that a
        cached or supplied trajectory corresponds to the event the caller
        believes it is querying, nor that the file has not changed on disk since
        it was cached. Long-lived \texttt{WindSpeedModel} instances spanning a
        regeneration of the event store will serve stale winds.
        \emph{Mitigation:} construct the model per pipeline run; call
        \texttt{clear\_cache()} after any event regeneration.
  \item[WS-L3 --- Ill-conditioned decay calibration near the anchor.] For
        $V_{\max}$ marginally above $V_{\text{outer,ref}}$ the solved decay
        length diverges, producing an implausibly flat outer profile. Storms at
        or below the anchor fall back to a geometric length and are explicitly
        not physically realistic. Sub-gale systems carry negligible damage
        weight, so the practical impact is small, but the model should not be
        used for tropical-depression-strength analysis.
  \item[WS-L4 --- Binary land mask.] Surface reduction is a two-valued function
        of land/sea at the evaluation point. Terrain roughness, exposure class
        and topographic speed-up are not represented, so winds at exposed
        coastal or elevated sites are understated relative to sheltered inland
        sites at the same distance.
  \item[WS-L5 --- Linear state interpolation.] A cyclone's intensity can change
        faster than linearly between stored hours, particularly during rapid
        intensification or at landfall. Sub-hourly queries during such phases
        carry more error than the hourly grid suggests.
  \item[WS-L6 --- Inherited calibration status.] Absolute wind speeds are only
        as good as \texttt{MKM-TC-001}'s seed parameters. Relative comparisons
        (across distance, azimuth or surface class) are more reliable than
        absolute levels.
\end{description}

% 9. GOVERNANCE VALIDATION QUESTIONS
% ===================================================================
\mkmgovernance

\mkmvqitem{1}{Model purpose and use}{A wind-at-point query layer over stored
tropical-cyclone events. Consumed by the per-property wind time-series stage,
by \texttt{MKM-WD-001} for damage, and by ad-hoc analysis. It does not itself
price or generate hazard.}

\mkmvqitem{2}{Conceptual soundness}{The model composes an established
parameterisation owned upstream. Its own contributions --- linear state
interpolation with circular heading handling, boundary clamping, and event
caching --- are standard and are each covered by a verification property in
Section~7.}

\mkmvqitem{3}{Data quality}{Inputs are the event store written by
\texttt{MKM-TC-001} and the property coordinates from the CDM. The loader
tolerates the pipeline's optional \texttt{summary} block but performs no
schema validation beyond what \texttt{TyphoonTrajectory.from\_dict} enforces.}

\mkmvqitem{4}{Assumptions}{Three material assumptions: that the latent state
varies linearly between stored hours (WS-L5); that clamping rather than
extrapolation is the correct treatment outside the event horizon; and that a
binary land/sea surface factor is adequate (WS-L4).}

\mkmvqitem{5}{Implementation verification}{Unit tests cover the query function,
the model class and its cache, interpolation including the circular-heading and
clamping edge cases, the loader including the missing-file and summary-block
paths, and the series wrapper.}

\mkmvqitem{6}{Sensitivity and stability}{See Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}. The
response is smooth and monotone in distance within the gale radius and in
azimuth. The non-monotonicity in $V_{\max}$ at long range and the
ill-conditioned decay calibration near the anchor wind are documented as WS-L3
and are properties of the upstream parameterisation.}

\mkmvqitem{7}{Limitations and weaknesses}{Documented in Section~8
(WS-L1--WS-L6). Silent clamping (WS-L1) and event staleness (WS-L2) are the
operationally material ones because both fail quietly.}

\mkmvqitem{8}{Ongoing monitoring}{Not yet in production. The monitoring plan
follows \texttt{MKM-TC-001}: distribution checks on queried peak winds per
event, and an alert on any query whose hour is clamped.}
