\mkmexecsummary
% =========================================================================

This document benchmarks the Storm Intensity Distribution (MKM-SI-001): a Normal
body joined to a Pareto tail, clamped to $[I_{\min},I_{\max}]$, with five scenario
families from \texttt{historical} to \texttt{extreme}. It asks whether the code
computes what its model document (\texttt{storm\_intensity.pdf}, v1.3) says, and
whether what the document says holds.

\paragraph{The model is not in the production chain.} Production storm
intensities are drawn in \texttt{src/port/src/storm\_multi/generators/
batch\_generator.py}: an intensity category from \texttt{DEFAULT\_INTENSITY\_WEIGHTS},
then a Normal draw per category from \texttt{BASE\_INTENSITY\_PARAMS}, varied by
$\pm20\%$ within a sequence. Nothing in the port, the routes, the app commands or the
scripts imports \texttt{IntensityDistribution}; only its own command line and tests
do. The model inventory records handovers from SI-001 to SG-001, SS-001, RA-001 and
FC-001 that the code does not implement, and its stress scenarios do not reach the
storm catalogue, the hazard curves (MKM-GH-001) or PRS pricing (MKM-PR-001).

\paragraph{The code computes its documented formulae.} Over \bmCases{} cases the
model agrees with a 50-digit implementation of the document's equations: exceedance
to \bmExcWorst, return-period intensity to \bmRpWorst, and the quantile to
\bmInvWorst{} --- the error of Acklam's inverse-normal approximation, carried through
$\mu+\sigma z$. \bmWolframConfirms

\paragraph{Findings.} Of the six claims tested (Table~\ref{tab:bm-claims}), two hold,
three do not, and one is a finding:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The clamp is not a slight distortion.} The Pareto tail runs past
  $I_{\max}=100$, and every storm it would place there is set to exactly 100: from
  \bmMassMaxLow\% of storms (\texttt{historical}) to \bmMassMaxHigh\% (\texttt{extreme}).
\item \textbf{So return periods saturate.} The $T$-year intensity is $I_{\max}$ for
  every $T$ beyond \bmCapHistorical{} years in the \texttt{historical} scenario and
  \bmCapBaseline{} years in \texttt{baseline}; in the stress scenarios, for every
  return period of interest (Table~\ref{tab:bm-rp}). A 1-in-2-year storm and a
  1-in-1000-year storm are the same.
\item \textbf{The density is discontinuous at the threshold.} The exceedance is
  continuous there, as the document proves; the code's docstring further says the
  Pareto scale makes the \emph{density} continuous, which it does not: the density
  jumps by a factor of \bmPdfLow{} to \bmPdfHigh.
\item \textbf{Acklam's accuracy is relative, not absolute.} The document quotes
  $1.15\times10^{-9}$ absolute error; the measured worst is \bmAcklamAbs{} absolute
  and \bmAcklamRel{} relative, in the far tail. The effect on intensities is
  negligible.
\end{enumerate}
The sampling jitter changes the mean and standard deviation by at most \bmJitter\%,
as the document says.

\subsection{What is benchmarked}
\texttt{IntensityDistribution} in \texttt{src/models/intensity/distribution}:
\texttt{exceedance\_probability}, \texttt{inverse\_exceedance},
\texttt{return\_period\_intensity} and the Acklam inverse normal behind them, for
each of the five \texttt{SCENARIO\_FAMILIES}, imported as production would import
the package.

\subsection{What is not}
The calibration the document describes (\S4.3: maximum likelihood on the body, Hill
estimation on the tail) has no code in the repository; the scenario parameters are
constants. The inventory's methodology line, ``maximum likelihood threshold
estimation'', contradicts the document's own \S8.6 (``no automatic threshold
selection''). Neither can be benchmarked. This is a benchmark of the implementation
(validation question Q8), not independent validation (Q9).

\mkmframework
% =========================================================================

With Normal body $(\mu,\sigma)$, threshold $u$, tail index $\alpha$ and clamp
$[I_{\min},I_{\max}]$, the document defines $p_{\text{tail}}=S_N(u)$, the Normal's
exceedance at $u$, and
\begin{equation}
S(x)=\begin{cases}
1 & x\le I_{\min}\\
S_N(x) & I_{\min}<x\le u\\
p_{\text{tail}}\,(u/x)^{\alpha} & u<x<I_{\max}\\
0 & x\ge I_{\max}
\end{cases}
\qquad
S^{-1}(p)=\begin{cases}
\mu+\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(1-p) & p\ge p_{\text{tail}}\\
u\,(p_{\text{tail}}/p)^{1/\alpha} & p<p_{\text{tail}}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $S^{-1}$ clamped to $[I_{\min},I_{\max}]$, and the $T$-year intensity
$S^{-1}\bigl(1/(T\lambda)\bigr)$ for $\lambda$ storms a year. Above $u$ the tail is
a generalised Pareto with shape $1/\alpha$ and scale $u/\alpha$: a GPD whose scale is
tied to the threshold, not fitted.

\paragraph{The clamp.} Because the tail is unbounded and the clamp is not a
renormalisation, the sampled intensity has point masses
$p_{\text{tail}}(u/I_{\max})^{\alpha}$ at $I_{\max}$ and $1-S_N(I_{\min})$ at
$I_{\min}$. The $T$-year intensity reaches $I_{\max}$ once $1/(T\lambda)$ falls
below the first of these, that is for $T>1/\bigl(\lambda\,p_{\text{tail}}(u/I_{\max})^{\alpha}\bigr)$.

\section{Benchmark Method}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{References}
The equations above are evaluated with \texttt{mpmath} at 50 significant digits, from
the document rather than the code, with the exact inverse normal. Wolfram evaluates
the same expressions from each input's exact value at 60-digit precision. The Normal
exceedance is written out as $\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl((x-\mu)/(\sigma\sqrt2)\bigr)$:
Wolfram~15.0's \texttt{SurvivalFunction} of a \texttt{NormalDistribution} returns a
machine-precision result even for a 60-digit argument, which would have capped the
oracle at 16 digits. Acklam's approximation is compared with Wolfram's exact
\texttt{InverseCDF} on the very doubles it is handed, so input rounding is not
counted as approximation error. The moments of the sampled intensity are integrated
over $p$ by both, and the jitter's effect is computed in closed form.

\subsection{Cases}
For every scenario: the exceedance at every 2.5 from $I_{\min}$ to $I_{\max}$; the
quantile at $p=1,2,5\times10^{-k}$ ($k=1..6$) and their complements; the return-period
intensity for $T$ from 2 to 1000 years. Acklam's approximation alone over both tails
down to $10^{-15}$ and the body in steps of 0.01.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Benchmark, not validation.} The code is shown to compute the documented
  formulae; whether a Normal--Pareto hybrid suits storm intensity, and whether the
  scenario parameters are calibrated, are separate questions.
\item \textbf{Wiring.} Whether SI-001 should drive the storm catalogue, or the
  inventory's handovers should be corrected to the code, is for the model owner and
  the MRC.
\item \textbf{The clamp.} Renormalising the tail on $[u,I_{\max}]$, or raising
  $I_{\max}$, would remove the point mass and restore informative return periods;
  either changes the model and is for the owner.
\item \textbf{Documentation.} \S3.5 and \S5.3 should quote Acklam's accuracy as
  relative; \S8.4 should state the masses above; the code's docstring should drop
  the claim of a continuous density.
\end{enumerate}
