% 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
% ===================================================================
\mkmexecsummary

The Wind Damage Model (\texttt{MKM-WD-001}) converts the peak sustained wind a
property experienced during a tropical-cyclone event into a damage ratio, and
into the binary damage-onset trigger the Property Risk Spread (PRS) pricer
consumes as a wind event count. It is the wind-side twin of the flood
depth-damage model (\texttt{MKM-DD-001}) and deliberately mirrors its
architecture: a pure scalar curve for baseline analysis, and a resilience-aware
wrapper that shifts the curve using the Building Resilience Index
(\texttt{MKM-BRI-001}).

The model has three components:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Severity.} A logistic vulnerability curve
        $DR(v) = \bigl[1 + e^{-a(v - v_{50})}\bigr]^{-1}$ maps peak sustained
        wind to a damage ratio. The slope $a$ and the baseline $v_{50}$ are
        configuration parameters.
  \item \textbf{Resilience.} The 50\%-damage threshold $v_{50}$ starts at the
        property's operational threshold (from the CDM hazard profile) and
        receives a signed, capped shift derived from the property's BRI wind
        sub-score and composite score. A hardened building tolerates more wind
        before the same damage; a poor building tolerates less.
  \item \textbf{Persistence.} A saturating duration-of-load factor
        $\Phi(t) \in [\phi_{\text{gust}}, 1]$ scales the peak-curve damage by how
        long the wind stayed above the damage-onset threshold. A momentary gust
        realises only the floor; a prolonged blow realises the full peak-curve
        damage. The peak-only curves are exactly the $t \to \infty$ limit, so the
        two families remain consistent and existing peak-only callers are
        unchanged.
\end{itemize}

The PRS leg is deliberately \emph{binary}, not continuous: the spread is a
frequency payout driven by the count of events where the peak wind reached the
BRI-adjusted effective threshold. The continuous damage ratio is the
insurance-loss view served by the wind-impact route and must not feed the
spread.

\textbf{Status:} Development (Tier 2, RAG Amber). All three components, the CDM
navigation layer, the threshold resolver, the event composer and the port stage
are delivered with unit tests. Every numeric parameter is an
engineering-judgement seed; the results in this document are directional, not
calibrated.

\textbf{Key risk:} The model has never been calibrated against observed wind
loss. The $\pm$5~m/s cap on the BRI shift is large relative to the curve's
transition width, so the resilience credit is materially sensitive to a
parameter that has no empirical basis --- see WD-L1.

% ===================================================================
% 2. MODEL PURPOSE AND SCOPE
% ===================================================================
\mkmpurpose

\subsection{Purpose}
To price building wind resilience pari-passu with flood, fire and seismic
resilience, so that a property's total physical-risk spread reflects wind
exposure and wind hardening on the same footing as the other perils. The model
supplies two distinct outputs to two distinct consumers: a damage ratio for
insurance-loss and impact reporting, and a binary trigger for PRS event counting.

\subsection{Scope}
In scope: per-property wind damage for events generated by the coupled
storm-typhoon pipeline; resolution of the operational wind threshold from the
CDM; the BRI-driven shift of that threshold; the duration-of-load adjustment;
and the per-event composition that joins a wind time-series to a portfolio.

\subsection{Out of Scope}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Generating the wind field or the event set (\texttt{MKM-TC-001}) and
        querying it at a point (\texttt{MKM-WS-001}).
  \item Gust-factor conversion. The curve is defined on \emph{sustained} wind;
        no peak-gust conversion is applied.
  \item Debris impact, wind-driven rain ingress and cascading failure between
        adjacent buildings.
  \item Contents, business interruption and additional living expense --- the
        damage ratio is a structure-damage ratio.
  \item Directional vulnerability. The curve is isotropic; a building with a
        weak elevation facing the storm is treated the same as one facing away.
\end{itemize}

% 3. MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK
% ===================================================================
\mkmframework

\subsection{Vulnerability Curve}
The pure curve is a logistic in peak sustained wind $v$ (m/s):
\[
  DR(v) \;=\; \frac{1}{1 + \exp\bigl(-a\,(v - v_{50})\bigr)},
  \qquad DR(v) = 0 \text{ for } v \le 0,
\]
with $a = 0.20$~m$^{-1}$s and baseline $v_{50} = 27.8$~m/s. The exponent is
guarded at $\pm 700$ to avoid overflow at extreme inputs, saturating cleanly to
0 and 1. The choice of a logistic rather than the piecewise-linear form used on
the flood side reflects the smoother observed onset of wind damage: there is no
wind analogue of the sharp threshold a floor level imposes on flood depth.

\subsection{BRI Threshold Shift}
The effective threshold is the property's operational threshold translated by a
signed resilience term:
\[
  v_{50}^{\text{eff}} \;=\; v_{50}^{\text{thr}}
    + \operatorname{clip}\Bigl(
        \alpha_w \ln\frac{B_w}{\mu_w}
      + \beta_c \ln\frac{B_c}{\mu_c},
      \;\pm\, \delta_{\max}\Bigr),
\]
where $B_w$ is the BRI wind sub-score, $B_c$ the BRI composite score,
$\mu_w = \mu_c = 0.50$ the neutral references, $\alpha_w = 6.0$~m/s and
$\beta_c = 3.0$~m/s per log-unit, and $\delta_{\max} = 5.0$~m/s the cap. Scores
are floored at $10^{-6}$ before the logarithm.

The structure is the direct analogue of the flood-side \emph{stilt}, in m/s
rather than metres. It is log-differential so that the credit is proportional to
the \emph{ratio} of the score to the neutral reference: moving from 0.25 to 0.50
earns the same shift as moving from 0.50 to 1.00. The wind sub-score carries
twice the weight of the composite, reflecting that wind-specific measures
(roof-to-wall connection, opening protection, roof covering) bear more directly
on wind damage than general building quality.

Passing \texttt{None} for either score substitutes the neutral reference,
contributing zero from that term. This is a deliberate no-information default,
not an error path.

\subsection{Persistence}
The duration-of-load multiplier is a saturating exponential in the hours $t$
the sustained wind stayed above the damage-onset threshold:
\[
  \Phi(t) \;=\; \phi_{\text{gust}}
    + \bigl(1 - \phi_{\text{gust}}\bigr)\bigl(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\bigr),
  \qquad \Phi(t \le 0) = \phi_{\text{gust}},
\]
with $\phi_{\text{gust}} = 0.45$ and $\tau = 4.0$~h. The full damage ratio is
the product
\[
  DR = DR_{\text{peak}}\bigl(v;\, v_{50}^{\text{eff}}\bigr)\;\Phi(t).
\]
The rationale is physical: fastener fatigue, progressive covering loss and water
ingress all accumulate with exposure time, so a single momentary gust at a given
speed is less destructive than hours at the same speed. The floor
$\phi_{\text{gust}}$ encodes that even an instantaneous peak does real damage.

\subsection{PRS Damage-Onset Trigger}
The PRS leg counts events, not losses. The trigger is
\[
  \mathbf{1}\bigl[v_{\text{peak}} \ge v_{50}^{\text{eff}}\bigr],
\]
falling back to the raw operational threshold when the effective threshold is
absent from a damage row (legacy rows written before the field existed). Because
the threshold is BRI-adjusted, a resilient building fires the trigger less
readily than a poor building exposed to the same wind --- which is precisely the
mechanism by which resilience earns spread. Wind-without-flood is therefore
possible, and its frequency is governed by where the BRI-adjusted exceedance
sits relative to the coupled storm's wind.

\subsection{Threshold Resolution}
The operational threshold is resolved from the CDM in a fixed precedence order:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{WindThresholdMinorMps} --- damage onset, preferred.
  \item \texttt{WindThresholdMajorMps} --- severe, used as fallback.
  \item \texttt{WindThresholdKph} --- legacy, divided by 3.6.
  \item The configured default (100~km/h $\to$ 27.8~m/s).
\end{enumerate}

The unit convention is a known historical hazard in this model: the CDM
originally carried km/h and now carries m/s. All internal mathematics is in m/s;
the resolver is the single conversion point.

\subsection{Calibration Status}
None of the ten parameters above is empirically calibrated. Each is an
engineering-judgement seed chosen so that the curve behaves sensibly across the
range of winds the coupled pipeline produces: damage onset around gale force,
half damage near 28~m/s (roughly 100~km/h), and saturation by 50~m/s. The
persistence parameters are anchored on the observation that a typical tropical
cyclone subjects a point to damaging winds for a few hours, so $\tau = 4$~h
places realistic events on the steep part of $\Phi$ rather than at either
asymptote.

Four curve functions are exposed, forming a two-by-two grid over
\{pure, BRI-aware\} $\times$ \{peak-only, persistence-aware\}:

    Pure curve & \texttt{scalar\_peak\_damage} & \texttt{scalar\_wind\_damage} \\

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The peak-only column is the $t \to \infty$ limit of the persistence column, so
the grid is internally consistent by construction rather than by convention.

\subsection{Separation from Pricing}
\texttt{is\_prs\_wind} lives in this model, but it returns a boolean only. No
part of this model converts damage to currency or to basis points; that is the
PRS pricer's responsibility. The separation matters because the continuous
damage ratio and the binary trigger must not be conflated --- feeding the
damage ratio into the spread would double-count severity that the spread's
frequency formulation already handles.

% 6. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
% ===================================================================
\mkmsensitivity
\label{sec:sensitivity}

Generated by calling the model directly
(\texttt{python -m docs.models.sensitivities.generate\_all --model winddamage}).
The tables demonstrate the three axes --- severity, resilience and duration ---
and the resulting PRS trigger behaviour.

The material observations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The resilience credit is large.} At 27.8~m/s an NR-grade
        building takes 0.85 damage against 0.24 for AA --- a factor of 3.5. The
        spread narrows at both tails, as the logistic saturates.
  \item \textbf{The credit is widest in the mid-range.} Below 15~m/s and above
        55~m/s, grade barely matters. Between 20 and 40~m/s it dominates. This
        is the designed behaviour: resilience should not be credited for
        surviving a breeze, nor for surviving a wind that destroys everything.
  \item \textbf{The trigger is grade-discriminating exactly where it should be.}
        At 30~m/s, NR and B fire while A and AA do not; by 35~m/s all four fire.
        The discriminating band is roughly 19--33~m/s, which is where the
        effective thresholds spread across grades.
  \item \textbf{Persistence is material but bounded.} $\Phi$ ranges from 0.45 at
        zero duration to 0.997 at 24~h, so duration can vary realised damage by
        slightly more than a factor of two at fixed severity.
\end{itemize}

% 7. VALIDATION AND BACKTESTING
% ===================================================================
\mkmvalidation

\subsection{Verification Approach}
Verified by unit test against calibration-independent properties:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bounds.} Every curve returns a value in $[0, 1]$ for all inputs,
        including extreme and negative winds.
  \item \textbf{Monotonicity.} Damage is non-decreasing in wind and in duration,
        and non-increasing in BRI score.
  \item \textbf{Midpoint identity.} $DR(v_{50}^{\text{eff}}) = 0.5$ exactly,
        for any threshold and any BRI pair.
  \item \textbf{Neutral BRI.} Scores at the reference 0.50 produce a zero shift;
        \texttt{None} behaves identically to the reference.
  \item \textbf{Cap.} The shift never exceeds $\pm\delta_{\max}$ regardless of
        score, including at the log floor.
  \item \textbf{Persistence limits.} $\Phi(0) = \phi_{\text{gust}}$ and
        $\Phi(t) \to 1$; the persistence-aware curves converge to the peak-only
        curves as $t$ grows.
  \item \textbf{Threshold precedence.} Each of the four resolution branches is
        exercised, including the legacy km/h conversion.
  \item \textbf{Trigger fallback.} \texttt{is\_prs\_wind} falls back to the raw
        threshold when the effective threshold is absent, and returns
        \texttt{False} rather than raising on malformed rows.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Backtesting}
No backtest has been performed. A meaningful backtest requires paired
observations of peak sustained wind at the property and realised structural
loss, which the platform does not currently hold. This is remediation item
WD-R1 and is a precondition of MRC sign-off.

% 8. LIMITATIONS
% ===================================================================
\mkmlimitations
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{description}
  \item[WD-L1 --- Uncalibrated resilience cap.] $\delta_{\max} = 5$~m/s is large
        relative to the logistic's transition width ($1/a = 5$~m/s). A capped
        shift therefore moves the curve by a full transition width, changing
        mid-range damage by a factor of several. The credit the model awards is
        thus highly sensitive to a parameter with no empirical basis.
  \item[WD-L2 --- Sustained wind, no gust conversion.] The curve is defined on
        sustained wind but real damage is driven substantially by gusts. The
        implied gust factor is folded into the seed calibration of $v_{50}$
        rather than modelled, so any change to the wind-field model's averaging
        convention would silently invalidate the curve.
  \item[WD-L3 --- Isotropic vulnerability.] Direction of attack, building
        orientation, and shielding by neighbouring structures are not
        represented.
  \item[WD-L4 --- Duration measured against a moving threshold.] The persistence
        term is defined as hours above the damage-onset threshold, which is
        itself BRI-adjusted. A resilient building therefore accrues fewer hours
        as well as a lower severity, so the resilience credit compounds across
        the two terms. This is intentional but means the two effects cannot be
        attributed separately from the output alone.
  \item[WD-L5 --- Independence from flood damage.] Wind and flood damage to the
        same property in the same event are computed independently and, where
        combined downstream, may double-count damage to shared building
        elements.
  \item[WD-L6 --- Binary trigger discards near-misses.] A property whose peak
        wind falls one m/s short of its effective threshold contributes nothing
        to the PRS count, while one m/s above contributes fully. The spread is
        therefore discontinuous in wind at the threshold, by design, but this
        makes portfolio PRS sensitive to small shifts in the wind field near the
        threshold band.
  \item[WD-L7 --- Grade mid-points for commercial assets.] Commercial assets
        carry letter-grade envelopes rather than numeric sub-scores; using band
        mid-points understates within-grade dispersion.
\end{description}

\subsection{Remediation}
\begin{description}
  \item[WD-R1] Calibrate $a$, $v_{50}$ and the persistence pair against observed
        wind loss; precondition of sign-off.
  \item[WD-R2] Calibrate or justify $\delta_{\max}$, $\alpha_w$ and $\beta_c$
        against resilience-stratified loss experience (addresses WD-L1).
  \item[WD-R3] Make the averaging convention explicit in the interface, so a
        change upstream cannot silently invalidate the curve (WD-L2).
  \item[WD-R4] Define and document the treatment of combined wind-and-flood
        damage to a single property (WD-L5).
\end{description}

% 9. GOVERNANCE VALIDATION QUESTIONS

\mkmgovernance

\mkmvqitem{1}{Model purpose and use}{Converts per-property peak sustained wind
into a damage ratio for loss reporting, and into a binary damage-onset trigger
for PRS event counting. Used by the wind-impact route and by the PRS pricer's
wind leg.}

\mkmvqitem{2}{Conceptual soundness}{A logistic vulnerability curve is standard
practice for wind. The log-differential resilience shift mirrors the
already-reviewed flood stilt, and the saturating persistence factor is a
conventional duration-of-load treatment. The peak-only curves being the exact
$t \to \infty$ limit of the persistence-aware curves is a structural
consistency guarantee.}

\mkmvqitem{3}{Data quality}{Peak wind and duration come from the wind
time-series stage; thresholds and BRI scores from the CDM. Missing BRI scores
default to the neutral reference, and a missing threshold to a configured
default --- both are documented no-information defaults rather than failures,
which means data gaps degrade quietly. Coverage of the CDM wind-threshold field
should be monitored.}

\mkmvqitem{4}{Assumptions}{Sustained wind is the correct severity measure
(WD-L2); vulnerability is isotropic (WD-L3); damage accumulates with exposure
time on a 4-hour scale; wind and flood damage are independent (WD-L5).}

\mkmvqitem{5}{Implementation verification}{Unit tests cover all four curves, the
shift including its cap and log floor, the persistence limits, all four
threshold-resolution branches, the CDM navigation helpers, the extraction layer
and the event composer.}

\mkmvqitem{6}{Sensitivity and stability}{See Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}. The
model is monotone and materially sensitive along all three axes, with the
resilience credit concentrated in the 20--40~m/s band by design. Stability is
not a concern --- the model is a deterministic closed-form evaluation with no
sampling.}

\mkmvqitem{7}{Limitations and weaknesses}{Documented in Section~8
(WD-L1--WD-L7). WD-L1 is the most material: the resilience credit rests on an
uncalibrated cap comparable to the curve's own transition width.}

\mkmvqitem{8}{Ongoing monitoring}{Not yet in production. The plan under WD-R1
covers CDM threshold-field coverage, the distribution of realised triggers per
event, and a backtest of predicted against realised wind loss once data exists.}
