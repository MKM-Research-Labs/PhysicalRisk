% ===================================================================
% 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
% ===================================================================
\mkmexecsummary

This document describes the Stress Test Pipeline (MKM-SP-001), an end-to-end
orchestration system that generates spatially correlated multi-storm stress
scenarios and trains per-gauge flood classifiers for use in portfolio stress
testing and P\&L estimation.

The pipeline ingests 10{,}000 synthetically generated storm sequences (from
MKM-SS-001), parses the gauge portfolio with historical daily baselines,
builds a spatial correlation model across all gauges, computes compound
168-hour gauge responses with seasonal base-level adjustment, populates
per-gauge time series files (\texttt{gaugets/}), extracts the alert-breaching
subset (\texttt{stress\_storms.json}), and optionally trains a Gradient
Boosting Machine (GBM) flood classifier per gauge.

The pipeline is invoked via \texttt{phys.py port --stressm} and produces the
following principal outputs:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \texttt{storm\_sequences.json} --- 10{,}000 multi-storm sequences
    \item \texttt{sequences\_summary.json} --- lightweight metadata per sequence
    \item \texttt{sequence\_gauge\_summary.json} --- per-sequence per-gauge
          peaks and flood flags ($\sim$20--30\,MB)
    \item \texttt{gaugets/*.json} --- per-gauge base simulation + storm responses
    \item \texttt{stress\_storms.json} --- alert-breaching subset for UI consumption
    \item \texttt{data/output/stressm/*.joblib} --- GBM classifiers (optional)
    \item \texttt{data/output/stressm/training\_summary.json} --- classifier metrics
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Key design principle:} The pipeline replaces several previously
independent generation steps (storm generation, gauge time series, stress
storm extraction, classifier training) with a single orchestrated flow that
ensures consistency between the spatial correlation model, the compound
gauge responses, and the downstream classifier training data.

% 2. MODEL PURPOSE AND SCOPE

\mkmpurpose

\subsection{Purpose}
The Stress Test Pipeline serves as the central orchestrator for multi-storm
stress testing within the platform. It fulfils three functions:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Scenario generation:} Produce a large catalogue of spatially
          correlated, multi-storm gauge responses that capture compounding
          antecedent-wetness effects across the catchment.
    \item \textbf{Data population:} Generate the \texttt{gaugets/} directory and
          \texttt{stress\_storms.json} file consumed by downstream modules
          (property time series, hazard curves, stress test UI, trading desk).
    \item \textbf{Classifier training:} Train per-gauge GBM flood classifiers
          on the full 10{,}000-sequence corpus, producing models with
          $\sim$1{,}680{,}000 training vectors per gauge.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{In scope:} Storm sequence generation, spatial precipitation
          correlation, compound gauge response computation, seasonal baseline
          adjustment, gauge time series population, stress storm extraction,
          GBM classifier training.
    \item \textbf{Out of scope:} Property-level flood modelling, PRS pricing,
          depth-damage estimation, climate change projection.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model Chain Position}
\label{sec:model_chain}
The pipeline sits at the centre of the stress testing model chain:

\[
\underbrace{\text{MKM-SI-001}}_{\text{Storm Intensity}}
\to
\underbrace{\text{MKM-SS-001}}_{\text{Sequences}}
\to
\boxed{\textbf{MKM-SP-001}\ (\text{this pipeline})}
\to
\underbrace{\text{MKM-FC-001 v2.0}}_{\text{Classifiers}}
\to
\text{Stress P\&L}
\]

\noindent Internally, MKM-SP-001 invokes MKM-SS-001 for sequence generation and
delegates classifier training to MKM-FC-001 v2.0. The pipeline's primary
contribution is the spatial correlation model and the compound response
computation that connect these components.

\subsection{Stage 1: Storm Sequence Generation}

The pipeline delegates to \texttt{generate\_event\_set()} from the Storm
Sequence Generator (MKM-SS-001) to produce $N$ storm sequences (default
$N = 10{,}000$). Each sequence contains 1--5 individual storms within a
168-hour event window, with calibrated durations, inter-storm gaps, and
correlated intensity factors.

Output files:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \texttt{storm\_sequences.json} --- full sequence catalogue (schema 2.0-multi-storm)
    \item \texttt{sequences\_summary.json} --- lightweight metadata per sequence
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage 2: Gauge Portfolio Parsing}
\label{sec:gauge_parsing}

The gauge parser (\texttt{gauge\_parser.py}) loads the gauge portfolio from
\texttt{gauge.json} and enriches it with historical baselines from
\texttt{gaugehd/}.

\subsubsection{Gauge Extraction}
The \texttt{\_extract\_gauges()} function handles both schema variants of
\texttt{gauge.json}: a list under the \texttt{flood\_gauges} key, or a
dictionary keyed by gauge ID. Each record is normalised by
\texttt{\_parse\_gauge()} to extract:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llp{7cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Source} \\
\midrule
\texttt{gauge\_id} & string & \texttt{Header.GaugeID} or \texttt{gauge\_id} \\
\texttt{lat}, \texttt{lon} & float & \texttt{Location.GaugeLatitude/Longitude} \\
\texttt{base\_level} & float & gaugehd mean level, or $0.35 \times \text{flood\_alert}$ \\
\texttt{monthly\_means} & dict & gaugehd monthly means (keys \texttt{"01"}\ldots\texttt{"12"}) \\
\texttt{flood\_alert} & float & \texttt{FloodStages.FloodAlert} \\
\texttt{flood\_warning} & float & \texttt{FloodStages.FloodWarning}, or $1.33 \times \text{alert}$ \\
\texttt{severe\_warning} & float & \texttt{FloodStages.SevereFloodWarning}, or $1.58 \times \text{alert}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsubsection{Historical Baselines from gaugehd}

The function \texttt{\_load\_gaugehd\_baselines()} reads each
\texttt{gauge\_*\_hd.json} file from the \texttt{gaugehd/} directory and
extracts:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \texttt{mean\_level}: the annual mean daily water level (m), used as
          the default \texttt{base\_level}.
    \item \texttt{monthly\_means}: a dictionary mapping month strings
          (\texttt{"01"}\ldots\texttt{"12"}) to the mean daily water level for
          that month. This enables seasonal base-level adjustment (Section~\ref{sec:seasonal}).
\end{itemize}

\noindent When gaugehd data is unavailable, the parser falls back to the
heuristic $\text{base\_level} = 0.35 \times \text{flood\_alert}$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Spatial Correlation Model}
\label{sec:spatial}

The spatial model distributes catchment-average precipitation across gauges
using an exponential correlation kernel calibrated to the Thames catchment.

\subsubsection{Distance Matrix}

An $N \times N$ Haversine distance matrix $\mathbf{D}$ is computed from the
gauge coordinates:
\[
D_{ij} = R \cdot 2 \arctan2\!\left(\sqrt{a},\; \sqrt{1-a}\right)
\]
where $R = 6{,}371$\,km and
\[
a = \sin^2\!\left(\tfrac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right)
  + \cos\phi_i \cos\phi_j \sin^2\!\left(\tfrac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right)
\]

\subsubsection{Correlation Matrix}

The exponential correlation matrix $\mathbf{C}$ is:
\[
C_{ij} = \begin{cases}
1 & i = j \\
(1 - \nu) \exp\!\left(-D_{ij} / r\right) & i \ne j
\end{cases}
\]
where $\nu = 0.05$ is the nugget (micro-scale variability) and $r$ is the
intensity-conditioned effective range:
\[
r = r_0 \left(1 + \rho \cdot \max(0,\; f - 1)\right)
\]
with $r_0 = 40$\,km (base range), $\rho = 0.40$ (intensity scaling), and
$f$ the storm's intensity factor. Higher-intensity storms produce broader
spatial coherence, increasing the effective correlation range.

\subsubsection{Cholesky Decomposition}

The Cholesky factor $\mathbf{L}$ satisfying $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top = \mathbf{C}$
is computed and cached per distinct effective range (rounded to 0.1\,km).

\subsubsection{Spatially Correlated Multipliers}

For each hour with non-zero precipitation, correlated per-gauge multipliers
are sampled:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{z} &= \mathbf{L} \cdot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \quad
    \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_N)
    \label{eq:corr_normals} \\
M_i &= \exp\!\left(\sigma z_i - \tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)
    \label{eq:lognormal} \\
\tilde{M}_i &= M_i \,/\, \overline{M}
    \label{eq:normalize}
\end{align}
where $\sigma = 0.40$ is the lognormal spatial coefficient of variation.
Equation~\eqref{eq:lognormal} produces mean-1 lognormal variates, and
equation~\eqref{eq:normalize} normalises to preserve the catchment total:
$\overline{\tilde{M}} = 1$ exactly.

Per-gauge precipitation at hour $h$:
\[
P_i(h) = P_{\text{catch}}(h) \cdot \tilde{M}_i(h)
\]

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
\texttt{base\_range\_km} & 40.0 & Half of Thames corridor length \\
\texttt{nugget} & 0.05 & 5\% micro-scale variability \\
\texttt{rho\_intensity} & 0.40 & Range scaling per unit intensity above 1 \\
\texttt{sigma\_lognormal} & 0.40 & 40\% spatial coefficient of variation \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Stage 4: Compound Gauge Response}
\label{sec:compound}

For each of the $N$ sequences and each gauge, the pipeline computes a
continuous 168-hour river level hydrograph that captures the compounding
effect of multiple storms.

\subsubsection{Precipitation Profile}

Each storm within a sequence is assigned a triangular intensity profile.
The peak position is at $p \cdot d$ hours from the storm start, where $p$
is the \texttt{peak\_position} parameter and $d$ is the storm duration.
The total precipitation per storm is preserved exactly.

\subsubsection{Hydrological Routing}

The precipitation series is routed through:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item A soil moisture / groundwater hydrology model that tracks
          antecedent wetness between storms.
    \item A quickflow-to-level first-order lag filter with asymmetric
          rise/fall:
\end{enumerate}

\[
\Delta_{\text{excess}}(h) = \begin{cases}
\min\!\left(1,\; \alpha \cdot \beta\right) \cdot
    \left(Q(h) \cdot \gamma - e(h{-}1)\right)
    & Q(h) \cdot \gamma > e(h{-}1) \quad (\text{rising}) \\[6pt]
\alpha \cdot
    \left(Q(h) \cdot \gamma - e(h{-}1)\right)
    & \text{otherwise} \quad (\text{falling})
\end{cases}
\]

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item $e(h) = \max(0,\; e(h{-}1) + \Delta_{\text{excess}}(h))$ is the
          excess above base level (m)
    \item $Q(h)$ is the lagged quickflow (mm/h) at time $h - \tau_{\text{lag}}$
    \item $\gamma = 0.50$ is the quickflow-to-level scale (m per mm/h)
    \item $\alpha = 1/24$ is the hourly decay rate
    \item $\beta = 3.0$ is the rise factor (channels rise 3$\times$ faster than they recede)
\end{itemize}

\noindent The final river level at hour $h$ is:
\[
L_i(h) = b_i(m) + e_i(h)
\]
where $b_i(m)$ is the seasonally adjusted base level for gauge $i$ in month
$m$ (Section~\ref{sec:seasonal}).

\subsubsection{Seasonal Base-Level Adjustment}
\label{sec:seasonal}

When monthly means are available from gaugehd, the base level is adjusted
to reflect seasonal variation:

\[
b_i(m) = \begin{cases}
\mu_i^{(m)} & \text{if monthly\_means available} \\
\bar{\mu}_i & \text{otherwise (annual mean)}
\end{cases}
\]

\noindent where $\mu_i^{(m)}$ is the mean daily water level for gauge $i$ in
month $m$, and $\bar{\mu}_i$ is the annual mean. This ensures that winter
storms (higher baseline due to elevated groundwater) produce higher peak
levels than equivalent summer storms.

The function \texttt{\_seasonal\_base\_level(monthly\_means, mean\_level, month)}
implements this lookup, falling back to the annual mean when monthly data is
absent.

\subsubsection{Threshold Exceedance}

After computing peaks, each sequence is classified against the three flood
thresholds:
\[
\text{alert}_i = \mathbb{1}\!\left[\max_h L_i(h) \geq \text{flood\_alert}_i\right], \quad
\text{warning}_i = \mathbb{1}\!\left[\max_h L_i(h) \geq \text{flood\_warning}_i\right], \quad
\text{severe}_i = \mathbb{1}\!\left[\max_h L_i(h) \geq \text{severe\_warning}_i\right]
\]

\noindent The output file \texttt{sequence\_gauge\_summary.json} (schema 2.1-spatial)
records per-sequence:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \texttt{peaks\_m}: peak water level per gauge (m)
    \item \texttt{peak\_hours}: hour of peak per gauge
    \item \texttt{alert}, \texttt{warning}, \texttt{severe}: boolean flags per gauge
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage 5: Gaugets Population}

This stage generates the \texttt{gaugets/} directory consumed by all
downstream modules. It executes three sub-steps:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Base simulation:} Generate 168-hour flood simulation files
          using \texttt{GaugeTimeSeriesGenerator}.
    \item \textbf{Storm response merge:} Append per-gauge
          \texttt{storm\_responses} from the sequence records into each
          \texttt{gaugets/GAUGE-*.json} file.
    \item \textbf{Stress storm extraction:} Build \texttt{stress\_storms.json}
          containing only the alert-breaching subset, via
          \texttt{generate\_stress\_storms()}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 6: GBM Classifier Training (Optional)}

When \texttt{--train-classifier} is passed, the pipeline trains a
GBM flood classifier per gauge. This stage delegates to
\texttt{train\_gauge\_stressm\_classifier()} which:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item Re-computes the full 168-hour compound hydrograph per sequence
          (using the spatial model for gauge-specific precipitation).
    \item Extracts four log-transformed features per hour:
          $\ell(t) = \ln(w/s)$, $\tau(t) = \ln((t{+}1)/168)$,
          $\Delta\ell(t)$, $\Delta^2\ell(t)$.
    \item Labels each vector: $y=1$ if $\max_{t' \geq t} w(t') \geq s$.
    \item Delegates to \texttt{FloodClassifierTrainer.train\_gauge()} from
          MKM-FC-001 for the actual GBM fitting and evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent If no sequences breach the severe threshold for a given gauge, the
labelling falls back to the flood alert level. This ensures the classifier
is always trained with both positive and negative examples.

Total training corpus per gauge: $10{,}000 \times 168 = 1{,}680{,}000$ vectors.

% ===================================================================
% 7. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
% ===================================================================
\mkmsensitivity

The pipeline's output is sensitive to the following parameters:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Spatial correlation range} (\texttt{base\_range\_km}):
          Larger ranges produce more uniform precipitation across gauges,
          reducing spatial diversity in stress scenarios.
    \item \textbf{Lognormal sigma} (\texttt{sigma\_lognormal}):
          Higher values increase gauge-to-gauge precipitation variability.
          At $\sigma = 0$, all gauges receive identical precipitation.
    \item \textbf{Seasonal baselines}: Winter months (November--February) produce
          higher base levels from gaugehd data, systematically increasing
          peak levels and alert exceedance rates for winter-starting sequences.
    \item \textbf{Rise factor} ($\beta = 3.0$): Controls the asymmetry of
          hydrograph shape. Higher values produce sharper peaks.
    \item \textbf{Sequence count} ($N$): Affects the statistical stability of
          exceedance rates and classifier training quality.
\end{enumerate}

% ===================================================================
% 8. LIMITATIONS
% ===================================================================
\mkmlimitations

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Sequential processing:} Sequences are processed one at a time.
          For 10{,}000 sequences $\times$ 52 gauges, the compound response
          computation takes approximately 10--15 minutes on a single core.
          No parallelisation is currently implemented.
    \item \textbf{Stationary correlation:} The spatial correlation kernel uses a
          single exponential form with fixed parameters. Real precipitation
          fields exhibit anisotropy and non-stationary correlation structures.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic precipitation:} The triangular intensity profile is
          a simplification. Real storms exhibit irregular, multi-modal
          precipitation patterns.
    \item \textbf{No feedback:} River levels do not feed back into the
          precipitation or hydrology model. In reality, floodplain storage
          attenuates downstream peaks.
    \item \textbf{Heuristic thresholds:} When gaugehd data is absent, the
          $0.35 \times \text{alert}$, $1.33 \times \text{alert}$, and
          $1.58 \times \text{alert}$ ratios are uncalibrated assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Classifier re-computation:} The classifier training stage
          re-computes all compound hydrographs from scratch rather than
          reusing Stage~4 results, approximately doubling the computation
          time when \texttt{--train-classifier} is enabled.
    \item \textbf{Single-catchment calibration:} All default parameters
          (spatial correlation, hydraulic routing, heuristic thresholds)
          are calibrated for the Thames catchment and may not generalise.
\end{enumerate}
