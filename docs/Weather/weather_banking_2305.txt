23.5 Stress Testing as Operational Discipline
================================================================

SECTION PURPOSE
---------------
Show how stress testing operates as a daily discipline for the PRS desk, not
just a regulatory exercise. Cover pre-trade stress, portfolio stress, classifier
management, and scenario design.

PRE-TRADE STRESS
-----------------
Before committing a new PRS position, the trader stress-tests the proposed trade
against the historical storm catalogue:

  1. Select the gauge and trigger level of the proposed trade
  2. Run the 260 alert-breaching storm scenarios through the classifier
  3. For each scenario: compute stressed P(flood) at each hour of the 168-hour
     storm horizon
  4. CDS-in-stress valuation:
     cash_price = notional x P(flood_at_peak) x direction
     stress_pnl = cash_price - current_mtm
  5. Summary: worst-case scenario P&L, average stressed P&L, scenario distribution

This tells the trader: "if I take this position and the worst historical storm
hits, what is my maximum loss?" -- identical to how a CDS desk stress-tests a
new position against credit spread shock scenarios before execution.

DAILY PORTFOLIO STRESS
-----------------------
The full PRS book is stressed daily or weekly against the storm catalogue:

  For each storm scenario in the catalogue:
    1. Hydrograph synthesis: generate realistic flood profile from storm parameters
    2. For each gauge referenced in the portfolio:
       - GBM classifier produces P(flood) time series over 168 hours
       - Peak P(flood) used for CDS-in-stress revaluation
    3. Portfolio-level aggregation:
       - Sum stressed P&L across all positions
       - Account for spatial correlation between gauges
       - Identify which scenarios produce the worst portfolio outcomes
    4. Compare against risk limits

  Output: stress P&L waterfall showing contribution by position and scenario,
  summary statistics (mean, median, 95th percentile, worst case).

GBM CLASSIFIER MANAGEMENT
---------------------------
The flood classifiers are the core stress testing models. The PoC provides
a complete management interface:

  Training:
  - Per-gauge GradientBoostingClassifier
  - 4 log-transformed features from gauge timeseries data
  - 1,000 training storms per gauge (260 alert + 740 sub-alert)
  - Stratified 80/20 train/test split, random_state=42 for reproducibility
  - Metrics: accuracy, AUC-ROC, Brier score, log-loss, feature importance

  Monitoring:
  - Classifier readiness status per gauge (trained / untrained / stale)
  - Aggregate training readiness across portfolio
  - Feature importance tracking for model drift detection

  Retraining:
  - Triggered when new flood data arrives (post-event recalibration)
  - Train individual gauges or bulk-train all gauges
  - Training progress tracking for bulk operations
  - Clear-all option for fresh start

  Production Governance:
  - Model saved as .joblib with training_summary.json metadata
  - Severe_level stored per gauge for feature normalisation
  - Falls back to severe_level=1.0 if metadata missing
  - All training decisions auditable through governance interface

PROBABILITY SURFACES
---------------------
The classifier produces 2D probability grids:
  F(water_level, hour) -> P(flood)

This surface shows the trader:
- At what water level does P(flood) become significant?
- How quickly does probability ramp up as the storm progresses?
- Where is the transition from "manageable" to "severe"?

These surfaces are the PRS equivalent of credit migration probability matrices
-- they show how the underlying risk evolves over time under different conditions.

STORM SCENARIO DESIGN
-----------------------
The PoC's storm catalogue contains:
- 260 storms that breached alert level at one or more gauges
- 740 sub-alert storms providing balanced training data
- Each storm: per-gauge water level time series over 168 hours (7 days)

Scenarios can be extended with:
- New historical events: as floods occur, add to catalogue
- Synthetic scenarios: Gumbel-adjusted tail exploration
- Compound scenarios: sequential storms testing cumulative portfolio degradation
- Climate-conditioned scenarios: adjusted return periods (with governance caveats
  from Chapter 18 about historical vs climate-projected methodologies)

SEQUENTIAL STORM ANALYSIS
---------------------------
The PoC supports multi-storm sequences that test cumulative portfolio degradation:
- Storm 1 hits: damage, LTV adjustment, PD increase
- Storm 2 hits (weeks later): additional damage on already-weakened portfolio
- Properties not yet recovered face amplified impact
- Insurance gaps widen as claims accumulate
- Portfolio capital position deteriorates non-linearly

This sequential analysis captures what single-event stress testing misses:
the compounding effect of repeated physical events on a mortgage book that
has not recovered from the prior event. It is analogous to multi-default
stress testing in CDS portfolios, where sequential defaults in correlated
names create cascade losses.

INTEGRATION WITH RISK LIMITS
------------------------------
Stress testing results feed directly into risk management:
- Maximum FS01 per gauge: prevent concentration at a single trigger point
- Maximum portfolio stress loss: cap worst-case scenario P&L
- Minimum diversification ratio: require exposure across multiple gauges
- Maximum sequential storm impact: limit cumulative degradation exposure

These limits mirror CDS risk limits (single-name concentration, sector limits,
portfolio VaR limits) and are managed through the same risk governance framework.
