23.6 Portfolio and Mortgage Book Interaction
================================================================

SECTION PURPOSE
---------------
Show the dual perspective: PRS as a trading product AND as a hedge for the
mortgage book's physical risk exposure. Detail the linkage from gauge events
through property damage to mortgage portfolio impact, and how PRS payouts
offset the capital hit.

THE DUAL PERSPECTIVE
---------------------
A bank trading PRS holds two related but distinct views:

  TRADING DESK VIEW:
  - PRS is a derivative position with mark-to-market, Greeks, and P&L
  - Risk is measured in FS01 (flood spread sensitivity)
  - Portfolio management is by gauge, trigger, and tenor
  - Profit comes from bid-offer, curve positioning, and flow intermediation

  MORTGAGE BOOK VIEW:
  - PRS is protection against physical risk to mortgage collateral
  - Risk is measured in stressed LGD and portfolio ECL
  - Exposure is by property, catchment, and flood zone
  - Value comes from capital relief and loss reduction under stress

The PoC connects these views through the CDM data model: Property CDM links
to Mortgage CDM via PropertyID/UPRN, and both link to Flood Gauge CDM via
geographic proximity. PhysicalSwap CDM references the same gauges.

PROPERTY FLOOD RESPONSE
-------------------------
The property flood response model (MKM-PF-001) links gauge behaviour to
property-level impact:

  Gauge reading -> flood depth at property (via spatial model):
  - KDTree identifies nearest gauge(s) to each property
  - IDW interpolation estimates property-level flood depth from gauge readings
  - Elevation difference between gauge and property modifies depth estimate
  - EA flood zone classification (Zone 1/2/3/3a/3b) provides prior probability

  Flood depth -> property damage (via depth-damage functions):
  - Input: estimated flood depth at ground floor level
  - Output: damage ratio (0-1) for structural and contents
  - Functions vary by property type (residential, commercial, industrial)
  - Resilience measures (flood doors, raised electrics) reduce damage ratio

MORTGAGE IMPACT CASCADE
-------------------------
When a flood event occurs, the mortgage impact unfolds:

  1. PROPERTY VALUE ADJUSTMENT
     - pre_flood_value: from Property CDM valuation section
     - structural_damage = depth_damage_ratio x pre_flood_value
     - contents_damage = contents_ratio x contents_value
     - post_flood_value = pre_flood_value - structural_damage
     - Market stigma: additional 10-50% discount for 4-7 years (Belanger &
       Bourdeau-Brien, Chapter 8.1)

  2. LTV RECALCULATION
     - pre_flood_LTV = outstanding_balance / pre_flood_value
     - post_flood_LTV = outstanding_balance / post_flood_value
     - If post_flood_LTV > 100%: property in negative equity
     - Negative equity drives strategic default risk ("keys on the table")

  3. AFFORDABILITY IMPACT
     - Insurance premium increase (if insurer doesn't withdraw entirely)
     - Repair costs affecting household cash flow
     - Potential income disruption if property is business-related
     - Updated affordability ratio feeds the capital markets approach (Ch 11):
       higher ratio -> steeper credit spread -> higher hazard rate

  4. DEFAULT PROBABILITY ADJUSTMENT
     - Via the CDS-style affordability framework from Chapter 11
     - Piecewise: linear at low ratios, steepening 15-30%, exponential above 30%
     - Flood events push borrowers into the steepening or exponential zone

  5. LOSS GIVEN DEFAULT ADJUSTMENT (Frye-Jacobs)
     - Chapter 17's central result: physical events create compounding PD-LGD
     - cLGD = Phi((Phi^-1(EL/PD) - sqrt(rho) x Phi^-1(cDR)) / sqrt(1-rho))
     - Worked example: PD rises from 1.5% to 8% under flood scenario:
       baseline LGD 25% -> conditional LGD 43% (72% relative increase)
       ECL: 2.0% -> 3.4% of exposure (nearly doubles)
     - LGD adjustment contributes 30-50% of incremental scenario losses

  6. PORTFOLIO AGGREGATION
     - Sum ECL adjustments across all affected loans
     - VaR and Expected Shortfall from Monte Carlo (1,000 paths)
     - Spatial correlation: properties within 1000m are strongly correlated
     - 95% VaR for concentrated portfolio: 2-3x higher than diversified

PRS AS HEDGE: THE CAPITAL RELIEF CALCULATION
----------------------------------------------
When PRS protection is in place, the payout offsets collateral impairment:

  WITHOUT PRS:
  - Flood event -> collateral impairment -> LTV breach -> PD increase
  - Frye-Jacobs cLGD amplifies the impact
  - ECL increases -> capital increases -> CET1 ratio deteriorates

  WITH PRS:
  - Flood event -> PRS trigger -> T+2 payout to protection buyer (the bank)
  - Payout offsets collateral impairment:
    adjusted_LGD = max(0, base_LGD - PRS_payout / EAD)
  - Reduced LGD -> reduced ECL -> reduced capital requirement
  - Capital relief = delta(credit risk capital) - [counterparty + CVA + market
    risk capital on PRS position] (Chapter 16 formula)

  The PRS payout arrives before the Frye-Jacobs PD-LGD correlation fully
  materialises, providing recovery certainty at the point of physical event --
  analogous to how CDS provides recovery certainty at credit event.

CLAIMS REPORTING
-----------------
The PoC generates automated flood damage claim reports per property:
- Property identification (from Property CDM: UPRN, address, type)
- Estimated flood depth and source gauge
- Damage ratio and loss estimate (structural + contents)
- Mortgage details (from Mortgage CDM: outstanding balance, current LTV)
- Post-flood LTV and negative equity assessment
- Recovery timeline estimate
- PRS coverage status and payout amount if applicable

SEQUENTIAL STORM ANALYSIS
---------------------------
The PoC supports analysis of cumulative portfolio degradation from multiple
storm events:
- First storm: initial damage, LTV adjustment, PD increase
- Interval: partial recovery, repair progress, insurance claims processing
- Second storm: additional damage on weakened portfolio
- Cumulative: properties not yet recovered face amplified damage ratios,
  insurance gaps widen, portfolio capital position deteriorates non-linearly

This captures the mortgage book reality that single-event stress misses:
physical risk is not a one-off shock but a recurring threat where recovery
timelines matter as much as event severity.
