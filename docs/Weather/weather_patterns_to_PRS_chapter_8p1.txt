Chapter 8.1: From Hazard to Damage (pp. 273-293)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
Physical flood hazard becomes useful for banking only when translated through a
transparent, auditable chain into asset-level damage distributions and portfolio-level
loss tails. The chapter builds this chain: hazard curves describing frequency and
intensity, vulnerability/damage functions converting intensities into loss distributions,
and spatial clustering dynamics turning independent asset risks into correlated portfolio
losses with fat tails.

Hazard curves are positioned as the natural analogue of hazard rates in a CDS framework,
explicitly stated as feeding into expected loss and tail loss calculations in
mathematically familiar form.

KEY ARGUMENTS
-------------
1. Hazard curves are the interface between flood models and banking risk engines
   - Exceedance probability: P(X^h_t >= 1) = 1 - e^(-t/tau)
   - Translate "1-in-X-year" into continuous probability distributions
   - OS-Climate/FINOS framework constructs from WRI Aqueduct and regional datasets

2. Vulnerability curves complete the hazard-to-damage translation
   - Conditional probability f_{D|S}(d,s) maps intensity to damage distribution
   - Effective impact: f_D(d) = integral of f_S(s) * f_{D|S}(d,s) ds
   - Approaches: expert-derived, statistical (Random Forest), parametric (Beta)

3. Uncertainty is structured, not monolithic
   - Aleatory: irreducible variability (two identical buildings, same depth, different damage)
   - Epistemic: reducible through data (building characteristics, calibration gaps)

4. Property clustering creates correlated, fat-tailed portfolio losses
   - Properties within ~1000m share strongly correlated impacts
   - Shared infrastructure creates systemic vulnerability amplification
   - Market contagion: 10-50% post-flood discounts, 4-7 year recovery
   - 95% VaR for concentrated portfolio can be 2-3x higher than diversified

5. Credit impact metrics are the entry points for physical risk into banking
   - PD: income disruption, expense increase -> higher defaults
   - LGD: collateral damage, insurance withdrawal -> lower recoveries
   - EAD: drawdown of undrawn lines -> higher exposure at default
   - RWA/CET1: systematic shifts in PD/LGD/EAD -> higher capital

KEY MODELS AND FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------
- Hazard curve: P(X^h_t >= 1) = 1 - e^(-t/tau)
- Vulnerability curve: f_{D|S}(d,s) conditional probability density
- Beta distribution for parametric vulnerability (OS-Climate)
- OS-Climate/FINOS physrisk engine; WRI Aqueduct
- Basel III: PD, LGD, EAD, RWA, CET1; IFRS 9 provisioning

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
Strongest direct support so far. Hazard curves are explicitly described as analogous to
CDS hazard rates. Exceedance probability formulas mirror default probability term
structures. The chain hazard -> vulnerability -> damage -> portfolio loss parallels
reference entity -> recovery rate -> loss distribution -> portfolio CDS pricing.

Complication: spatial correlation and clustering effects (~1000m correlation distance)
introduce a geographic dimension that standard CDS models don't handle.
