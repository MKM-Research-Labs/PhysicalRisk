Chapter 18: Forward or Backwards? (pp. 463-475)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
Catastrophe insurance modelling should prioritise OASIS-type approaches -- historical
weather event catalogues combined with synthetic scenario generation and near-term weather
forecasting -- over climate model integration. Weather prediction and climate projection
are fundamentally different scientific endeavours (initial-value vs boundary-value problems)
and must not be conflated.

The chapter attacks IPCC scenario construction (SSPs/RCPs) as socio-economic narratives
disconnected from climate physics, arguing the chain from SSP storyline through emissions
to temperature to circulation to precipitation compounds uncertainties at every link.

KEY ARGUMENTS
-------------
1. The temporal disconnect is fundamental, not computational
   - Weather = initial-value problem (7-14 days, validated against observations)
   - Climate = boundary-value problem (decades-centuries, cannot be validated until arrival)
   - The S2S "predictability desert" reflects physical limits, not computational ones

2. Cloud feedback is irreducible uncertainty
   - Largest source of inter-model spread in climate sensitivity
   - Models disagree on sign of key cloud feedbacks
   - Only ~4 decades of satellite observations -- too short for trend detection

3. Historical-synthetic approaches are superior for pricing
   - Grounded in verified observations with documented loss impacts
   - Monte Carlo/copula/EVT extensions introduce quantifiable statistical uncertainty
   - Computationally tractable (10K-100K events, GPU-accelerated, minutes not hours)
   - Backtesting, spatial correlation verification accessible validation

4. IPCC scenarios are unsuitable for insurance pricing
   - SSPs are stakeholder-constructed narratives, not physics-constrained projections
   - Historical scenario performance has been poor

5. Near-term weather has a proper but limited role
   - Dynamic risk management: temporary defences, early warnings, claims reserves
   - Must NOT extend to fundamental pricing adjustments

KEY MODELS AND FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------
- OASIS-type historical-synthetic catastrophe modelling
- Monte Carlo with copula-based dependence; EVT for tail extrapolation
- Clausius-Clapeyron thermodynamic relationship
- IPCC SSP/RCP framework (critiqued)
- Change-point detection and trend analysis
- Backtesting validation framework

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
Strengthens the analogy by constraining PRS to observable events. Just as CDS avoids
pricing off corporate earnings models and uses market spreads, PRS should use observed
hazard data, not climate projections. This mirrors how CDS credit events are legal/factual
determinations based on observable occurrences.
