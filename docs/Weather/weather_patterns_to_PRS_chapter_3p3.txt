Chapter 3.3: Weather - Sampling and Dependence (pp. 117-125)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
Naive Monte Carlo in a million-dimensional atmospheric state space is both
computationally prohibitive and meteorologically unrealistic. This chapter solves the
problem through three techniques: (a) quasi-Monte Carlo sampling using Sobol sequences
for more uniform coverage, (b) dimension reduction via Empirical Orthogonal Functions
(EOFs) to concentrate sampling on the 50-200 atmospheric patterns capturing 90%+ of
variance, and (c) copula-based dependence structures (especially vine copulas) to
ensure that when one variable signals storm conditions, correlated variables behave
consistently. A Hidden Markov Model over weather regimes adds temporal persistence
and regime-dependent dependence.

KEY MATHEMATICAL MODELS
-----------------------
1. Sobol Quasi-Monte Carlo Sequences
   - Low-discrepancy points in [0,1]^d
   - Convergence rate O(N^{-1}) vs O(N^{-1/2}) for pseudo-random
   - Dramatically more efficient exploration of high-dimensional uncertainty space

2. Inverse CDF Mapping
   - X = F^{-1}(U) for marginals
   - For multivariate normal: X_0 = mu_0 + L . Phi^{-1}(U), L = Cholesky(Sigma_0)

3. EOF Dimension Reduction
   - X_0 = mu_0 + sum_{i=1}^{K} alpha_i e_i
   - e_i are empirical orthogonal functions (principal modes of atmospheric variability)
   - K ~ 100-200 retained modes capture 90%+ of variance
   - alpha_i coefficients sampled via Sobol sequences
   - Reduces effective dimensionality from millions to hundreds

4. Copula Framework
   - Sklar's theorem: separate marginals from dependence structure
   - Copula families: Gaussian (symmetric), Student-t (symmetric, heavier tails),
     Gumbel (upper tail dependence), Clayton (lower tail dependence)

5. Vine Copulas
   - Hierarchical decomposition of d-dimensional copula into bivariate building blocks
   - Tree 1: direct pairwise relationships (T-p, T-q, u-v)
   - Tree 2: conditional dependencies (q-P|T, w-P|q)
   - Tree 3+: higher-order conditional dependencies
   - Sampling: Sobol uniforms -> vine transform -> correlated uniforms -> inverse
     marginal CDFs -> physical variables
   - Enables realistic multivariate tail dependence without assuming Gaussian copula

6. Hidden Markov Model for Weather Regimes
   - Transition matrix A_0 = [a_{ij}] where a_{ij} = P(regime j at t+1 | regime i at t)
   - Regimes identified from historical clustering: dry continental, moist maritime,
     frontal, convective
   - Copula parameters and SDE drift/diffusion are regime-dependent
   - Seasonal variation in transition probabilities
   - Adds temporal persistence: weather regimes tend to persist for days

FORWARD REFERENCES
------------------
- Initial ensemble D_0 feeds into Ch 3.4's Bayesian update cycle
- HMM regime structure carries through to Ch 3.5's precipitation mapping (different
  regimes produce different rainfall characteristics)
- Vine copula structures ensure updated ensemble members maintain physically coherent
  multivariate relationships

WHAT THIS CHAPTER DEMANDS OF CHAPTER 22
-----------------------------------------
1. Governance of copula family selection and recalibration: wrong copula for a critical
   pair (e.g., T-q under convective regime) biases tail dependence and flood
   probabilities

2. HMM regime definitions must be validated and the transition matrix updated.
   How is model risk around regime classification managed?

3. EOF basis functions require periodic recomputation as climate evolves. Who owns
   this recalibration cycle?

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
Copulas are VERY familiar to structured credit desks -- they were central (and
infamous) in CDO pricing. The vine copula machinery here is more sophisticated than
the Gaussian copula models that caused problems in 2008.

Banks have hard-won experience with copula model risk from the credit crisis, which
could be an institutional advantage in deploying this framework -- or a source of
institutional skepticism.

The HMM regime structure maps reasonably well to credit cycle regime models already
used in some banks' ECL frameworks.
