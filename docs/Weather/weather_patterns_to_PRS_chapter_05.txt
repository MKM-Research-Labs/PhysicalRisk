Chapter 5: Hydrological Modelling (pp. 165-199)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
Hydrological modelling is the critical bridge between weather events and flood risk --
it translates meteorological data into actionable insights about how much water arrives
and when. All models derive from the same Newtonian physical laws (mass and energy
conservation). The intellectual property does not reside in the models themselves (public-
domain academic creations) but in their implementation -- analogous to how banks treat
instrument pricing models.

The chapter establishes the complete precipitation-to-flood chain: precipitation occurrence
and intensity determine effective rainfall, which determines runoff partitioning and
hydrograph shape, which determines flood metrics (peak flow, volume, duration above
threshold). Each link introduces uncertainty that propagates forward.

KEY ARGUMENTS
-------------
1. Water balance P = Q + ET + dS is the master constraint
   - ET accounts for 60-90% of precipitation in many watersheds
   - Penman-Monteith is the FAO standard for reference ET

2. Infiltration is the "gatekeeper" converting rainfall to excess rainfall
   - Green-Ampt (physically based) and SCS Curve Number (empirical, CN 30-100)
   - Small changes in storm intensity can push a catchment across the threshold
     where runoff dominates, producing disproportionate peak discharge

3. Hidden Markov Models represent a paradigm shift in precipitation modelling
   - 3-6 hidden atmospheric states drive observable precipitation
   - Viterbi algorithm decodes state sequences; Baum-Welch estimates parameters
   - NHMMs enable downscaling of GCM output to station scale

4. GLMs provide two-stage framework: occurrence (logistic) + intensity (gamma)
   - Separation reflects physics: mechanisms governing whether rain occurs differ
     from those controlling intensity

5. Non-stationarity under climate change invalidates classical flood frequency
   - Clausius-Clapeyron scaling (~7% moisture increase per degree C)
   - Historical "T-year" floods may correspond to shorter return periods
   - Backtesting must be paired with stress-testing beyond observed range

6. Data-driven approaches (LSTM, CNN) offer promise but create model governance
   challenges -- the "black box" problem addressed in Ch 19

KEY MODELS AND FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------
- Water balance: P = Q + ET + dS
- Penman-Monteith, Hargreaves, Priestley-Taylor equations
- Green-Ampt infiltration; SCS Curve Number
- Hymod (5 parameters), SWAT (semi-distributed), HEC-HMS, TOPMODEL
- Unit Hydrograph theory, SCS Dimensionless UH, Snyder's Method
- Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency, Kling-Gupta Efficiency, GLUE
- Hidden Markov Models, GLMs for precipitation
- Non-stationary GEV for flood frequency under climate change

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
Reinforces the analogy through implementation-over-model-ownership argument: just as
CDS uses publicly known credit models with bank-specific calibration, PRS uses publicly
available hydrological models with proprietary calibration. The precipitation-to-flood
chain has far more links than the credit chain, multiplying uncertainty. PRS needs an
equivalent market-observable anchor to collapse multi-model uncertainty into a single price.
