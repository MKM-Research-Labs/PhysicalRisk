Chapter 3.2: Weather - Stochastic Evolution (pp. 110-116)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
Given the physics-informed prior from 3.1, this chapter specifies how atmospheric
states evolve forward in time using a stochastic differential equation (SDE). The
drift term encodes deterministic atmospheric physics; the diffusion term captures
irreducible uncertainty from initial condition errors, model approximations, and
chaotic amplification. Numerical integration via the Euler-Maruyama scheme, repeated
with different initial conditions and random perturbations, generates the ensemble
of weather trajectories -- the "cloud of possibilities."

KEY MATHEMATICAL MODELS
-----------------------
1. Core SDE
   - dX(t) = a(X(t), t) dt + b(X(t), t) dW(t)
   - This is the master equation: deterministic physics plus stochastic uncertainty

2. Drift Term a(X,t) -- The Deterministic Physics
   - Thermal dynamics: dT/dt = -v.grad(T) - w(dT/dz) + Q/c_p
   - Pressure gradient forces
   - Moisture transport
   - Terrain interactions (orographic forcing)
   - Combined advection-diffusion: dC/dt = -v.grad(C) + D nabla^2 C
   - Peclet number Pe = vL/D governs relative importance of advection vs diffusion

3. Diffusion Term b(X,t) dW(t) -- The Uncertainty
   - W(t) is a Wiener process: E[dW]=0, Var[dW]=dt, independent increments
   - b(X,t) scales and correlates random fluctuations
   - Grows with forecast lead time (reflecting chaotic error amplification)

4. Euler-Maruyama Discretization
   - X_{n+1} = X_n + a(X_n, t_n) Delta_t + b(X_n, t_n) Delta_W_n
   - Delta_W_n ~ N(0, Delta_t . I)
   - Milstein's method mentioned for strongly nonlinear regimes

5. Ensemble Generation
   - Repeated SDE integration with different initial states and random perturbations
   - Each trajectory is one member of the ensemble
   - Ensemble spread represents forecast uncertainty

FORWARD REFERENCES
------------------
- The SDE provides temporal propagation between Bayesian update cycles in Ch 3.4
  (the "propagate-update-propagate" loop)
- The ensemble generated here is the raw material that Ch 3.3 samples efficiently
  and Ch 3.5 converts into catchment-scale rainfall

WHAT THIS CHAPTER DEMANDS OF CHAPTER 22
-----------------------------------------
1. The SDE is continuous-time; banking systems operate in discrete time (daily P&L,
   intraday risk reports, regulatory snapshots). Chapter 22 must specify how the
   continuous ensemble evolution maps to the bank's operational time grid.

2. Model risk: drift and diffusion parameterizations embed meteorological assumptions
   requiring validation and governance. Who owns the SDE parameters? How often are
   they recalibrated?

3. What happens when the diffusion term systematically under-predicts ensemble spread
   (leading to overconfident risk estimates)?

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
STRONGEST POINT OF ALIGNMENT: The SDE ensemble generation is computationally
analogous to Monte Carlo VaR engines that banks already run for market risk. The
concept of running thousands of scenarios forward in time is standard practice on
derivatives desks.

The complication: atmospheric SDEs are much higher-dimensional than typical
market-risk factor models, and the drift term requires domain-specific meteorological
knowledge rather than calibration to market prices.
