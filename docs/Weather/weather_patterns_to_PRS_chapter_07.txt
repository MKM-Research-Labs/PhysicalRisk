Chapter 7: Flood Risk Assessment (pp. 238-272)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
Flood risk assessment is the analytical layer bridging raw hydrological modelling and
financial decision-making. Flood risk is the product of hazard probability and
vulnerability/consequence, decomposed into four stacked layers -- hazard, exposure,
vulnerability, and decision/pricing -- before it becomes useful for banking.

The chapter makes an explicit case that flood risk assessment only becomes "decision-useful"
when mapped to banking levers: loan origination, collateral valuation, portfolio
concentration limits, and PRS hedge design. The chain hazard -> property risk score ->
loan term <-> PRS hedge is stated directly as the organising logic.

KEY ARGUMENTS
-------------
1. Flood classification must go beyond source-based categories
   - Multi-parameter, compound-event, and infrastructure-failure classifications needed
   - Compound events (e.g., Hurricane Harvey) create amplified impacts

2. Regulatory frameworks create standardised hazard layers
   - EU Floods Directive, US NFIP, UK Flood Re, Australian ARR Guidelines
   - UK Hazard Rating: HR = d x (v + 0.5) + DF

3. Property positioning analysis translates hazard into site-specific exposure
   - Five-step workflow producing a 0-100 exposure index
   - Plugs directly into loss models, pricing tools, and portfolio dashboards

4. Environmental resilience factors (wetlands, forests, soil) modify depth-damage curves
   - Nature-based solutions can link to PRS coupons (green bond KPI analogy)

5. Property-specific resilience scoring completes the chain
   - Structural vulnerability and adaptation measures are scorable
   - Flood Performance Certificates close the information gap for risk-based pricing

6. From assessment to transaction: analytical layers feed into portfolio loss curves,
   then PRS trigger metrics and attachment points

KEY MODELS AND FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------
- UK Hazard Rating: HR = d x (v + 0.5) + DF
- EU Floods Directive (2007/60/EC), US NFIP, UK Flood Re
- Exposure index (0-100) from five-step positioning workflow
- Multi-criteria resilience scoring and Flood Performance Certificates

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
Hazard -> exposure -> vulnerability -> pricing parallels credit event -> recovery rate ->
spread calculation. The 0-100 exposure index is analogous to a credit score feeding a
CDS spread model. Compound-event complexity goes beyond single-name CDS, and Ch 22
must reconcile this with simpler trigger mechanics.
