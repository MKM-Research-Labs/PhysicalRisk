Chapter 6: Hydraulic Modelling (pp. 200-237)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
Hydraulic modelling is where the pipeline delivers its critical banking output: flood
depth at specific locations. While hydrological modelling shows how much water arrives
and when, hydraulic modelling reveals how it flows through channels, spills across
floodplains, and exerts force on property. The Saint-Venant equations (1871) provide
the mathematical foundation; HEC-RAS is the global industry-standard implementation.

Coupled 1D-2D models have emerged as the preferred approach, reducing simulation times
by 30-50% vs full 2D while improving flood extent accuracy by 15-25% over standalone 1D.
Subgrid bathymetry enables basin-scale modelling while resolving meter-scale features.

KEY ARGUMENTS
-------------
1. Three approaches form the modelling hierarchy
   - Hydrological modelling: entire water cycle, rainfall to runoff
   - Hydrologic routing: flood wave propagation via continuity equation only
     (Muskingum, Modified Puls); sacrifices flow mechanics for efficiency
   - Hydraulic modelling: full Saint-Venant equations (mass + momentum)

2. The Muskingum method (1938) remains a workhorse
   - S = K[xI + (1-x)O]; Muskingum-Cunge transformed it from empirical to
     physically based (approximates diffusion wave equation)

3. Saint-Venant equations (1871) are the mathematical foundation
   - Continuity + momentum; hydrostatic pressure assumption
   - CFL condition (Cr = (|V|+c)*dt/dx <= 1) governs stability

4. Coupled 1D-2D is now the preferred standard
   - 1D for efficient channel routing, 2D for floodplain dynamics
   - FEMA guidelines specifically recommend coupled approaches

5. HEC-RAS is the global standard
   - Subgrid bathymetry: 500m cells preserving meter-scale features (10,000x reduction)
   - GPU acceleration: 100x speedups
   - Outputs: inundation depth/extent, velocity, water surface profiles, arrival times,
     shear stress, stream power

6. Model quality depends heavily on practitioner experience and judgment

KEY MODELS AND FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------
- Saint-Venant equations (1D and 2D shallow water form)
- Muskingum and Muskingum-Cunge routing
- Modified Puls (level pool routing)
- CFL condition; Manning's roughness coefficient
- Nash Cascade; Convolution integral
- SCS Dimensionless UH, Snyder's Method, GUH
- HEC-RAS (1D, 2D, coupled 1D-2D, subgrid bathymetry)

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
HEC-RAS depth categories (0-1m, 1-2m, 2m+) parallel CDS credit event types with
defined payout mechanics. The Muskingum-Cunge breakthrough (empirical to physically
grounded) parallels what PRS needs: transforming bespoke flood assessment into
standardised pricing using observable parameters.

Complication: HEC-RAS runs are computationally intensive. CDS uses closed-form
solutions. PRS needs either pre-computed hazard surfaces (analogous to vol surfaces
in options) or reduced-order surrogate models.
