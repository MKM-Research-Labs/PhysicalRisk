Chapter 16: Banking Supervisory Capital (pp. 417-436)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
The Basel three-pillar framework provides the regulatory architecture for PRS, but the
progression from Pillar 3 disclosure to Pillar 2 capital charges has stalled due to
fundamental methodological incompatibilities (1-3 year stress horizons vs multi-decade
physical risks). This impasse creates the opportunity for market-based solutions: PRS
producing short-term quantifiable risk metrics compatible with existing Pillar 1 frameworks.

PRS are positioned not as a new risk category but as derivatives whose cashflows transmit
physical risk through existing credit, market, counterparty, and liquidity risk channels --
aligned with BCBS's view that climate risks are drivers of existing categories.

KEY ARGUMENTS
-------------
1. Pillar 3 is operational but Pillar 2 has stalled
   - EBA ITS made disclosure mandatory; Basel Committee June 2025 became voluntary
   - Fewer than half include scenario analysis in Pillar 2 processes

2. BCBS 530: 18 principles that PRS satisfies structurally
   - Climate risks are "risk drivers that materialise through traditional categories"

3. PRS capital treatment maps to existing CDS capital logic
   - Pillar 1 credit: lower LGD/EL on hedged mortgage book (IRB)
   - Pillar 1 counterparty/CVA: same as CDS
   - Pillar 1 market: hazard-rate shocks analogous to spread shocks
   - Capital relief if credit risk reduction > counterparty + CVA + market risk capital

4. Pillar 2 requires supervisory conditions
   - Clear gauge-to-portfolio loss mapping
   - PRS payouts captured in capital projections and stress tests
   - Robust ISDA documentation, trigger verification

5. IFRS 9 hedge accounting creates practical challenges
   - Timing mismatch: continuous MTM vs infrequent trigger payouts
   - Basis risk: gauge triggers vs property-level damage
   - Designation: fair value hedge vs cash flow hedge

KEY MODELS AND FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------
- Basel III three-pillar framework; BCBS 530 (18 principles)
- PRS capital formula: relief = delta(credit) - [counterparty + CVA + market]
- IFRS 9 hedge effectiveness testing
- EBA ITS disclosure templates (10 templates)
- Three lines of defence; SR 11-7

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
The regulatory validation of the CDS analogy. Capital treatment explicitly mapped:
counterparty and CVA charges "in exactly the same way as a CDS." Market risk uses
"hazard-rate shocks analogous to spread shocks." Capital relief logic "mirrors early CDS
capital trades." Key complication: basis risk between gauge readings and portfolio losses
(CDS references a specific entity; PRS references a gauge that may not perfectly
correlate with the hedged exposure).

INTEGRATION COST SIGNALS
-------------------------

three lines of defence, Pillar 3 templates -- all exist for CDS
Novel/new-build: Gauge-to-portfolio loss mapping, IFRS 9 hedge methodology for physical
triggers, climate Pillar 2 buffer sizing, regulatory acceptance pathway (no precedent)
