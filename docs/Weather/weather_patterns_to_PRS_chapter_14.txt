Chapter 14: Extreme Flood Events (pp. 400-408)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
The Gumbel distribution (Type I extreme value) provides the mathematically rigorous
foundation for modelling flood extremes and bridging hydrological science to PRS pricing.
Classical insurance uses a one-year horizon, but PRS spans multiple years, requiring
understanding of how extreme-event probabilities accumulate -- and why naive exponential
models systematically underestimate that accumulation.

A "Gumbel-adjusted" multi-year formula produces 75-120% higher multi-year exceedance
probabilities than simple Poisson approaches, validated against 50-year Thames records.

KEY ARGUMENTS
-------------
1. Gumbel is the industry standard for flood frequency analysis
   - Heavy right tail, fixed positive skewness (~1.14), two parameters (mu, beta)
   - 1-in-100-year flood level ~ mu + 1.527*beta

2. Return period interpretation connects hydrology to finance
   - Return period T -> annual exceedance probability p = 1/T
   - Design flood level scales logarithmically: x_T ~ mu + beta*ln(T)

3. Multi-year hazard accumulation requires heavy-tail correction
   - Simple Poisson: P = 1 - (1-p)^t underestimates
   - Gumbel-adjusted: P = 1 - (1 - p*(1 + alpha*ln(t)))^t, alpha ~ 0.77
   - 75-120% higher multi-year probabilities

4. GEV unifies all extreme value types
   - Gumbel (xi=0), Frechet (xi>0), Weibull (xi<0)

5. Stationarity assumption is the key limitation
   - Climate change introduces non-stationary mu(t), beta(t)

KEY MODELS AND FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------
- Gumbel PDF: f(x) = (1/beta)*exp(-(x-mu)/beta)*exp[-exp(-(x-mu)/beta)]
- Gumbel CDF: F(x) = exp[-exp(-(x-mu)/beta)]
- Quantile: F^-1(p) = mu - beta*ln(-ln(p))
- Multi-year Gumbel-adjusted: P(t) = 1 - (1 - p*(1+0.77*ln(t)))^t
- GEV unified: F(x) = exp(-(1+xi*(x-mu)/sigma)^(-1/xi))
- Parameter estimation: MLE (Newton-Raphson) and PWM
- Data: EA annual maximum water level records (30-100 years)

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
Gumbel hazard curve is directly analogous to credit spread curve: both convert event
probability into a term structure of prices. Multi-year accumulation formula is the flood
equivalent of a CDS survival curve. Standardisation of distribution parallels
standardisation of credit models enabling CDS market growth.
