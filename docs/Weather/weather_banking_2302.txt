23.2 Making a Market Across the Catchment
================================================================

SECTION PURPOSE
---------------
Detail the operational considerations for a PRS market-maker: gauge selection,
trigger calibration, tenor structure, bid-offer dynamics, and concentration risk
management across a catchment.

GAUGE SELECTION
----------------
The Thames catchment in the PoC contains 40 EA gauges. Not all are equally
suitable for PRS reference:

  Liquid gauges (high information value):
  - Long historical records (30-100+ years of annual maxima)
  - Well-calibrated flood stage thresholds (alert, warning, severe)
  - Proximity to significant property portfolios
  - Reliable telemetry (minimal data gaps)
  - NRFA metadata available (catchment area, elevation, river name)

  Illiquid gauges (limited use):
  - Short history (insufficient for GEV fitting)
  - Remote from mortgage exposure concentrations
  - Intermittent telemetry
  - Ambiguous flood stage definitions

The market-maker selects reference gauges based on information quality and
relevance to hedgeable exposure. This is analogous to CDS reference entity
selection: liquid single-name CDS exists for ~2,200 entities (per Markit RED)
out of thousands of potential obligors. PRS will similarly concentrate on
the most informative gauges.

TRIGGER LEVEL CALIBRATION
---------------------------
The PoC uses Environment Agency "severe flood warning" thresholds as contractual
trigger levels. This choice provides:

  Objectivity:  Thresholds set by government agency, not by counterparties
  Verifiability: Real-time gauge data published by EA, auditable
  Calibration:   Decades of data enable hazard curve fitting at these levels
  Regulatory:    Direct alignment with emergency preparedness definitions

Chapter 15 showed the five Thames reference gauges:
  Oxford (Osney Lock): 1.80m AOD, 40-year return, 2.5% AEP
  Reading:             7.25m AOD, 30-year return, 3.3% AEP
  Windsor:             2.80m AOD, 20-year return, 5.0% AEP
  London Bridge:       3.50m AOD, 100-year return, 1.0% AEP
  Tilbury:             4.00m AOD, 200-year return, 0.5% AEP

Different trigger levels (alert vs warning vs severe) produce different spreads:
higher thresholds = lower probability = tighter spreads. The market-maker
offers prices across the trigger spectrum, just as a CDS desk quotes different
spreads for different reference entities.

TENOR STRUCTURE
----------------
The PoC supports tenors from 1 to 5 years:

  1-year: baseline pricing, most liquid, direct comparison to insurance
  3-year: standard term, Gumbel accumulation materially higher than simple
  5-year: longest standard tenor, highest Gumbel adjustment

Multi-year pricing uses the Gumbel-adjusted survival curve from Chapter 14.
The term structure of PRS spreads is analogous to the CDS term structure:
longer maturities carry higher cumulative hazard and therefore wider spreads,
but the per-annum cost may decrease as survival probability is factored in.

Illiquidity premiums increase with tenor: 2% for 3-year, 3% for 5-year
(Chapter 15), reflecting the same liquidity dynamics as off-the-run CDS.

BID-OFFER DYNAMICS
-------------------
The PRS bid-offer spread reflects:

  Model uncertainty:     +/- 2bp (irreducible)
  Basis adjustment range: up to +8bp for portfolio-specific pricing
  Hazard curve confidence: wider at longer return periods
  Competitive positioning: tighter for liquid gauges with more counterparties
  Inventory management:  wider when concentrated in one direction

A market-maker accumulates a book of positions across gauges and tenors.
Portfolio management involves:
- Balancing long and short flood risk across the catchment
- Diversifying across gauges to reduce spatial concentration
- Hedging tail risk via portfolio-level PRS or reinsurance
- Managing the term structure of exposure

THE RISK GRID
--------------
The PoC's risk grid (Tab 2) shows FS01 exposure by gauge x trigger level,
ordered west to east along the river. This enables the trader to:

  Identify concentration: "80% of my FS01 is at the Reading gauge"
  Manage directional risk: "I'm net long flood risk at Windsor but short at Oxford"
  Assess correlation: "A single storm system moving east would hit Oxford,
    Reading, and Windsor sequentially -- are my positions hedged against this?"
  Set limits: "Maximum FS01 at any single gauge = X GBP per bp"

This is structurally identical to how a CDS desk manages its risk grid by
reference entity x maturity, monitoring single-name concentration and
sector correlation.

THREE PRODUCT TIERS (Chapter 13)
---------------------------------
The market-maker can offer PRS at three levels of customisation:

  Index PRS: Single gauge, 55bp baseline, all-or-nothing, 1-day execution
  - Highest basis risk (gauge may not represent portfolio exposure)
  - Most liquid, standardised, clearable

  Portfolio PRS: Multi-gauge weighted, 63bp with basis adjustments, 2-3 weeks
  - Zero basis risk (custom gauge basket matches portfolio geography)
  - Less liquid, bespoke documentation

  PRO Tranches: CDO-style structuring
  - Equity (0-0.2m, 180bp), Mezzanine (0.2-1.0m, 85bp), Senior (1.0m+, 35bp)
  - Blended cost 64.5bp
  - Enables risk segmentation by flood severity

This mirrors CDS market evolution: single-name -> index (CDX/iTraxx) ->
tranched (CDO). The market-maker decides which products to offer based on
counterparty demand and hedging capability.
