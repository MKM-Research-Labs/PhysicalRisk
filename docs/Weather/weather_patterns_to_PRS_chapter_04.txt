Chapter 4: Climate Risk Modelling (pp. 143-164)
================================================================

CORE THESIS
-----------
Banking institutions face a fundamental problem of tool misapplication: weather models
and climate models serve entirely different purposes, operate on different timescales,
and require different validation approaches, yet banks routinely confuse them. Weather
prediction asks what will happen next week; climate modelling asks what patterns will
emerge over decades. Conflating these leads to flawed risk assessments.

The chapter traces the regulatory chain from IPCC scientific assessments through TCFD
disclosure frameworks to banking supervision, establishing that this chain is well-
developed at the disclosure end (Pillar 3) but stalled at the capital end (Pillar 1).
Academic frameworks like CLIMADA fill a compliance niche but lack the property-level
granularity and validation artefacts needed for pricing and capital decisions.

The pivotal claim is the "Basel Pillar Progression Paradox": Pillar 3 disclosures
have become detailed, Pillar 2 integration is patchy, and no Pillar 1 standard yet
uses vendor hazard chains in a capital-relevant way. The governance gap -- vendors
can support reporting but cannot provide SR 11-7 validation artefacts -- keeps physical
risk visible in disclosures but absent from the hard numbers that drive risk appetite.

KEY ARGUMENTS
-------------
1. Weather vs climate is not a timescale difference but a fundamentally different
   scientific question
   - Weather: chaotic, initial-condition-dependent, 3km resolution, hours-to-days
   - Climate: boundary-condition-dependent, 100km resolution, decades, statistical
     properties predictable even when individual events are not
   - Banking needs both: weather for tactical, climate for strategic

2. IPCC AR6 synthesised 3,131 scenarios from 95+ model families across 8,000 pages
   - The simplification from working group output to government narrative is a
     material failure of model risk governance

3. TCFD translates climate science into banking requirements through four pillars
   (Governance, Strategy, Risk Management, Metrics and Targets)
   - Over 120 regulators have adopted TCFD-aligned disclosure

4. CLIMADA (ETH Zurich) is the leading open-source probabilistic framework
   - Risk = Hazard x Exposure x Vulnerability
   - 4km resolution insufficient for property-level underwriting
   - Validation: correct within an order of magnitude for 63% of past events --
     insufficient for mortgage pricing

5. The IP of hazard modelling originates in academia and is public domain;
   competitive advantage resides in implementation, not in the models themselves

6. The Basel Pillar Progression Paradox: vendors can support Pillar 3 but cannot
   provide SR 11-7 artefacts needed for Pillar 1 capital models

KEY MODELS AND FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------
- IPCC AR6 (SSP1-1.9, SSP2-4.5, SSP5-8.5 scenario pathways)
- TCFD four-pillar framework
- CLIMADA: Risk = Hazard x Exposure x Vulnerability; IBTrACS, ISIMIP, LitPop
- Impact metrics: EAI, AAI, PMI, exceedance probability curves
- HAZUS (US), CAPRA (Latin America), RiskScape (New Zealand)
- Basel Pillar 1/2/3 structure; SR 11-7

CDS INFRASTRUCTURE ANALOGY
---------------------------
Strongly supports the analogy: CDS instruments moved credit risk from qualitative
assessment to quantified, tradeable, capital-relevant pricing -- exactly the journey
physical risk has not yet completed. PRS could close this gap the same way CDS did
for credit: by creating a market price that becomes the validation artefact itself.

Complication: CLIMADA's order-of-magnitude accuracy and 4km resolution gap suggest
PRS needs its own hazard chain with property-level precision -- a new-build requirement.
